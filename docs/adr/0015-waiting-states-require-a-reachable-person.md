# States that wait on a later message require a reachable person

Amended by [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) (2026-10-15): the unit that waits is the Entry. An Entry can wait while someone reachable can act for it — an ad-hoc team's captain (an account), or a Squad's staff (always reachable, ADR 0024) — and an Entry made only of typed names has nobody and so never waits, as a typed-name Entry did not; an Offer for a team is accepted by its captain or any active account Entrant, or by the staff, and taken back by the captain or staff alone, an Entrant otherwise only leaving.

A waitlist only works if the person can be told when a seat frees up, and an anonymous Registrant cannot be told anything: they have no channel, and one person can queue many times from private windows. We therefore make it a platform rule, not an Organizer setting, that any state which waits on a future message to its holder — a waitlisted Entry today, and approval-pending registrations when they arrive — requires a user with at least one verified, deliverable channel (a confirmed email address, or an equivalent the platform can actually reach). Being signed in is not the test: a passkey-only account has no channel, and a Discord login cannot be DMed without a shared server. (Invitations the Organizer hands out as links need no channel; only invitations cobracket itself delivers do.)

Anonymous Registrants can still take a free seat on an anonymous-allowed Track (ADR 0013); what they cannot do is queue. Entries the Organizer enters by name have no holder and so are never waitlisted — when a Track is full, the Organizer confirms them over Capacity or leaves them out. Account sign-in ships with Events, so the Waitlist is available from the start. The same rule reaches the Organizer: a published Event is a state many people wait on, so publishing one requires a reachable Organizer (amended 2026-10-14, ADR 0021).

Reachability is what makes a waitlist fair to run automatically: while registration is open, a freed seat goes to the next waitlisted Registrant — confirmed at once and told, by default, or held as a time-limited Offer for someone who chose to be asked first — never a silent promotion the person may never notice before the event. An Offer's deadline is the Organizer's hold time; if the Track stops accepting registrations first, for any reason, the Offer returns to the Waitlist with its place kept, so only the hold time itself can lapse an Offer. Returns are derived from stored state rather than written by whatever caused them — a passed close from the clock, the Event's cancellation or suspension from a stop count the Offer records, which follows commit order where instants would not — and when a lapse and a return could both apply, the return wins; only the two single-Track operations that could otherwise undo a return (the attach clamp and moving a passed close later) write their returns first. Accepting by hand needs the holder's session (automatic acceptance is settle confirming the person directly), the deadline not passed, the Offer not returned, and the Track accepting now — the predicate Offers are issued under; the entry gates passed on joining the Waitlist are not asked again. Declining passes the seat on at once, and expiry ends the Entry as lapsed and invites the person to queue again. Automatic acceptance is the default: joining the Waitlist says that a freed seat confirms the person at once, with a notice and a one-tap cancel, because queueing already says "I want in" and asking again only lets places sit empty or lapse overnight. A person who would rather be asked chooses an Offer instead, and switching back to automatic acceptance while holding one accepts it. Offers stay because paid registration will need exactly this held-seat-with-a-deadline, extended into a payment hold. Offers run for as long as the Track accepts registrations — until its effective close, which publication of its tournament brings forward (ADR 0016). A late Offer costs nobody anything, since the close returns it rather than lapsing it; and a seat freed before the close is filled even when processing runs after the close, while one freed after it is not, because whichever write or processing first follows the close records the seats free at it (amended 2026-10-11). Whenever anyone is waiting, no seat is released to the public (holders of Grants that do not reserve their seats join the Waitlist too), so a newcomer never overtakes the queue; after the close, the Organizer confirms waitlisted people by hand, and they are told.

## Considered Options

- **Waitlists open to anyone, promoted silently.** Promotions close to the event reach nobody and turn into no-shows; queueing is free to abuse.
- **Let each Organizer allow anonymous waitlists per Track.** A waitlist that cannot reach its members does not work, so offering the switch is a trap rather than a choice.
- **Verify an email only when joining the waitlist.** Verifying an address is signing in with it; this is the same rule under another name.
- **Host-approved promotion only** (Luma). Reliable, but it leaves seats empty until the Organizer acts; automatic confirmation (or an Offer, for those who ask) keeps the queue moving without surprising anyone.

## Consequences

- Capacity cannot be lowered below the confirmed Entries plus outstanding Offers plus unused reserved seats.
- One predicate — whether a Track is accepting registrations now — serves both the decision function and the settle function, so an Offer is never issued where a registration would be refused — except that settle running after a close fills the seats recorded as free at it (amended 2026-10-11).
- The waitlist order and the moment an Entry was confirmed are stored explicitly; creation time is fixed when a mutation starts, not when it commits, and an Entry confirmed from the Waitlist is confirmed long after it was created.
