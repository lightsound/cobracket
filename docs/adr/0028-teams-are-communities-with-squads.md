# A team is a Community with a Squad: Communities nest under one parent, people relate to them in four ways, and a team page is a Community page

ADR 0023 left teams to this decision with a note that "a competing team is not a Community", and the vision's open question asked whom a Ranking in a team Discipline stands. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 started from the owner's decision that cross-tournament teams exist as pages — fans follow a team and want its tournaments, strength, members, and sponsors — and then dissolved the Community-versus-team distinction: what separates an organizing group from a competing one is not the kind of group but two lists a group may carry, its staff (members with roles) and its players (a Squad). Liquipedia models organizations and their competitive divisions as nodes of one tree; sports clubs are club → section → team; sendou.ink separates owner/manager (permissions) from captain/sub/coach (display roles). [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) decides how a team enters a tournament and [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) how Rankings identify a team across tournaments.

**A team is a Community that carries a Squad.** There is one kind of group: the Community of ADR 0023, with its one owner, roles, bans, Events, and Rankings. A **Squad** is a standing list of players a Community keeps for competition — any node of the tree may carry several (a club's first and second teams, a Splatoon team's A and B units), and a Community with none is an ordinary organizing group. "Team" is the everyday word for a Community with a Squad; data, URLs (`/c/:id`), permissions, and pages do not distinguish them. A person's first Community (created with the account, ADR 0023) can therefore carry the person's own team, and a group that both runs a circuit and fields a team is one Community, not two records. Being on a Squad requires an account (joining is standing consent to be entered in tournaments, ADR 0029, which only a reachable person can give) and confers no permission: the Squad is a list of Players (joining creates the person's Player if they have none yet), the staff a list of members, and one person may be on both. "Staff" here means the Community's members acting for its Squads and Squad-linked Entries, whatever their role (ADR 0023's staff role included), and the capabilities split in two: every member — owner, admin, organizer, staff — operates a Squad-linked Entry (registers it, sets its lineup, accepts or declines an Offer, cancels), while creating or deleting a Squad and editing its players and labels is the owner's, admin's, and organizer's. Each Squad member may carry one free-text label (coach, manager, sub, analyst — suggestions, not a vocabulary; at most 20 characters), set by whoever may edit the Squad (owner, admin, organizer), display-only, treated as a name for reporting and redaction. The Squad's display name is the Community's name, plus the Squad's own name where the Community has more than one (the grilling once said "more than one in the same Discipline"; the Squad's Discipline is only a hint, so the simpler rule is kept); a Squad's Discipline is a hint for the registration form, never a constraint. Squad membership is public — joining a Squad means appearing on the team page and on one's own Player page, and in the published lineup of each tournament one plays for it once that tournament completes (ADR 0029), which the join screen says (a hidden Player page shows the name without a link, as Rankings do). Joining is an Access Grant with the action "join this Squad", with the mechanics of ADR 0023's Community invitation (a use limit, a seven-day window by default, revocation; whoever opens it signed in joins once they accept the join screen), issued by whoever may edit the Squad; promoting an ad-hoc team issues one single-use such Grant per account Entrant, delivered as a notice, each counting against the sender's 50 team invitations a day (ADR 0029). A Squad member leaves at will; whoever may edit the Squad removes at will; nobody is held on a Squad. Deleting a Squad ends it, as a Community's deletion ends its Squads: the row stays with its name and the instant it ended (Entries and team Rankings read it), no longer counted against the Squad limit, its memberships kept as left rows, its "join this Squad" Grants revoked, and no Entry linked to it afterwards; its Entries in Events not over keep their name, Entrants, and Squad link, which stays live until the Community is suspended or deleted, so the staff still act for them, and only the Organizer adds anyone to them, their Squad having no members left to come from. An Entry linked to a Squad of the owning Community, of one of its ancestors, or of one of its descendants (never of a sibling branch) is marked "the host's team" on the bracket and the registrant list.

**Communities nest, under exactly one parent, three deep.** A Community may be the child of another (an organization → its divisions → their teams), with one parent, no cycles, and at most three levels; a child is created by the parent's owner or admin, who becomes its owner or names another member of the parent as its owner; later the parent's owner or admin may name a new owner for it from the child's or the parent's members, and whenever ownership moves while the former owner is still a member — so named, transferred by the owner (ADR 0023), or taken by a successor's request (below) — the former owner stays a member as an admin and is told. Ownership is the only relation that forms a tree, because everything that must be unambiguous hangs on it: the owner and admin roles of a Community apply to its descendants as they do to it (they can re-parent, delete, and re-role a child, and remove players from its Squads), so governance reaches down; organizer and staff roles are confined to their node, so operations do not — a parent who wants to help run a child's Event joins it. Moving a node, children and all, between parents is an Access Grant in the direction member invitations take: the receiving side — its owner or admin — invites, and the owner or admin of the node's current parent accepts (the node's own owner when it is a root); a move out to the root is the parent's release, which needs no Grant and not the child's consent, so a child's owner cannot leave the parent without release — they ask, or re-create; a move that would exceed the depth is refused with the reason and the ways out (move to a shallower parent, detach children first, or stand as a root), and a move, a release, or the detach at a root's sole member's account deletion is recorded as an Activity on each moved node so its page says where it came from. A move, a release, and a child's deletion are notified to the parent's members (for a move, the members of the parent the node leaves, when it had one, and of the one it joins; ordinary notices stay with the owning node's members; ancestors learn by looking); when a root joins another tree, Registrants of its Events that are not over are told that who sees their lists changed, as ADR 0023's hand-over rule tells them, and finished Events get the list Activity only. The move Grant is single-use, seven days, revocable, and stored hashed like the other governance Grants. A parent's deletion is refused while it has children (ADR 0023's "refused while anyone relies on it"); the deletion of a root's sole member's account, which cannot be refused, detaches the children as roots and freezes the parent (a child's ownership passes upward instead; a detached child whose sole member was the same person ends as a root does), as ADR 0022 freezes the rest. Succession of a child's owner resolves upward (designated successor → longest-standing member → the parent's owner). For an absent owner this decision replaces ADR 0027's 90-day rule: a designated successor — who must be a member, the designation lapsing when they leave or ownership moves (the new owner designates their own) — may request ownership at any time; a member without a designation may request it after 30 days in which the owner did nothing in a session (a session that merely stays signed in is not activity); when a root has no member other than its absent owner and no designated successor, a child's owner is the last candidate. Every request is a 14-day notice: the owner's address is told, and ownership moves after 14 days unless the owner signs in and cancels; several requests are served first-come, the others seeing theirs pending; an owner suspended by the operator counts as absent from the first day and cannot cancel; there is no re-application ban, since a hostile successor is simply un-designated by the owner. An organizer's work — running Events, recording results — continues meanwhile.

**Four relations, not one "belongs to".** The grilling found every "X belongs to Y" to be one of four relations with different cardinality, consent, and effect, and keeps them separate rather than typing one table: _ownership_ (Community → Community, one parent, governance and bans flow down); _membership_ (person → Community with a role, many-to-many, ADR 0023); _Squad membership_ (person → Squad, many-to-many: a person plays for several teams; the only constraint is per Track — one active playing Entrant per person, ADR 0029); and _affiliation_ (Community ↔ Community, mutual, shown on both pages, sharing no permission, ban, or code — leagues, federations, circles of clubs). The test for parent versus affiliate: "would you accept their bans and let their governors see your registrant lists?" — if yes, parent; if no, affiliate. An affiliation is requested by the owner or an admin of one Community and accepted or declined by the owner or an admin of the other, whose owner and admins receive the request as a notice; a request expires after seven days, an account sends at most 10 a day, and a declined one blocks only a repeat from the same Community to the same one; either side's owner or admin ends an affiliation at will, and each answer or end is told to the other side's owner and admins.

**Bans are per node and inherited downward; a ban may target a person or a Community.** A Community's ban applies to its Events and to every descendant's, which cannot lift it but may add their own (a parent that is really a peer is an affiliate, which is what makes inheritance always right). Re-parenting applies the new ancestry's bans to new registrations only (existing Entries stay, the preview counts them, ADR 0023's hand-over rule); a detached child — released by a living parent or set free by a root's sole member's account deletion — receives copies of the bans it inherited that target account-holding people (dormant email-tag rows stay with the parent, frozen or not — a reassigned mailbox inheriting a stranger's ban is bad enough once), and the copies carry their origin. An inherited ban is visible read-only to the child's owner and admin, labelled "ban by <ancestor name>". A ban's target is a person (the primary form: it follows the email tag, ADR 0022) or a Community (Entries of that Community's Squads, and of every Squad in its subtree, are refused in the banning Community's Events; the ban does not follow a former descendant once it is detached, released or moved out; the ban preview offers to ban the current Squad members as people too, since a banned team can re-form under a new Community). Bans touch neither memberships nor Squads; they are registration gates, and a ban, when it is set, also ends what already stands in the Events it applies to that are not over, unless their Track's tournament has completed — a banned person's active and invited Entrant rows and requests, as ADR 0029's Track-scope sanction does on its Track, and the Entries of the Squads a Community ban covers, as the Organizer's removal, each told only as that ordinary act —, while a ban that reaches a person or an Event otherwise (re-parenting, a hand-over, a merge, a dormant row re-attached at verification, or a detached child's copy) applies to new registrations only. The gate reads every Entrant, an Organizer's own entry included: an ancestor's ban binds a member registering on their own Community's Track, or entering anyone there as an Organizer (an Organizer's entry skips only the host's own sanctions on others, its Tracks' and its Community's), and cannot be waived by the child; a Community ban does not apply to the same people entering ad hoc — the preview says so, and the person ban is what follows people. A ban is never announced when it is set; it is told when someone tries to register — a person ban to the banned person only, a Community ban plainly to the team's staff; how a team's registration is refused without revealing which member is banned is ADR 0029's.

**Participant numbers are derived, not stored.** ADR 0013's per-Community participant number becomes an eight-character code computed as `HMAC(server secret, root Community id, identity id)` (two blocks of four, 40 bits, keyed and versioned like the email tag). The number was a row per Community and person, renumbered on every hand-over and re-parenting, counted against the anonymous holdings cap, and re-pointed on merge; the code is none of that: a release, or a hand-over or move into another tree, changes the root and so the code (the recognition the renumbering job existed for, at no cost; one within a tree changes neither), different roots give uncorrelatable codes, and the whole tree shares one code per person — so a club's governors, who see every division's registrant lists, see one person as one code. Two things keep the old behavior where it mattered: a dormant sanction row stores the code the person had, and an anonymous identity merged into an account (its code changes, since the identity id does) stores its former code on its Entrant rows, and the Organizer's list says the code changed. Codes are not sequential, which stops a code from leaking a headcount. Under a tree, the registration screen names the Community and its root ("T, part of R"; the chain when it is longer), and the privacy notice says that the ancestors' owners and admins are also Organizers of the Event (the notice telling a Squad member that the staff entered them says both, since they never see that screen); the merge preview counts how many Community trees, named by their roots, will see the code change.

**A Community page is one template; its sections appear when they have content.** Competitor face (shared with the Player page): Squads, tournaments entered, placements, standings in public Rankings (public tournaments and public Rankings only, `canDiscover`, as the Player page), and on a Community page the public Events its Squads' Entries are confirmed on, which a Follow's first kind reports. The Player page (`/p/:id`) lists the person's Squads by default — one switch hides both the Player page and the Squad listings, leaving the name without a link on team pages — and shows the Communities the person runs only when the person turns that on (off by default). Organizer face: public Events hosted and public Rankings published (`canDiscover`). Structure: members with their roles — shown unless the owner hides the section; a member whose Player page is hidden appears as a name without a link —, child Communities, affiliates. Profile: a description (links carry `nofollow ugc`), a logo (the first user-uploaded image: image types only, 512 KB, hidden while a report is under review, removed as the sanction — redaction does not apply to images), a list of external links, all three edited by the owner, an admin, or an organizer; sponsors are text and links in these, with no model of their own. A label the owner chooses (team, organization, league, personal — no effect on permissions, defaulted from the structure), shown with "owns N teams" beside it where N, the number of the Community's descendants that carry a Squad, is above 0; a solo first Community that adds a Squad reads "Taro's team Alpha", and the UI says to rename, or to create a child, for a brand name. The page has a Visibility (unlisted by default, private = members only, public with the directory); since it lists Events, tournaments, and Rankings only as `canDiscover` allows, it opens by its id (`/c/:id`), as the Player page does, without the relation ADR 0019 asks of id-addressed routes, while it is unlisted or public. Team-name impersonation gets no reservation and no verified badge (the operator decides what is allowed, never who someone is, ADR 0027; handles stay deferred, ADR 0025): the structural tell is that a Squad-linked team in a bracket links to its Community page while an ad-hoc team links nowhere, and an impostor Community shows an empty history and no followers; reports and the operator's redaction handle the rest. The link stays whatever becomes of the Community, whose page answers as its state says: the reason only while it is suspended, "not found" while it is private to anyone who may not see it (as for anything a viewer may not see), and its name and that it was deleted once it is a deleted marker. A Community's Visibility governs its page, never its name, which shows wherever it is part of something else — a bracket, a registration screen's "T, part of R", its parent's list of child Communities, an affiliate's page, a Squad member's Player page — linking to the page, which answers as above.

**Following a Community is the first explicit Follow.** ADR 0014 designed explicit Follows and shipped only implicit ones; a Community is the first subject that gets one. A Follow is a subscription row the subscription function consumes: the Community appears in the follower's home list, and its public activity reaches the follower — exactly what the Community page would show that follower if opened now, in four fixed kinds: an Entry confirmed on an Event whose Visibility is public, a tournament completed with the team's placement (a public tournament, `canDiscover` — the page lists no other), a Squad member joined or left, and a move in a public Ranking's standings. Three of the four deliver only once an Event, tournament, or Ranking can be `public`, which Visibility reserves until a directory exists, so until then a Follow delivers Squad changes alone. Per-match results are never delivered (email would become spam and producer-side fan-out unbounded); registrations on unlisted Events are not either (that would hand out their links). Following requires an email account (an anonymous identity has no address and is reaped), is allowed only on a page the follower can view and re-checked at delivery (ADR 0014's dynamic audiences), is in-app always and by email only where the follower turns it on per Follow, and ends with the follower's unfollow, the Community's deletion, or the follower's account deletion. Members need no Follow — the relation already subscribes them, and the function deduplicates. The follower count is public. Announcements from a Community to its followers are not built; the owner decides whether they ever are.

**Limits, suspension, and the rest follow ADR 0027's shape.** Per Community: 20 Squads, 100 players per Squad, 10 child Communities (so a tree holds at most 111 nodes and every subtree enumeration — a deletion preview, a move preview — stays bounded), 50 affiliations; a team name — a Squad's, and an Entry's — is at most 40 characters (a default, whether built from the Community's name or joined from the Entrants' names, is cut to fit), a label 20; per account 500 Follows (owned Communities keep ADR 0027's 10, counted over the nodes a person owns) and 10 affiliation requests a day. The operator can suspend a Community as it suspends a person: the page shows the reason only, nothing is created in it or handed or moved to it (no Event, child, Squad, Grant, or member) and its members can view it and leave it but change nothing in it, as a suspended person creates and operates nothing (ADR 0027), the Squads cannot be entered, no Entry is created, its Rankings are withdrawn as its page is (their standings leaving Player and Community pages, their Revisions still computed), Follow deliveries stop, its Events are suspended with it; lifting restores all of it, and children are not suspended with their parent. Existing Entries of a suspended or deleted Community keep their name and Entrants; the Squad link is inert while the Community is suspended or after its deletion, so the Entry takes a captain by ADR 0029's succession rule (the longest-standing active account playing Entrant, else an anonymous one on an anonymous-allowed Track, never a suspended person), and only one with none falls under its "Entry nobody can act for" rule. The admin role is built in the Communities slice, ahead of the tree (the Squads and the Community tree slice), because the governance that reaches down and member management must not depend on one owner (staff stays defined, not built).

## Considered Options

- **A Team object owned by a Community, with per-team "assignments" for people who manage only the team.** The grilling's own first answer. It re-introduces the per-subject member list ADR 0023 rejected under another name, gives a group that both organizes and competes two records, and leaves a team whose Community ends with its sole member frozen while its managers live. Making the team a Community dissolves all three.
- **A team owned by a person's account.** A second ownership path beside ADR 0023 (succession, absence, suspension, limits, and the role table all live on the Community already).
- **Multiple parents.** Permissions would descend from two lines, bans could contradict, one person would hold two codes, deletion would not know whom to follow, and the controller of personal data would be ambiguous. "A team in two leagues" is affiliation.
- **The node's current parent issuing the move Grant, as the side giving an Event up issues its hand-over (ADR 0023).** The owner's call: a move takes the direction member invitations take, the receiving side inviting, since a team is welcomed into its new parent while an Event is pushed to its new owner; the two directions differ on purpose.
- **Keeping ADR 0027's 90-day absence rule, with a child's owner as the last candidate.** The owner's objection: after an owner's sudden death the Community could do nothing for 90 days. A designated successor's request at any time and a member's after 30 days, each behind a 14-day notice, answer it, and the admin role keeps member management from depending on one owner.
- **Bans per node only, or at the root only.** The first makes an organization repeat its bans per child; the second stops a division from banning anyone. Inheritance is wrong only when the parent is really a peer, and that case is an affiliate.
- **Stored participant numbers kept, renumbered on moves.** A row per Community and person, a renumbering job per move, a line in the holdings cap, a re-point per merge — for a number that is only ever used to recognise a returning person, which a derived code does for free.
- **Per-node participant numbers, or sequential numbers plus a derived tree code.** The first fragments recognition inside one organization; the second is two numbers for one purpose.
- **A fixed vocabulary of display roles (captain, sub, coach).** Scenes name roles differently (a fighting-game crew has a commentator); one free label per Squad member covers them, and permissions never depend on it.
- **Labels on ad-hoc teams too.** It hands an anonymous captain a free-text field that reaches the bracket (ADR 0027), and ad-hoc teams have no non-playing members to label.
- **Letting a Squad member hide from the team page, counted but not named.** Joining a Squad is a public act (the owner's call); the Player-page switch already removes the link to the person, leaving the name, as Rankings do.
- **Name reservation, or an operator "verified team" badge.** The operator would be certifying who someone is; the bracket's link to the Community page already distinguishes a real team from a name.
- **Follows per Squad, or a bookmark without delivery.** The subscription row's subject is typed, so a Squad Follow is one row kind later if asked for; a Follow that delivers nothing does not serve the fan who wants to know when the team plays.
- **Community announcements to followers now.** Deferred by the owner's decision until they judge it needed.
- **A dedicated sponsor model.** Not modelled (the grilling's own round-19 call); text and links on the page serve.

## Consequences

- Schema: `communities` gains `parentId`, `ancestorIds`, `depth`, Visibility, profile fields (description, logo, links, label), whether the members section is hidden, and a suspension marker; `squads` (Community, name, Discipline hint, ended instant); `squadMemberships` (Squad, user, Player, label, joined and left instants — kept after leaving, since lineup history is read from Entries, not from here); `affiliations` (requesting and requested Community, state, request instant); `follows` (user, subject kind and id, email flag); `ownershipRequests` (Community, requester, instant, due instant, state), and users gain the instant of their last act in a session (written at most once a day), which the absence rule reads. The `sanctions` table of ADR 0029 carries the target kind (person or Community), an ended instant, and, on a detach's copies, their origin, and its Community-scope rows are read along `ancestorIds`; participant-number rows are deleted from the design, and the code is computed in the guard that renders Organizer-facing lists.
- ADR 0023's capability table gains rows in its body: "Create, move, release, and delete a child Community; name its owner" (owner, admin — applying downward), "Create and delete Squads; edit their players and labels" (owner, admin, organizer), "Operate Squad-linked Entries: register, lineup, Offers, cancel" (owner, admin, organizer, staff), "Remove a player from a descendant's Squad" (owner, admin), "Edit the Community page's description, logo, and external links" (owner, admin, organizer), and "Request, accept, decline, and end affiliations" (owner, admin); its "a competing team is not a Community" note is reversed. ADR 0027's absence rule is replaced by the succession above, and its note carries the new limits, the Community suspension, the user-content rule for names, labels and profiles, and the 10 owned Communities counted over nodes. ADR 0023's note also carries admin in slice 2, a hand-over's preview counting the Entries the receiving Community or its ancestors ban, an inherited ban binding a member's own registration on their Community's Tracks, upward succession, refusal while children exist, "different for every other tree", the Community suspension, and deletion ending Squads, affiliations and Follows; its hand-over row reads "ban a person or a Community". ADR 0018 gains the "move a Community under another" and "join this Squad" Grants, the derived code, and an ancestor's ban binding Organizer entries; ADR 0019 the Community page's Visibility; ADR 0021, 0022, and 0024 the derived codes. ADR 0019's Organizer relation includes an owner or admin membership of an ancestor of the owning Community. ADR 0013's note adds an ancestor's owner or admin to the Organizer, makes a person visible across Organizers through public Squad membership, and makes its participant number the derived code; ADR 0022's deletion passes a child Community's ownership to the parent's owner when the child has no designated successor and no other member, keeps `squadMemberships` as left rows, detaches a sole-member root's children as roots (each with copies of the bans it inherited that target account-holding people), and deletes the person's Follows; ADR 0014's "explicit Follows come later" is amended for Communities.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains Team, Squad, Affiliation, and the Community entry says what "team" means; its Follow entry names Communities, Organizer includes an ancestor's owner or admin, Visibility covers the Community page, Suspension covers a Community, and Roster's avoid list names "squad". `docs/vision.md` records the owner's decision that teams are pages and that fans follow them.
- `docs/specs/events.md` records the tree, Squads, codes, Follows, the Community page, and the new limits in its own sections, stories, and slices (10, Squads and the Community tree, and 12, Follows), and rewrites the earlier stories and slices they change; the suspended-Community rule joins ADR 0027's operator tooling.
- The next two decisions depend on this one: ADR 0029 (how a Squad, or an ad-hoc group, enters a Track) and ADR 0030 (what a team is to a Ranking).
