# A team is a Community with a Squad: Communities nest under one parent, people relate to them in four ways, and a team page is a Community page

ADR 0023 left teams to this decision with a note that "a competing team is not a Community", and the vision's open question asked whom a Ranking in a team Discipline stands. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 started from the owner's decision that cross-tournament teams exist as pages — fans follow a team and want its tournaments, strength, members, and sponsors — and then dissolved the Community-versus-team distinction: what separates an organizing group from a competing one is not the kind of group but two lists a group may carry, its staff (members with roles) and its players (a Squad). Liquipedia models organizations and their competitive divisions as nodes of one tree; sports clubs are club → section → team; sendou.ink separates owner/manager (permissions) from captain/sub/coach (display roles). [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) decides how a team enters a tournament and [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) how Rankings identify a team across tournaments.

**A team is a Community that carries a Squad.** There is one kind of group: the Community of ADR 0023, with its one owner, roles, bans, Events, and Rankings. A **Squad** is a roster of players a Community keeps for competition — any node of the tree may carry several (a club's first and second teams, a Splatoon team's A and B units), and a Community with none is an ordinary organizing group. "Team" is the everyday word for a Community with a Squad; data, URLs (`/c/:id`), permissions, and pages do not distinguish them. A person's first Community (created with the account, ADR 0023) can therefore carry the person's own team, and a group that both runs a circuit and fields a team is one Community, not two records. Being on a Squad requires an account (joining is standing consent to be entered in tournaments, ADR 0029, which only a reachable person can give) and confers no permission: the Squad is a list of Players, the staff a list of members, and one person may be on both. Each Squad member may carry one free-text label (coach, manager, sub, analyst — suggestions, not a vocabulary; at most 20 characters), set by the staff, display-only, treated as a name for reporting and redaction. The Squad's display name is the Community's name, plus the Squad's own name where the Community has more than one; a Squad's Discipline is a hint for the registration form, never a constraint. Squad membership is public — joining a Squad means appearing on the team page and on one's own Player page, which the join screen says (a hidden Player page shows the name without a link, as Rankings do). A Squad member leaves at will; the staff removes at will; nobody is held on a roster.

**Communities nest, under exactly one parent, three deep.** A Community may be the child of another (an organization → its divisions → their teams), with one parent, no cycles, and at most three levels; a child is created by the parent's owner or admin, who names its owner. Ownership is the only relation that forms a tree, because everything that must be unambiguous hangs on it: the owner and admin roles of a Community apply to its descendants as they do to it (they can re-parent, delete, and re-role a child, and remove players from its Squads), so governance reaches down; organizer and staff roles are confined to their node, so operations do not — a parent who wants to help run a child's Event joins it. Moving a node, children and all, between parents or out to the root is an Access Grant in the direction member invitations take: the receiving side invites, the side that manages the node today accepts (a parent may also release a child without its consent); a move that would exceed the depth is refused with the reason, and a move is recorded as an Activity on the moved node so its page says where it came from. A parent's deletion is refused while it has children (ADR 0023's "refused while anyone relies on it"); the sole-member account deletion that cannot be refused detaches the children as roots and freezes the parent, as ADR 0022 freezes the rest. Succession of a child's owner resolves upward (designated successor → longest-standing member → the parent's owner); a root with neither member nor successor can be claimed under ADR 0027's absence rule by a child's owner as the last candidate.

**Four relations, not one "belongs to".** The grilling found every "X belongs to Y" to be one of four relations with different cardinality, consent, and effect, and keeps them separate rather than typing one table: _ownership_ (Community → Community, one parent, governance and bans flow down); _membership_ (person → Community with a role, many-to-many, ADR 0023); _Squad membership_ (person → Squad, many-to-many: a person plays for several teams; the only constraint is per Track — one active playing Entrant per person, ADR 0029); and _affiliation_ (Community ↔ Community, mutual, shown on both pages, sharing no permission, ban, or code — leagues, federations, circles of clubs). The test for parent versus affiliate: "would you accept their bans and let their governors see your registrant lists?" — if yes, parent; if no, affiliate. Affiliation requests are account-rate-limited and expire; a declined one is not repeated.

**Bans are per node and inherited downward; a ban may target a person or a Community.** A Community's ban applies to its Events and to every descendant's, which cannot lift it but may add their own (a parent that is really a peer is an affiliate, which is what makes inheritance always right). Re-parenting applies the new ancestry's bans to new registrations only (existing Entries stay, the preview counts them, ADR 0023's hand-over rule); a detached child receives copies of the bans it inherited from account-holding people (dormant email-tag rows stay with the frozen parent — a reassigned mailbox inheriting a stranger's ban is bad enough once). A ban's target is a person (the primary form: it follows the email tag, ADR 0022) or a Community (that Community and its Squads cannot be entered in the banning Community's Events; the ban preview offers to ban the current Squad members as people too, since a banned team can re-form under a new Community). Bans touch neither memberships nor Squad rosters; they are registration gates. A ban is told to the banned person only, when they try to register; how a team's registration is refused without revealing which member is banned is ADR 0029's.

**Participant numbers are derived, not stored.** ADR 0013's per-Community participant number becomes an eight-character code computed as `HMAC(server secret, root Community id, identity id)` (two blocks of four, 40 bits, keyed and versioned like the email tag). The number was a row per Community and person, renumbered on every hand-over and re-parenting, counted against the anonymous holdings cap, and re-pointed on merge; the code is none of that: a hand-over or a move changes the root and so the code (the recognition the renumbering job existed for, at no cost), different roots give uncorrelatable codes, and the whole tree shares one code per person — so a club's governors, who see every division's registrant lists, see one person as one code. Two things keep the old behavior where it mattered: a dormant sanction row stores the code the person had, and an anonymous identity merged into an account (its code changes, since the identity id does) stores its former code on its Entrant rows, and the Organizer's list says the code changed. Codes are not sequential, which stops a code from leaking a headcount.

**A Community page is one template; its sections appear when they have content.** Competitor face (shared with the Player page): Squads, tournaments entered, placements, standings in public Rankings (public tournaments and public Rankings only, `canDiscover`, as the Player page). Organizer face: Events hosted, Rankings published. Structure: members (the owner chooses what is shown), child Communities, affiliates. Profile: a description (links carry `nofollow ugc`), a logo (the first user-uploaded image: image types only, 512 KB, hidden while a report is under review, removed as the sanction — redaction does not apply to images), a list of external links; sponsors are text and links in these, with no model of their own. A label the owner chooses (team, organization, league, personal — no effect on permissions, defaulted from the structure). The page has a Visibility (unlisted by default, private = members only, public with the directory). Team-name impersonation gets no reservation and no verified badge (ADR 0027: the operator decides what is allowed, never who someone is; handles stay deferred): the structural tell is that a Squad-linked team in a bracket links to its Community page while an ad-hoc team links nowhere, and an impostor Community shows an empty history and no followers; reports and the operator's redaction handle the rest.

**Following a Community is the first explicit Follow.** ADR 0014 designed explicit Follows and shipped only implicit ones; a Community is the first subject that gets one. A Follow is a subscription row the subscription function consumes: the Community appears in the follower's home list, and its public activity reaches the follower — exactly what the Community page would show that follower if opened now, in four fixed kinds: an Entry confirmed on an Event whose Visibility is public, a tournament completed with the team's placement, a Squad member joined or left, and a move in a public Ranking's standings. Per-match results are never delivered (email would become spam and producer-side fan-out unbounded); registrations on unlisted Events are not either (that would hand out their links). Following requires an email account (an anonymous identity has no address and is reaped), is allowed only on a page the follower can view and re-checked at delivery (ADR 0014's dynamic audiences), is in-app always and by email only where the follower turns it on per Follow, and ends with the Community's deletion. Members need no Follow — the relation already subscribes them, and the function deduplicates. The follower count is public. Announcements from a Community to its followers are not built; the owner decides whether they ever are.

**Limits, suspension, and the rest follow ADR 0027's shape.** Per Community: 20 Squads, 100 members per Squad, 10 child Communities (so a tree holds at most 111 nodes and every subtree enumeration — a deletion preview, a move preview — stays bounded), 50 affiliations; a team name is at most 40 characters, a label 20; per account 500 Follows (owned Communities keep ADR 0027's 10, counted over the nodes a person owns). The operator can suspend a Community as it suspends a person: the page shows the reason only, the Squads cannot be entered, no Entry is created, Rankings are not published, Follow deliveries stop, its Events are suspended with it; lifting restores all of it, and children are not suspended with their parent.

## Considered Options

- **A Team object owned by a Community, with per-team "assignments" for people who manage only the team.** The grilling's own first answer. It re-introduces the per-subject member list ADR 0023 rejected under another name, gives a group that both organizes and competes two records, and leaves a team whose Community ends with its sole member frozen while its managers live. Making the team a Community dissolves all three.
- **A team owned by a person's account.** A second ownership path beside ADR 0023 (succession, absence, suspension, limits, and the role table all live on the Community already).
- **Multiple parents.** Permissions would descend from two lines, bans could contradict, one person would hold two codes, deletion would not know whom to follow, and the controller of personal data would be ambiguous. "A team in two leagues" is affiliation.
- **Bans per node only, or at the root only.** The first makes an organization repeat its bans per child; the second stops a division from banning anyone. Inheritance is wrong only when the parent is really a peer, and that case is an affiliate.
- **Stored participant numbers kept, renumbered on moves.** A row per Community and person, a renumbering job per move, a line in the holdings cap, a re-point per merge — for a number that is only ever used to recognise a returning person, which a derived code does for free.
- **Per-node participant numbers, or sequential numbers plus a derived tree code.** The first fragments recognition inside one organization; the second is two numbers for one purpose.
- **A fixed vocabulary of display roles (captain, sub, coach).** Scenes name roles differently (a fighting-game crew has a commentator); one free label per Squad member covers them, and permissions never depend on it.
- **Labels on ad-hoc teams too.** It hands an anonymous captain a free-text field that reaches the bracket (ADR 0027), and ad-hoc teams have no non-playing members to label.
- **Name reservation, or an operator "verified team" badge.** The operator would be certifying who someone is; the bracket's link to the Community page already distinguishes a real team from a name.
- **Follows per Squad, or a bookmark without delivery.** The subscription row's subject is typed, so a Squad Follow is one row kind later if asked for; a Follow that delivers nothing does not serve the fan who wants to know when the team plays.
- **Community announcements to followers now.** Deferred by the owner's decision until they judge it needed.
- **A dedicated sponsor model.** The owner declined designing for it; text and links on the page serve.

## Consequences

- Schema: `communities` gains `parentId`, `ancestorIds`, `depth`, Visibility, profile fields (description, logo, links, label), and a suspension marker; `squads` (Community, name, Discipline hint); `squadMemberships` (Squad, user, Player, label, joined and left instants — kept after leaving, since lineup history is read from Entries, not from here); `affiliations` (two Communities, state); `follows` (user, subject kind and id, email flag). `bans` gain a target kind (person or Community) and are read along `ancestorIds`; participant-number rows are deleted from the design, and the code is computed in the guard that renders Organizer-facing lists.
- ADR 0023's capability table gains rows: "Create, re-parent, release, and delete child Communities; name a child's owner" (owner, admin — applying downward), "Field Squads: create and edit Squads, invite and remove players, set labels; operate Squad-linked Entries" (owner, admin, organizer), and "Remove a player from a descendant's Squad" (owner, admin); its "a competing team is not a Community" note is reversed. ADR 0013's participant number becomes the derived code; ADR 0022's deletion keeps `squadMemberships` as left rows; ADR 0014's "explicit Follows come later" is amended for Communities.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains Team, Squad, Affiliation, and the Community entry says what "team" means; its Follow entry names Communities. `docs/vision.md` records the owner's decision that teams are pages and that fans follow them.
- `docs/specs/events.md` records the tree, Squads, codes, Follows, the Community page, and the new limits in its own sections and slices; the suspended-Community rule joins ADR 0027's operator tooling.
- The next two decisions depend on this one: ADR 0029 (how a Squad, or an ad-hoc group, enters a Track) and ADR 0030 (what a team is to a Ranking).
