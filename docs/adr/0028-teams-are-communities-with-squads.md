# A team is a Community with a Squad: Communities nest under one parent, people relate to them in four ways, and a team page is a Community page

Revised 2026-10-22 (the owner's call): the parent-child link is display only and each Community governs itself — no role, ban, or code reaches through it; the first form (governance and bans reaching down the tree, a tree-wide code, a move Grant, succession upward, deletion refused while children exist) is in Considered Options. On the same day the owner kept the logo upload and decided that a reported logo is not hidden automatically and that the logo file lives in Convex file storage; and had a suspended Community's page show the state to everyone and the reason to its members. On 2026-10-22 the owner also had the Follow email delivery built with the directory, since three of the four kinds deliver only once `public` exists, and had a Squad's display name reach the team names of past Entries as an account rename reaches a person's rows, when the renamer so chooses, Community and Squad renames taking the account's limit (ADR 0029). On 2026-10-22 the owner also gave the Community page's description, logo, and external links to the owner and admins alone, the Ranking picks staying with organizers (ADR 0023's table; organizers editing the profile is in Considered Options), and made a decline of a parent-link or affiliation request an answer rather than a bar, the same Community free to ask again under the daily cap (the bar after a decline is in Considered Options).

ADR 0023 left teams to this decision with a note that "a competing team is not a Community", and the vision's open question asked whom a Ranking in a team Discipline stands. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 started from the owner's decision that cross-tournament teams exist as pages — fans follow a team and want its tournaments, strength, members, and sponsors — and then dissolved the Community-versus-team distinction: what separates an organizing group from a competing one is not the kind of group but two lists a group may carry, its staff (members with roles) and its players (a Squad). Liquipedia models organizations and their competitive divisions as nodes of one tree; sports clubs are club → section → team; sendou.ink separates owner/manager (permissions) from captain/sub/coach (display roles). [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) decides how a team enters a tournament and [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) how Rankings identify a team across tournaments.

**A team is a Community that carries a Squad.** There is one kind of group: the Community of ADR 0023, with its one owner, roles, bans, Events, and Rankings. A **Squad** is a standing list of players a Community keeps for competition — any node of the tree may carry several (a club's first and second teams, a Splatoon team's A and B units), and a Community with none is an ordinary organizing group. "Team" is the everyday word for a Community with a Squad; data, URLs (`/c/:id`), permissions, and pages do not distinguish them. A person's first Community (created with the account, ADR 0023) can therefore carry the person's own team, and a group that both runs a circuit and fields a team is one Community, not two records. Being on a Squad requires an account (joining is standing consent to be entered in tournaments, ADR 0029, which only a reachable person can give) and confers no permission: the Squad is a list of Players (joining creates the person's Player if they have none yet), the staff a list of members, and one person may be on both. "Staff" here means the Community's members acting for its Squads and Squad-linked Entries, whatever their role (ADR 0023's staff role included), and the capabilities split in two: every member — owner, admin, organizer, staff — operates a Squad-linked Entry (registers it, sets its lineup, accepts or declines an Offer, cancels), while creating, renaming (under the account's limit), or deleting a Squad and editing its players and labels is the owner's, admin's, and organizer's. Each Squad member may carry one free-text label (coach, manager, sub, analyst — suggestions, not a vocabulary; at most 20 characters), set by whoever may edit the Squad (owner, admin, organizer), display-only, treated as a name for reporting and redaction. The Squad's display name is the Community's name, plus the Squad's own name where the Community has more than one (the grilling once said "more than one in the same Discipline"; the Squad's Discipline is only a hint, so the simpler rule is kept), and a change of it — a rename of the Community or the Squad, or the Community's going from one Squad to several or back — reaches the team names of the Entries that follow it as an account rename reaches a person's Entrant rows, past ones included when whoever makes the change so chooses, asked about the Entries of the Squad whose display name changes (ADR 0029; the owner's call of 2026-10-22), a Community or a Squad being renamed under the account's limit of once every seven days with one correction within an hour; a Squad's Discipline is a hint for the registration form, never a constraint. Squad membership is public — joining a Squad means appearing on the team page and on one's own Player page, and in the published lineup of each tournament one plays for it once that tournament completes (ADR 0029), which the join screen says (a hidden Player page shows the name without a link, as Rankings do). Joining is an Access Grant with the action "join this Squad", with the mechanics of ADR 0023's Community invitation (a use limit, a seven-day window by default, revocation; whoever opens it signed in joins once they accept the join screen), issued by whoever may edit the Squad; promoting an ad-hoc team issues one single-use such Grant per account Entrant, delivered as a notice, each counting against the sender's 50 team invitations a day (ADR 0029). A Squad member leaves at will; whoever may edit the Squad removes at will; nobody is held on a Squad. Deleting a Squad ends it, as a Community's deletion ends its Squads: the row stays with its name and the instant it ended (Entries and team Rankings read it), no longer counted against the Squad limit, its memberships kept as left rows, its "join this Squad" Grants revoked, and no Entry linked to it afterwards, so neither "last time's team" nor queueing again after an Offer (ADR 0029) is offered from its Entries (a copy would have no Squad to link and no members to take); its Entries in Events not over keep their name, Entrants, and Squad link, which stays live until the Community is suspended or deleted, so the staff still act for them, and only the Organizer adds anyone to them, their Squad having no members left to come from. An Entry linked to a Squad of the owning Community, of one of its ancestors, or of one of its descendants (never of a sibling branch) is marked "the host's team" on the bracket and the registrant list.

**Communities nest, under exactly one parent, three deep, and the link is display only: each Community governs itself.** A Community may be the child of another (an organization → its divisions → their teams), with one parent, no cycles, and at most three levels; the parent, its ancestor ids, and the depth are stored on the row. A child is created by a Community's owner or admin, who becomes its owner (ownership transfers afterwards as any Community's does, ADR 0023). Two existing Communities are linked as an affiliation is formed (below): the owner or an admin of either side requests the link, naming which side is the parent, the request reaching the other side's owner and admins as a notice, and any of them accepts or declines it; a request expires after seven days and counts against the sender's 10 affiliation and link requests a day, and a decline is an answer, not a bar: the same Community may ask again under that cap (the owner's call, 2026-10-22; the bar after a decline is in Considered Options); a link that would exceed the depth, or close a cycle, is refused with the reason; a link to a new parent ends the link to the old one, whose owner and admins are told. Either side's owner or admin ends a link at will, the other side's owner and admins told, and a link's start and end are Activities on both pages, so each page says where it came from and what it was part of. A suspended Community neither requests, accepts, nor ends a link, and nothing is linked to it, while the other side may still end the link; a Community's deletion ends the links to its children, which stand as roots. The link carries no permission, no ban, and no code: the owner, admin, organizer, and staff roles of a Community act in that Community alone — a parent who wants to help run a child's Event joins it — and a child's owner is replaced by its own succession (the designated successor, else the longest-standing member, ADR 0023; for an absent owner, the rule below). What the link does is show and count: the child's page says it is part of the parent and the parent's page lists its children; an Entry linked to a Squad of the owning Community, of one of its ancestors, or of one of its descendants is marked "the host's team" (above); the registration screen names the Community and its root ("T, part of R"); and a Ranking condition that names a Community covers the Communities below it, as recorded at each tournament's first completion (ADR 0025) — nothing already completed moves when a link starts or ends, and the new ancestors' conditions admit only tournaments completed from then on, which the link's request and acceptance screens, the notices to the other side, and the Activity say, adding that private keeps a tournament out of every Ranking. The owner decided this on 2026-10-22, replacing the first form of this decision (Considered Options): a parent's owner and admin governing its descendants, bans inherited down the tree, a code shared by a tree, a move Grant with the parent's release, succession upward, and deletion refused while children exist — about twenty-five rules whose one case, a parent that must act in a child it does not run, also arises with no parent at all and so cannot be prevented, at the cost of a second permission path beside ADR 0023's one question. For an absent owner this decision replaces ADR 0027's 90-day rule: a designated successor — who must be a member, the designation lapsing when they leave or ownership moves (the new owner designates their own) — may request ownership at any time; a member without a designation may request it after 30 days in which the owner did nothing in a session (a session that merely stays signed in is not activity). Every request is a 14-day notice: the owner's address is told, and ownership moves after 14 days unless the owner signs in and cancels; several requests are served first-come, the others seeing theirs pending; an owner suspended by the operator counts as absent from the first day and cannot cancel; there is no re-application ban, since a hostile successor is simply un-designated by the owner; and whenever ownership moves while the former owner is still a member — transferred by the owner (ADR 0023) or taken by a successor's request — the former owner stays a member as an admin and is told. An organizer's work — running Events, recording results — continues meanwhile. A Community whose sole member lost their mailbox is not recoverable (ADR 0027).

**Four relations, not one "belongs to".** The grilling found every "X belongs to Y" to be one of four relations with different cardinality, consent, and effect, and keeps them separate rather than typing one table: _the parent link_ (Community → Community, one parent: shown as part of, and counted by the parent's Rankings; no permission, ban, or code); _membership_ (person → Community with a role, many-to-many, ADR 0023); _Squad membership_ (person → Squad, many-to-many: a person plays for several teams; the only constraint is per Track — one active playing Entrant per person, ADR 0029); and _affiliation_ (Community ↔ Community, mutual, shown on both pages, sharing no permission, ban, or code — leagues, federations, circles of clubs). The test for parent versus affiliate: "should you be shown as part of them, and should their Rankings count your unlisted tournaments?" — if yes, parent; if no, affiliate; neither shares a permission, a ban, or a code. An affiliation is requested by the owner or an admin of one Community and accepted or declined by the owner or an admin of the other, whose owner and admins receive the request as a notice; a request expires after seven days, an account sends at most 10 a day, and a decline is an answer, not a bar — the same Community may ask again under that cap (the owner's call, 2026-10-22); either side's owner or admin ends an affiliation at will, and each answer or end is told to the other side's owner and admins.

**Bans are per Community; a ban may target a person or a Community.** A Community's ban applies to its own Events only: a parent's ban does not reach a child's Events, nor a child's the parent's — an organization that wants one ban list runs its Events in one Community, and each node bans whom it bans. A ban's target is a person (the primary form: it follows the email tag, ADR 0022) or a Community (Entries of that Community's Squads, and of every Squad in its subtree, are refused in the banning Community's Events — the gate reads the Entry's Squad's Community along its ancestors, the one read along the tree, since a team's divisions are the team's own; the ban does not follow a former descendant once its link has ended; the ban preview offers to ban the current Squad members as people too, since a banned team can re-form under a new Community). Bans touch neither memberships nor Squads; they are registration gates, and a ban, when it is set, also ends what already stands in the Events it applies to that are not over, unless their Track's tournament has completed — a banned person's active and invited Entrant rows and requests, as ADR 0029's Track-scope sanction does on its Track, and the Entries of the Squads a Community ban covers, as the Organizer's removal, each told only as that ordinary act —, while a ban that reaches a person or an Event otherwise (a hand-over, a merge, or a dormant row re-attached at verification) applies to new registrations only, except that it withdraws the pending requests it bars, as every barred request is withdrawn (ADR 0029). The gate reads every Entrant; an Organizer's entry skips the host's own sanctions on others, its Tracks' and its Community's (ADR 0029); a Community ban does not apply to the same people entering ad hoc — the preview says so, and the person ban is what follows people. A ban is never announced when it is set; it is told when someone tries to register — a person ban to the banned person only, a Community ban plainly to the team's staff; how a team's registration is refused without revealing which member is banned is ADR 0029's.

**Participant numbers are derived, not stored.** ADR 0013's per-Community participant number becomes an eight-character code computed as `HMAC(server secret, Community id, identity id)` (two blocks of four, 40 bits, keyed and versioned like the email tag). The number was a row per Community and person, renumbered on every hand-over, counted against the anonymous holdings cap, and re-pointed on merge; the code is none of that: a hand-over changes the Community and so the code (the recognition the renumbering job existed for, at no cost), a parent link or its end changes nothing, different Communities give uncorrelatable codes, and a Community sees one person as one code across every Event it runs. Two things keep the old behavior where it mattered: a dormant sanction row stores the code the person had, and an anonymous identity merged into an account (its code changes, since the identity id does) stores its former code on its Entrant rows, and the Organizer's list says the code changed. Codes are not sequential, which stops a code from leaking a headcount. Under a tree, the registration screen names the Community and its root ("T, part of R"; the chain when it is longer), so a player knows which organization the host is part of (the notice telling a Squad member that the staff entered them names the host the same way, since they never see that screen); the merge preview counts how many Communities will see the code change.

**A Community page is one template; its sections appear when they have content.** Competitor face (shared with the Player page): Squads, tournaments entered, placements, standings in the public Rankings the page shows (public tournaments and public Rankings only, `canDiscover`, picked and shown as the Player page's are — on a Community page by its owner, an admin, or an organizer; the owner's call, 2026-10-17, kept on 2026-10-22 when the profile went to the owner and admins, since Rankings are the organizer's field), and on a Community page the public Events its Squads' Entries are confirmed on, which a Follow's first kind reports. The Player page (`/p/:id`) lists the person's Squads by default — one switch hides both the Player page and the Squad listings, leaving the name without a link on team pages — and shows the Communities the person runs only when the person turns that on (off by default). Organizer face: public Events hosted and public Rankings published (`canDiscover`). Structure: members with their roles — shown unless the owner hides the section, which the Community invitation screen says (ADR 0023); a member whose Player page is hidden appears as a name without a link —, child Communities, affiliates. Profile: a description (links carry `nofollow ugc`), a logo (the first user-uploaded image: PNG, JPEG, or WebP — never SVG —, at most 512 KB and 2,048 pixels a side (the option the owner accepted named a pixel cap; the figure is Claude's), re-encoded in the browser before the upload, which drops its metadata, checked on the server afterwards — type by its bytes, size, dimensions —, which deletes what fails, and served from an address that stops answering once the file is deleted (the option the owner accepted served the file only through cobracket's own gateway; Convex's storage URL took that place with the storage choice below, since a URL that dies with the file revokes the same way); a reported logo stays up until the operator reviews it, as every reported content does, a report on an image told to the operator at once and sitting first in the operator's queue, and removal is the sanction — redaction does not apply to images; the file lives in Convex file storage, the owner's choice of 2026-10-22 — Considered Options), a list of external links, all three edited by the owner or an admin (the owner's call, 2026-10-22: the page's presentation sits with the roles that hold the Community's identity and its relations, as Battlefy's and Challonge's tournament-running roles cannot touch an organization's brand; an organizer who should edit the page is made an admin or asks the owner or an admin; organizers editing it is in Considered Options); sponsors are text and links in these, with no model of their own. A label the owner chooses (team, organization, league, personal — no effect on permissions, defaulted from the structure), shown with "N teams under it" beside it where N, the number of the Community's descendants that carry a Squad, is above 0; a solo first Community that adds a Squad reads "Taro's team Alpha", and the UI says to rename, or to create a child, for a brand name. The page has a Visibility (unlisted by default, public with the directory, never private: everything on it is public by its own rule, and a closed group keeps its play closed with private Events); since it lists Events, tournaments, and Rankings only as `canDiscover` allows, it opens by its id (`/c/:id`), as the Player page does, without the relation ADR 0019 asks of id-addressed routes. Team-name impersonation gets no reservation and no verified badge (the operator decides what is allowed, never who someone is, ADR 0027; handles stay deferred, ADR 0025): the structural tell is that a Squad-linked team in a bracket links to its Community page while an ad-hoc team links nowhere, and an impostor Community shows an empty history and no followers; reports and the operator's redaction handle the rest. The link stays whatever becomes of the Community, whose page answers as its state says: that the operator suspended it while it is suspended — the reason to its members —, and its name and that it was deleted once it is a deleted marker. A Community's name shows wherever it is part of something else — a bracket, a registration screen's "T, part of R", its parent's list of child Communities, an affiliate's page, a Squad member's Player page — linking to the page, which answers as above.

**Following a Community is the first explicit Follow.** ADR 0014 designed explicit Follows and shipped only implicit ones; a Community is the first subject that gets one. A Follow is a subscription row the subscription function consumes: the Community appears in the follower's home list, and its public activity reaches the follower — exactly what the Community page would show that follower if opened now, in four fixed kinds: an Entry confirmed on an Event whose Visibility is public, a tournament completed with the team's placement (a public tournament, `canDiscover` — the page lists no other), a Squad member joined or left, and a move in the standings of a public Ranking the page shows. Three of the four deliver only once an Event, tournament, or Ranking can be `public`, which Visibility reserves until a directory exists, so until then a Follow delivers Squad changes alone. Per-match results are never delivered (email would become spam and producer-side fan-out unbounded); registrations on unlisted Events are not either (that would hand out their links). Following requires an email account (an anonymous identity has no address and is reaped), is allowed only on a page the follower can view and re-checked at delivery (ADR 0014's dynamic audiences), is in-app always and by email only where the follower turns it on per Follow (the email delivery is built with the directory, the owner's call of 2026-10-22, since three of the four kinds deliver only once `public` exists; until then a Follow has no email switch and delivers in-app only, Claude's reading of that call), and ends with the follower's unfollow, the Community's deletion, or the follower's account deletion. Members need no Follow — the relation already subscribes them, and the function deduplicates. The follower count is public. Announcements from a Community to its followers are not built; the owner decides whether they ever are.

**Limits, suspension, and the rest follow ADR 0027's shape.** Per Community: 20 Squads, 100 players per Squad, 10 child Communities (so a tree holds at most 111 nodes and every subtree enumeration — a Community ban's gate, the "host's team" mark, the "N teams under it" count, a Ranking condition's reach — stays bounded), 50 affiliations; a team name — a Squad's, and an Entry's — is at most 40 characters (a default, whether built from the Community's name or joined from the Entrants' names, is cut to fit), a label 20; per account 500 Follows (owned Communities keep ADR 0027's 10, children included) and 10 affiliation and parent-link requests a day. The operator can suspend a Community as it suspends a person: the page tells everyone that the operator suspended it, and its members, signed in, the reason (the owner's call, 2026-10-22: the reason is written for the Community to act on and may name reports or people, so the public reads the state and the members the reason, as a suspended person reads theirs after signing in), nothing is created in it or handed or linked to it (no Event, child, Squad, Grant, member, or parent link; it neither requests, accepts, nor ends a link, while the other side may end one) and its members can view it and leave it but change nothing in it, as a suspended person creates and operates nothing (ADR 0027), the Squads cannot be entered, no Entry is created, its Rankings are withdrawn as its page is (Claude's extension of the page rule; their standings leaving Player and Community pages, their Revisions still computed), Follow deliveries stop, its Events are suspended with it; lifting restores all of it, and children are not suspended with their parent. Existing Entries of a suspended or deleted Community keep their name and Entrants; the Squad link is inert while the Community is suspended or after its deletion, so the Entry takes a captain by ADR 0029's succession rule (the longest-standing active account playing Entrant, never a suspended person), and only one with none falls under its "Entry nobody can act for" rule. The admin role is built in the Communities slice, ahead of the tree (the Squads and the Community tree slice), because member management must not depend on one owner (staff stays defined, not built).

## Considered Options

- **A Team object owned by a Community, with per-team "assignments" for people who manage only the team.** The grilling's own first answer. It re-introduces the per-subject member list ADR 0023 rejected under another name, gives a group that both organizes and competes two records, and leaves a team whose Community ends with its sole member frozen while its managers live. Making the team a Community dissolves all three.
- **A team owned by a person's account.** A second ownership path beside ADR 0023 (succession, absence, suspension, limits, and the role table all live on the Community already).
- **Multiple parents.** "Part of" would have two answers on every screen that names the root, a Ranking condition would count one tournament under two organizations, and the depth and cycle rules would need a graph. "A team in two leagues" is affiliation.
- **The tree as a governance line — this decision's first form (2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22).** A parent's owner and admin held their roles in every descendant (running its Events, reading its registrant lists, re-roling its members, removing players from its Squads, re-parenting and deleting it, naming its owner); bans were inherited downward, shown read-only as "ban by <ancestor>" and copied to a detached child; one code served a whole tree; a move was an Access Grant issued by the receiving side and accepted by the current parent, a release the parent's act; a child's owner succession resolved upward to the parent's owner; and a parent could not be deleted while it had children. The owner withdrew it on 2026-10-22: the one case it served — a parent that must act in a child it does not run — also arises with no parent at all, so it cannot be prevented; it was a second permission path beside ADR 0023's one question, about twenty-five rules, and two readings of one tree (start.gg's and Discord's groups also link without governing). What remains is the display and the Ranking count above.
- **Keeping ADR 0027's 90-day absence rule.** The owner's objection: after an owner's sudden death the Community could do nothing for 90 days. A designated successor's request at any time and a member's after 30 days, each behind a 14-day notice, answer it, and the admin role keeps member management from depending on one owner.
- **Bans inherited down the tree, or set at the root only.** The first was the first form above, right only while the parent governed the child; the second stops a division from banning anyone. A Community bans for its own Events, and a Community-target ban still covers a team's whole subtree of Squads, since those are the team's own.
- **Stored participant numbers kept, renumbered on hand-overs.** A row per Community and person, a renumbering job per hand-over, a line in the holdings cap, a re-point per merge — for a number that is only ever used to recognise a returning person, which a derived code does for free.
- **One code for a whole tree, or sequential numbers plus a derived code.** The first was the first form above, where a tree's governors read every division's lists; with each Community running itself nobody reads another node's lists, so a tree-wide code would only correlate people across Communities for no reader. The second is two numbers for one purpose.
- **A fixed vocabulary of display roles (captain, sub, coach).** Scenes name roles differently (a fighting-game crew has a commentator); one free label per Squad member covers them, and permissions never depend on it.
- **Labels on ad-hoc teams too.** It hands an ad-hoc captain a free-text field that reaches the bracket (ADR 0027), and ad-hoc teams have no non-playing members to label.
- **Letting a Squad member hide from the team page, counted but not named.** Joining a Squad is a public act (the owner's call); the Player-page switch already removes the link to the person, leaving the name, as Rankings do.
- **A private Community page (members only).** Everything on the page is public by its own rule — Events, tournaments, and Rankings only as `canDiscover` allows; the members section unless the owner hides it; a profile its owner and admins write; Squads whose joining is a public act (the owner's call); child Communities and affiliates, whose names show wherever they are part of something else — so a private value would hide mainly lists (the Squad members above all), while the Community's name, the completed lineups, and each player's Player-page listing stay public. It would need an audience of its own (the staff alone shut a team's players out of their own page), and it would take from everyone else the impostor tell, a ban set from the team's page, and the report button; a closed group keeps its play closed with private Events (ADR 0019). The owner decided (2026-10-17) that a Community page is never private.
- **Name reservation, or an operator "verified team" badge.** The operator would be certifying who someone is; the bracket's link to the Community page already distinguishes a real team from a name.
- **Follows per Squad, or a bookmark without delivery.** The subscription row's subject is typed, so a Squad Follow is one row kind later if asked for; a Follow that delivers nothing does not serve the fan who wants to know when the team plays.
- **Community announcements to followers now.** Deferred by the owner's decision until they judge it needed.
- **No logo upload: an emblem drawn from the Community's initials.** Proposed on 2026-10-18 so that cobracket would hold no user images (an operator alone answering for illegal images, with no automatic detection, and the one exception to "nothing happens automatically on a report"); the owner kept the logo (2026-10-22: an edge case, not worth the restriction) and decided that a reported logo is not hidden automatically, which removes the exception.
- **Cloudflare R2 through `@convex-dev/r2`, or `convex-fs`, for the logo file.** Compared on 2026-10-22 at the owner's request. R2 serves files with no egress charge and, under a custom domain, from a CDN, but every deployment then needs real R2 credentials — local development and the browser gate would reach the cloud, against the rule that keeps them local —, the file is checked only after a presigned upload, presigned reads bypass the cache, and the component's test helper and runtime carried open issues; `convex-fs` is not first-party and stores on bunny.net, a third vendor with a monthly minimum. Logos are small and few, so Convex file storage — no credential, cloud-free local development, a bearer URL that stops answering once the file is deleted — serves them, at the cost of no CDN and the free plan's 1 GB/month file-egress cap; the logo field is a storage id, so moving to R2 later is additive (the owner's choice, 2026-10-22). The logo option the owner accepted the same day also said that once cobracket's own domain is on Cloudflare, the images are served through R2 so that Cloudflare's image detection applies (it runs only on a zone's own traffic, not on workers.dev, *.convex.cloud, or r2.dev); the Convex choice defers that path to the day the storage moves, it does not drop it, and until then no detection runs on logos.
- **A dedicated sponsor model.** Not modelled (the grilling's own round-19 call); text and links on the page serve.
- **Organizers editing the Community page's description, logo, and external links** (this decision's first form, 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22). The owner moved them to the owner and admins: the capability table assigns by job — the owner holds the Community's existence and identity, admins its people and relations, organizers its competition — and the page's presentation, the one uploaded image among it, is none of the three but closest to identity; Battlefy's moderators and Challonge's collaborators likewise run tournaments without touching the organization's brand. The cost is a small team's doer who holds organizer and must be made an admin, or ask the owner or an admin, to fix a sponsor link. The Ranking picks stay with organizers, since Rankings are theirs.
- **A bar on asking again after a declined parent-link or affiliation request** (this decision's first form, 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22: a declined request blocked a repeat from the same Community to the same one, with no end). It was copied from ADR 0029's Entrant rule, where a declined row bars only a repeat with that Entry, which ends; between Communities, which last, it was a permanent one-way bar that mostly hurt the honest case (a "not now" became "never, unless the other side remembers to ask"), while the daily cap already bounds a pest account to ten requests a day and reports and the operator's suspension are what a pest is for; ownership requests carry no re-application bar either. The cost is a Community whose owner and admins may each ask the same one up to ten times a day until it is reported. Holding the pair for the original request's seven days, a receiver-side switch, and a Community-target ban that also bars link requests were the other forms.

## Consequences

- Schema: `communities` gains `parentId`, `ancestorIds`, `depth`, former names with their instants, Visibility, profile fields (description, logo as a Convex storage id, links, label), whether the members section is hidden, and a suspension marker; `squads` (Community, name with its former names and their instants, Discipline hint, ended instant); `squadMemberships` (Squad, user, Player, label, joined and left instants — kept after leaving, since lineup history is read from Entries, not from here); `affiliations` (requesting and requested Community, state, request instant) and parent-link requests of the same shape, naming which side is the parent (one table with a kind, or two — the implementation's choice); `follows` (user, subject kind and id, email flag); `ownershipRequests` (Community, requester, instant, due instant, state), and users gain the instant of their last act in a session (written at most once a day), which the absence rule reads. The `sanctions` table of ADR 0029 carries the target kind (person or Community) and an ended instant, and a Community-target row is read along the Entry's Squad's Community's `ancestorIds`; participant-number rows are deleted from the design, and the code is computed in the guard that renders Organizer-facing lists.
- ADR 0023's capability table gains rows in its body: "Create a child Community; request, accept, decline, and end a parent link" (owner, admin), "Create, rename, and delete Squads; edit their players and labels" (owner, admin, organizer), "Operate Squad-linked Entries: register, lineup, Offers, cancel" (owner, admin, organizer, staff), "Edit the Community page's description, logo, and external links" (owner, admin) and "Pick the Rankings the Community page shows" (owner, admin, organizer), and "Request, accept, decline, and end affiliations" (owner, admin); its "a competing team is not a Community" note is reversed. ADR 0027's absence rule is replaced by the succession above, and its note carries the new limits, the Community suspension, the user-content rule for names, labels and profiles, and the 10 owned Communities counted with children. ADR 0023's note also carries admin in slice 2, a hand-over's preview counting the Entries the receiving Community bans, the display-only link, the Community suspension, and deletion ending Squads, affiliations, parent links, and Follows; its hand-over row reads "ban a person or a Community", and its first row "choose its label and hide its page's members section" (the owner's). ADR 0018 gains the "join this Squad" Grant and the derived code; ADR 0019 the Community page's Visibility; ADR 0021, 0022, and 0024 the derived codes. ADR 0013's note makes a person visible across Organizers through public Squad membership and makes its participant number the derived code; ADR 0022's deletion keeps `squadMemberships` as left rows, ends the links to a sole-member Community's children, which stand as roots, and deletes the person's Follows; ADR 0014's "explicit Follows come later" is amended for Communities.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains Team, Squad, Affiliation, and the Community entry says what "team" means; its Follow entry names Communities, Visibility covers the Community page, Suspension covers a Community, and Roster's avoid list names "squad". `docs/vision.md` records the owner's decision that teams are pages and that fans follow them.
- `docs/specs/events.md` records the tree, Squads, codes, Follows, the Community page, and the new limits in its own sections, stories, and slices (10, Squads and the Community tree, and 12, Follows), and rewrites the earlier stories and slices they change; the suspended-Community rule joins ADR 0027's operator tooling.
- The next two decisions depend on this one: ADR 0029 (how a Squad, or an ad-hoc group, enters a Track) and ADR 0030 (what a team is to a Ranking).
