# Invitations, unlock codes, and claim links are one concept: the Access Grant

Amended by [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) (2026-10-15): the register action gains an Entry scope — a team's join link, issued by the captain (a Squad-linked Entry has none, ADR 0029), admitting Entrants onto one Entry through the activation function, one unended per Entry, ending with the Entry, the swap deadline, or a change that makes its Track invite-only or hidden or its Event private — and a claim Grant is scoped to an Entry and claims one of its typed-name Entrants ("which one are you?"), one unended per Entry (re-issuing replaces it) and issued by the Organizer or, on an anonymous-allowed Track that is neither invite-only nor hidden in an Event that is not private, by the Entry's captain too (who may there also copy and revoke it; a change that makes the Track invite-only or hidden or its Event private ends one the captain issued), its post-Event window and its view rule unchanged — below, "used while its Entry has a holder" reads per row (a typed-name row that has its claimer is a derived use, and the link stays open while another row is unclaimed), and "active and unclaimed" as the chosen row active and unclaimed and its Entry active; undoing a wrong claim re-enters the Entrant row, not the Entry, with a fresh claim link (the Entry's one claim link re-issued, ending the link the wrong claimer used) — the Entry, its seat, and its Participant stay — and a person's account deletion takes their rows out of the Entry's claim link, revoking it only when no claimable typed-name row remains; "an ended Entry that had a holder" below reads as any Entrant row that had an identity, active or ended (a Track-scope sanction ends the person's active and invited rows on that Track as `removed`), which is what a sanction (ADR 0029's one row for block and ban) is made from. Amended by [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) (2026-10-15): the participant number scoped to the Organizer below is now the derived code (per Community since 2026-10-22; a parent link is no Grant, it is requested and accepted as an affiliation is); a Grant with the action "join this Squad" (scope: one Squad) carries the Community invitation's mechanics (hashed, a use limit, a seven-day window by default, revocable; the one issued per account Entrant at promotion is single-use, each counting against the sender's 50 team invitations a day, ADR 0029) and confers no permission. "One active Entry per user per Track" reads as one active playing Entrant per person per Track, forming Entries included (ADR 0029's invariant 1), with "leave A and join B" offered as one step, and for Organizer entries it binds every Entrant with an identity they add, from the recruiting list or by a lineup change, not only "their own Entry". A team invitation (a captain's, or the staff's to a Squad member who left the Squad-linked Entry inviting them) is an Entrant row, not a Grant: cobracket delivers it as a person-addressed notice — in-app, and by email under the recipient's delivery preferences, never to a typed address — to a closed set (Entrants of recruiting Entries on that Track, last time's Entrants, and that Squad member), 50 a day per account. A Track-scope sanction ends the person's Entrant rows on the Track, never the Entry (which may become short-handed); a Community-scope one may target a Community (the gate reads the Entry's Squad's Community along its ancestors) and may be set on a Squad member who has no Entrant row, from the ban preview. The Entry-scope register Grant passes no Track setting — it cannot exist on an invite-only or hidden Track or in a private Event — and its uses are the Entrant rows it admitted (active or removed), bounded by the Track's maximum. Among a Track- or Event-scope register Grant's uses, the Entries "still active" below are those that have registered: a forming Entry counts none (ADR 0029). A team "make teams" made comes through no Grant and passes its Track's invite-only or hidden setting at registration as Organizer entries do (ADR 0029). Organizer entries also skip the minimum and are bound by the maximum (ADR 0029).

Organizers need to let some people in ahead of or around the general public — invited players, a community's early-access window, a late friend after the close, a walk-in at the door — and to let someone take over an Entry entered for them. Later they will invite co-organizers too. Rather than a feature per case, every secret link or code that lets someone _act_ is an Access Grant. (Viewing by link is separate: ADR 0019.)

Amended by [ADR 0023](./0023-communities-own-events-and-roles.md) (2026-10-14): three more actions — join a Community (the co-organizer invitation anticipated here, with the role fixed at issuance), take over an Event (a hand-over between Communities, accepted by the receiving Community's owner or admin), and record results on one tournament (a one-off helper who is not a member) — their secrets stored hashed, as left open below. Organizers are now verified accounts ([ADR 0024](./0024-organizer-side-requires-an-account.md)), and invitations still travel as links the Organizer delivers; email-addressed invitations stay a later option. Grants belong to the Event: an Organizer's account deletion revokes none of those they issued, and they go when the Community does (ADR 0022's note).

A Grant has an action (register, or claim one specific Entry; later, join an Event's organizers), a scope (one Track or the whole Event), a use limit (one, N, or unlimited), revocation, and — for a register Grant — its own time window and, on a single-Track Grant with a use limit, whether it reserves its seats; a claim Grant has no window. It only ever narrows — revoking, shortening its window, lowering its use limit — and widening means issuing a new one; the single exception is giving an unended Grant whose code was deleted a new code after its Event returns from private, which restores the way in chosen at issuance and is checked like issuance.

- **The window replaces a list of gates.** A register Grant passes its own Track's invite-only or hidden setting — a Grant that did not would be useless there — while an Event-scope Grant passes invite-only Tracks but never reveals or admits to hidden ones, which take Track-scope Grants only. The Grant's window replaces the Track's window for its holders. A register Grant's window is instants and never a reference to another setting, so closing or reopening a Track, publication, and Event-wide scopes cannot change it behind the Organizer's back — by default it stores no end at all and lasts until its Event is over, which ends every register Grant; presets such as "until registration closes" or "until the public opening" copy the Track's instant when the Grant is issued, and moving that instant lists the Grants that copied it, which do not move. Explicit windows shift with a reschedule like the Track's instants and never extend past the Event's end, and a register Grant ends when its Event is over. Early access is a Grant usable before the public opening; a late friend or a door walk-in uses a Grant after the close — publication closes public registration (ADR 0016), and this is how people still get in, as pending changes the Organizer decides on. Once a Grant's window has ended it stays ended: every mutation treats a Grant as ended when its end has passed, whether or not settle has recorded it yet, and a later reopening or reschedule does not revive it. A full Track, for the holder of a Grant that does not reserve, means the public seats — which are zero whenever someone waits — so a shared late link never jumps the Waitlist. A full Track admits a Grant's holder only through a Grant that reserves its seats — a flag, so it reserves exactly its remaining uses and its holders never wait — and issuing one whose uses exceed the Track's free Capacity is refused. Approval, when it arrives, is one more flag.
- **Absolute gates stay absolute** — the list lives in the one decision function (`docs/specs/events.md`): among them the Event being a draft, cancelled, over, being deleted, or suspended; the Track's tournament live or completed; the Roster limit; the Track's reachability requirement (ADR 0013); the Waitlist's (ADR 0015); a block on the person — a flag the Organizer sets, as a previewed operation, on any ended Entry that had a holder (removing their active Entry there too, if any) — which only the Organizer lifts, by unblocking. Someone who never held an Entry on the Track cannot be blocked there; they have no identity the Organizer could name.
- **Uses are derived, never counted:** (a reserving Grant, which exists only on a capped Track whose registrations already serialize on its seat document, also caches its remaining uses there) a register Grant's uses are the Entries created through it that are still active or were removed by the Organizer, a continuation chain (restoration, reset) counting once — a self-cancellation frees its use, a removal does not, so a removed person cannot walk back in on the same single-use link from a fresh identity (on a multi-use link they can, which is why the removal preview offers to revoke it). A claim Grant is used while its Entry has a holder; undoing a wrong claim re-enters the Entry with a fresh claim link. A person's account deletion revokes the claim Grants of their Entries, and an Organizer's revokes the Grants they issued (ADR 0022). No shared counter is written by concurrent registrations (except a capped Track's seat document, on which its registrations serialize anyway), and history stays in the Activity log. Public free seats are Capacity minus confirmed Entries, outstanding Offers, and unused reserved seats, each seat counted once — a reserving Grant's unused reserved seats being its remaining uses (never below zero), and none once it has ended for good (revoked, its window over, its Event cancelled or over; a draft or suspended Event or a window not yet open keeps them counted), so a Grant releases its seats when it ends. Invitees beyond its use limit take a second link that does not reserve and queue like anyone else. Revocation stores its instant and sets a register Grant's end to it, so a revoked register Grant is one whose end has passed. It never silently ends Entries already made through the Grant; it lists them, and revoking together with the ones still waiting is a previewed operation that marks the Grant withdrawing — those Entries then count as removed for every gate, and settle writes the removals in bounded pages.
- **Claims are subject to the registration gates that concern the person** — block, one active Entry per user per Track, the Event's lifecycle, suspension — but take no seat, so they ignore windows, publication, and a live tournament: a player entered by name can claim on the day, and the Organizer is told. A claim Grant has no window and is only ever revoked: it lasts while its Entry is active and unclaimed and a claim would pass the Event's lifecycle — published, not suspended, and neither cancelled nor over unless the Entry's tournament exists and has been published, that extension capped at seven days after the Event's end — so a claim during a tournament that outlives its Event still works — the link itself showing only that bracket, and the claimed Entry granting what any Registrant's does. Only active Entries can be claimed, so a leaked claim link is no way back in.

Organizers deliver Grants themselves, copying a link into a DM or a channel. cobracket never emails an address an Organizer typed in — that would let anyone make cobracket mail strangers (ADR 0014). It delivers invitations only to people who already have a relationship with the Organizer, such as past Registrants, through their verified channels. This differs from Luma, which sends invitations to any entered address; it can be revisited once Organizers themselves can be verified.

## Considered Options

- **A Grant naming which gates it passes** (before opening, after closing, invite-only, hidden, full). A matrix of flags nobody needs at launch, and every combination is a window plus a use limit that may reserve its seats.
- **Binding a Grant to one user.** It needs a reachable user the Organizer already knows, which does not exist before account sign-in, and its only other effect — letting a blocked person back — is served by unblocking. Deferred until inviting known people is built.
- **Counting uses in a field.** Every registration through a popular link would write the same document.

## Consequences

- A Grant lets its presenter view its scope only while it can still be acted on (ADR 0019); opening it stores nothing, and after acting, the Entry is the basis of access.
- Links carry long random secrets in the URL fragment, so no server log sees them, and the page strips them from history after reading. Typed codes are short, so they are exchanged for the long secret by a mutation rate-limited per (Event, user) with a global per-user cap — slowing attempts rather than locking anyone out — and entered only on pages the caller can already view; a private Event is reachable by link only. Registration and claim Grants are stored so an Organizer can copy them again; stronger Grants, such as a co-organizer invite, decide on hashing when they are built.
- A restricted pre-sale ("only invited people can see and register") is a published private Event switched to unlisted later — never a draft.
- An Organizer may create an Entry for themselves as holder, as with Entries entered by name: Organizer entries skip the window, the Track setting, Capacity, reachability, others' blocks, and a live or completed tournament, but not the Event being cancelled (except on a Track whose tournament has been published and not deleted), suspended, or tombstoned, the Roster limit, or — for their own Entry — one active Entry per Track. The Organizer's block list shows the blocked Entry's display name and the participant number scoped to the Organizer, never a user id; an Organizer may also ban a person from all of their Events, an absolute gate like a block. Bans and operator suspensions outlive the person's account deletion as dormant rows keyed by a tag of the email, re-attached when that email is verified again; per-Track blocks do not (ADR 0022).
