# A Ranking is a publisher's reading of public results: any Community publishes one, over the tournaments it chooses, with every intervention and every change on record

Amended by [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) (2026-10-15): a Ranking stands Players or teams, chosen at creation; a team is a chain of Entries identified by Squad or by shared players; every Revision stores its inputs (the Entries, Player ids, and join rule per chain, the Entries a placement tie-break decided, and, in its header, the Season's settings as applied and the counted tournaments' result versions) and a `players` row is never deleted, a merge aliasing it; the intervention set gains two team kinds — an Entry declared a new team, or the same team as an earlier Entry — and a point adjustment or an exclusion may target a Player across every team they are on; a Season's scope gains a lineup-size condition; a team Ranking counts Track tournaments only, since a standalone tournament has no Entries — as does a Player Ranking in a team Discipline, which credits through Entries — and the Season preview names the ones skipped; seeding from a team Ranking matches each registered Entry's playing lineup to a row by the chain predicate and stores the basis on the Participant; a team Season is also recomputed on a claim or its reset, a name link or refusal, a merge, a held-on date change, or a tournament's existence changing, and since chain assignment is separate from scoring a result correction rescores on the existing chains unless it changed a placement a tie-break read (ADR 0030).

The vision settled that a Ranking has a publisher, a Method, a Season, and a scope of counted tournaments, and left open who may publish, how a tournament gets into a scope, and how official Rankings keep farmed events out. The first draft of this decision had Communities counting their own Events and other Communities' by consent, and Organizers marking casual tournaments as "not for ranking". The grilling of 2026-10-14 replaced that with the model the strongest real rankings use — UltRank (Smash) is made by people who organize nothing, from results they did not record, and remains the discipline's authority — and the owner's own framing: results are public facts, and a Ranking is one publisher's reading of them, which anyone may offer and everyone can inspect. [ADR 0026](./0026-ranking-methods-three-types-named-knobs.md) decides how the reading is computed.

**Any Community publishes Rankings, and the publisher is always a Community.** A person who wants to rank a discipline publishes through their own first Community (ADR 0023: created with the account, named after them, not special), so a "ranking curator" who organizes nothing and a Community ranking its own circuit are the same thing to the system, and cobracket's official Rankings are those published by the operator's Community, marked as official on the page. There is no second publisher kind and no approval step: an account with a verified email (ADR 0024) and the organizer role or above (ADR 0023's table gains the row) creates one. Limits per Community and per Ranking belong to the anti-spam decision, and the operator's suspension applies as to any Organizer-side content.

**A Ranking counts tournaments, chosen by its publisher, without the tournament's Organizer consenting.** The unit is the tournament (the bracket whose results exist); choosing an Event means its tournaments in the Ranking's Discipline. A Season's scope is a set of conditions (Discipline, owning Communities, dates, minimum entrants, and the fields a tournament carries) plus tournaments added and excluded by hand, so a monthly series is a condition and a curated circuit is a list; a "series" object is not introduced. A condition is evaluated against the tournament's current owner, so a hand-over (ADR 0023) moves a tournament between condition-based scopes while hand-picked tournaments stay. A public tournament's results are already readable by anyone through its Share Link, so counting them is citation, not use of someone's data: an Organizer who wants a tournament out of every public Ranking makes it unlisted, and out of every Ranking makes it private. For the same reason there is no Organizer-side "casual, do not rank" mark; whether a four-player weeknight counts is the publisher's call, through their minimum-entrants condition or by hand.

**A Ranking is never more visible than what it counts.** A publisher may count any non-private tournament they can view (`canView`, ADR 0019), and the Ranking's own Visibility is capped at the narrowest counted tournament: a public Ranking counts only public tournaments, an unlisted one may count unlisted tournaments (its link reveals no more than forwarding theirs would), and a private tournament is counted by nobody, its own Community included. This is ADR 0019's rule that nothing reaching people indirectly leaks unlisted participation, applied to the one indirect surface that may legitimately read unlisted results.

**Players do not opt out, and names resolve at read.** A Ranking is a reading of results tables that already carry the names, so a person is in it as they are in those tables; removing someone would move everyone else for one person's preference. A Player who hides their Player page is listed without a link to it; a deleted account is listed under the name its records keep (ADR 0022), and a person who signs up again is a new Player (no operator merge). A name that must go is the operator's redaction across every record (ADR 0022), which reaches Rankings because every stored standing holds Player ids and resolves names when displayed.

**Every change is a Revision, and every hand of the publisher is public.** There is no "final" edition to freeze: standings are recomputed from current results (ADR 0005) whenever a counted tournament completes, a result is corrected, or the publisher changes settings or a manual order, and each recomputation is stored as a Revision carrying its instant, the tournaments it counted, the diff from the previous Revision and its cause ("the result of X was corrected on 11/3"). A Season's last Revision at its end is its final standings, permanently linkable, and a later correction produces a newer Revision beside it rather than rewriting it. Settings and manual orders are drafted and published, so a session of edits is one Revision; result-driven recomputation needs no draft. Every intervention — a tournament added or excluded by hand, a tier assigned, a point adjustment, a typed name linked to a Player, a manual reorder — is listed on the Ranking with a reason from a fixed set (below minimum size, outside the scope, ruleset, publisher's judgement, …) and optional text, so the standings can be audited by anyone, and no accusation has to be written to exclude something. The official Rankings' policy follows: scoring and selection rules are published, the detection of fraud and each individual judgement are not, and the policy says the operator includes and excludes at its discretion.

**A Ranking is a series of Seasons.** The Ranking holds what identifies it — name, publisher, Discipline, Method type — and each Season holds its window (start, end or rolling length, and the timezone of its boundaries, all chosen by the publisher; a calendar year in the publisher's timezone by default; "recurring" creates the next Season with the settings copied), its scope, and its Method settings, so changing a points table for 2026 cannot move 2025's standings. Seasons may overlap. The Player page shows a person's standing across a Ranking's Seasons for public Rankings only (`canDiscover`, ADR 0019, as for results — an unlisted Ranking may count unlisted tournaments, so it reaches people by its link alone). Tournaments get a held-on date for window membership (an Event's date where attached; a standalone tournament's creation day, editable by the Organizer), since play can cross midnight and a result recorded a day early must not move a tournament into the wrong month. Only completed tournaments count.

**Rankings feed seeding by copy.** An Organizer seeds a bracket from any Ranking they can view; the standings are copied into the tournament at that instant (the Revision is named), unranked Participants follow in the tournament's other order, and the copy survives the Ranking's later Revisions and its deletion. A Ranking is derived, so it may be deleted, Revisions and all.

## Considered Options

- **Only the owning Community counts a tournament, and other Communities by consent** (a proposal issued by either side and accepted by the other, revocable). It makes a curator ask every Organizer, which is exactly what UltRank does not do, and it gives an Organizer a veto over readings of public results that forwarding the Share Link already defeats. Visibility is the Organizer's tool — unlisted keeps a tournament out of public Rankings, private out of all — and it already exists.
- **An Organizer's "not for ranking" mark that every Ranking honours.** The mark is a request to publishers, not a fact about the tournament; a publisher may want to count it anyway, or exclude tournaments the mark does not cover. A tournament attribute publishers filter on can be added later if asked for.
- **Any public tournament countable without the publisher having to view it, or unlisted tournaments countable by their own Community in a public Ranking.** The first changes nothing (public is viewable); the second is the leak ADR 0019 closed.
- **A person publishes as themselves.** A second ownership path beside ADR 0023 for no gain, since the first Community is the person.
- **A frozen "final" edition per Season, or a publisher's choice between a live Ranking and editions.** Both exist to keep published standings from changing silently; Revisions with diffs and causes do that without contradicting ADR 0005, and remove the choice. A cutoff that computes a Season from results as recorded at an instant is reconstructible from the result log and can be added if a publisher asks.
- **Opt-out, or "hidden player" rows.** Opt-out moves other people's ranks; a hidden row hides nothing the results table shows.
- **Unique Community handles for Ranking URLs.** Deferred: id URLs with the publisher's name on the page serve until a public directory exists, and a handle can be added without breaking anything (nothing is shared yet).
- **A back-link from a tournament page to the Rankings that count it.** With anyone able to publish, that list is noise on the Organizer's page; the Ranking lists what it counts.

## Consequences

- Schema: `rankings` (Community, name, Discipline, Method type, Visibility), `rankingSeasons` (window, timezone, scope conditions, hand-picked and excluded tournaments, Method settings, draft and published states), `rankingRevisions` (instant, cause, standings as Player ids with scores, counted tournaments; per-player breakdowns in their own table), intervention rows with their reason category, and `tournaments.heldOn`. Recomputation runs as scheduled internal work, never inside the mutation that records a result, and a Revision says "as of" its instant.
- ADR 0023's capability table gains "Create, edit, publish, and delete Rankings" for owner, admin, and organizer; ADR 0019's `canDiscover` note on Ranking sources is amended to the cap above. Every publisher capability is a public function of a top-level `convex/` module (ADR 0001), so a Ranking is operable from chat.
- `docs/vision.md` closes its "official-ranking eligibility" question (the operator's Community curates, as any publisher does) and its seeding-provenance and season-timezone checklist items; `docs/specs/events.md` records the held-on date and the Player-page link rule.
- [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md) sets 20 Rankings per Community, 1,000 counted tournaments per Season, and the top 5,000 Players stored per Revision, and treats reason text as user content: reportable, never pre-moderated, redacted by the operator. Rankings skip an operator-suspended Event's tournaments.
- [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) (2026-10-15) decides what a Ranking in a team Discipline stands: Players or teams, chosen at creation, a team being a chain of Entries.
