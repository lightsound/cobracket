# A Ranking is a publisher's reading of public results: any Community publishes one, over the tournaments it chooses, with every intervention and every change on record

Amended by [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) (2026-10-15): a Ranking stands Players or teams, chosen at creation; a team is a chain of Entries identified by Squad or by shared players; every Revision stores its inputs (the Entries, Player ids, and join rule per chain, the Entries a placement tie-break decided, and, in its header, the Season's settings as applied and the counted tournaments' result versions) and a `players` row is never deleted, a merge aliasing it; the intervention set gains two team kinds — an Entry declared a new team, or the same team as an earlier Entry — and a point adjustment or an exclusion may target a Player across every team they are on; a Season's scope gains a lineup-size condition; a team Ranking counts Track tournaments only, since a standalone tournament has no Entries — as does a Player Ranking in a team Discipline, which credits through Entries — and the Season preview names the ones skipped; seeding from a team Ranking matches each registered Entry's playing lineup to a row by the chain predicate and stores the basis on the Participant; the Player page lists, from a public team Ranking the person picks, each chain one of whose Entries counted the person, with its standing, "as a member of <team>"; a merge recomputes the Seasons that counted either identity; a team Season is also recomputed on a claim or its reset, a name link or refusal, a held-on date change, or a tournament's existence changing, and since chain assignment is separate from scoring a result correction rescores on the existing chains unless it changed a placement a tie-break read (ADR 0030).

Market note (2026-10-22): the context below cites UltRank as ranking without asking any Organizer. Its 2025 rules are narrower: majors and regionals are counted without consent, weekly tournaments only when their Organizer opts in, and an Organizer who declines is honoured ([`docs/competitors.md`](../competitors.md)). The precedent therefore covers the no-consent rule for tournaments above weekly size and not the absence of an opt-out; the decision below stands as made — cobracket asks no consent at any size — with visibility as the Organizer's tool.

The vision settled that a Ranking has a publisher, a Method, a Season, and a scope of counted tournaments, and left open who may publish, how a tournament gets into a scope, and how official Rankings keep farmed events out. The first draft of this decision had Communities counting their own Events and other Communities' by consent, and Organizers marking casual tournaments as "not for ranking". The grilling of 2026-10-14 replaced that with the model the strongest real rankings use — UltRank (Smash) is made by people who organize nothing, from results they did not record, and remains the discipline's authority — and the owner's own framing: results are public facts, and a Ranking is one publisher's reading of them, which anyone may offer and everyone can inspect. [ADR 0026](./0026-ranking-methods-three-types-named-knobs.md) decides how the reading is computed.

**Any Community publishes Rankings, and the publisher is always a Community.** A person who wants to rank a discipline publishes through their own first Community (ADR 0023: created with the account, named after them, not special), so a "ranking curator" who organizes nothing and a Community ranking its own circuit are the same thing to the system, and cobracket's official Rankings are those published by the operator's Community, marked as official on the page. There is no second publisher kind and no approval step: an account with a verified email (ADR 0024) and the organizer role or above (ADR 0023's table gains the row) creates one. Limits per Community and per Ranking belong to the anti-spam decision, and the operator's suspension applies as to any Organizer-side content.

**A Ranking counts tournaments, chosen by its publisher, without the tournament's Organizer consenting.** The unit is the tournament (the bracket whose results exist); choosing an Event means its tournaments in the Ranking's Discipline. A Season's scope is a set of conditions (Discipline, owning Communities, dates, minimum entrants, and the fields a tournament carries) plus tournaments added and excluded by hand, so a monthly series is a condition and a curated circuit is a list; a "series" object is not introduced. A condition reads the tournament's owner and that owner's ancestors as recorded at the tournament's first completion, and a Community it names covers the Communities below it (ADR 0028) unless the publisher switches that off, so a league counts its clubs' tournaments without listing each club. Results count by the facts of their time: a Community's later link, unlink, or deletion leaves the tournaments already completed in its subtree where they were counted, and its new ancestors' conditions admit only those completed from then on, the earlier ones entering by hand. A tournament's own hand-over (ADR 0023), which also corrects one created in the wrong Community, records it again under its new owner and moves it only in Seasons not yet ended — a Season reads the record in force at its end, and the newest one while it has not ended — so each Season it leaves or enters is recomputed with the hand-over as the cause, and an ended Season keeps what it counted. The issuer's preview lists the Seasons not yet ended that count the tournament by condition, since the receiving Community is chosen only at acceptance, and the acceptor's lists the Seasons it leaves and enters (the split of the two previews is Claude's); each names the Rankings the confirming person views without a link and shows the others only as their publisher and a count. Hand-picked tournaments stay through all of this. Where a Community with a parent sets an Event's or a standalone tournament's Visibility, the choice says that the Community's ancestors' Rankings may count unlisted results and that private keeps them out of every Ranking, and a parent link's request and acceptance screens, its notices, and its Activity say the same (ADR 0028; the Activity entry is Claude's addition). A public tournament's results are already readable by anyone through its Share Link, so counting them is citation, not use of someone's data: an Organizer who wants a tournament out of every public Ranking makes it unlisted, and out of every Ranking makes it private. For the same reason there is no Organizer-side "casual, do not rank" mark; whether a four-player weeknight counts is the publisher's call, through their minimum-entrants condition or by hand.

**A Ranking is never more visible than what it counts.** A publisher may count any non-private tournament they can view (`canView`, ADR 0019) — a condition admits only public tournaments and those of its own Community and of the Communities below it (ADR 0028), as recorded above, so a tournament it admitted stays when the tournament's Community leaves the tree, and another Community's unlisted tournament enters by hand, from its link — and the Ranking's own Visibility is capped at the narrowest counted tournament: a public Ranking counts only public tournaments, an unlisted one may count unlisted tournaments (its link reveals no more than forwarding theirs would), and a private tournament is counted by nobody, its own Community included. The cap governs what a Ranking shows as well as what it counts: a tournament it may no longer show — since made private, unlisted under a public Ranking, or suspended — reads "withdrawn" wherever the Ranking names it, in every stored Revision (final standings included) and every intervention, its points kept so a row still adds up but its name, link, and results hidden from anyone who cannot view it, so the Organizer's withdrawal reaches the Ranking's history and not only its next Revision; and a Season names for prior standing (ADR 0026) only a Revision of a Ranking at least as visible as its own. A Ranking links a tournament it names by the Share Link the tournament had at its first completion, and by none once that link has been regenerated (ADR 0019), so it never hands out a link that replaced a leaked one. This is ADR 0019's rule that nothing reaching people indirectly leaks unlisted participation, applied to the one indirect surface that may legitimately read unlisted results. Public means for a Ranking what it means for an Event — findable without the link, once cobracket has a directory (ADR 0019) — and the directory lists public Rankings by Discipline, the official mark on the operator's; how that list is ordered is a UI decision.

**Players do not opt out, and names resolve at read.** A Ranking is a reading of results tables that already carry the names, so a person is in it as they are in those tables; removing someone would move everyone else for one person's preference. A Player who hides their Player page is listed without a link to it; a deleted account is listed under the name its records keep (ADR 0022), and a person who signs up again is a new Player (no operator merge; the one join is the person's own, at deletion — handing the Player's history to another account of theirs, ADR 0022 — which marks the row an alias resolved at read, as a merge's is, ADR 0030). A name that must go is the operator's redaction across every record (ADR 0022), which reaches Rankings because every stored standing holds Player ids and resolves names when displayed.

**Every change is a Revision, and every hand of the publisher is public.** There is no "final" edition to freeze: standings are recomputed from current results (ADR 0005) whenever a counted tournament completes, a result is corrected, a hand-over moves a tournament into or out of a Season not yet ended (above), or the publisher changes settings or a manual order, and each recomputation is stored as a Revision carrying its instant, the tournaments it counted, the diff from the previous Revision and its cause ("the result of X was corrected on 11/3"). A Season's last Revision at its end is its final standings, linkable while the Ranking exists (below), and a later correction produces a newer Revision beside it rather than rewriting it; pages read a Season by its newest Revision, an ended Season marked so and linking its final standings where the two differ. Settings and manual orders are drafted and published, so a session of edits is one Revision; result-driven recomputation needs no draft. Every intervention — a tournament added or excluded by hand, a tier assigned, a point adjustment, a typed name linked to a Player, a manual reorder — is listed on the Ranking with a reason from a fixed set (below minimum size, outside the scope, ruleset, publisher's judgement, …) and optional text, so the standings can be audited by anyone, and no accusation has to be written to exclude something. The official Rankings' policy follows: scoring and selection rules are published, the detection of fraud and each individual judgement are not, and the policy says the operator includes and excludes at its discretion. Nothing detects fraud automatically: the operator investigates and suspends (ADR 0027) from what results already record — who entered each result, when, and every correction.

**A Ranking is a series of Seasons.** The Ranking holds what identifies it — name, publisher, Discipline, Method type — and each Season holds its window (start, end or rolling length, and the timezone of its boundaries, all chosen by the publisher; a calendar year in the publisher's timezone by default; "recurring" creates the next Season with the settings copied; the official Rankings' length is chosen when the operator starts publishing them), its scope, and its Method settings, so changing a points table for 2026 cannot move 2025's standings. Seasons may overlap. The Player page shows a person's standing across a Ranking's Seasons only for the public Rankings the person picks (`canDiscover`, ADR 0019, as for results — an unlisted Ranking may count unlisted tournaments, so it reaches people by its link alone): the person picks from a list, in their settings, of the public Rankings they appear in, a pick holds for the Ranking's later Seasons, each is shown with its publisher's name, and a person who picks none shows no Ranking standings, their tournament results staying — so a Ranking anyone may publish, a harassing or advertising one included, reaches a person's page only by that person's choice: what others publish about a person reaches their page once they accept it, as a publisher's name link does (ADR 0026), and a well-regarded Ranking spreads through the people who pick it. A person may pick a Ranking they publish themselves, its publisher's name telling readers whose reading it is (the owner's call, 2026-10-17; a team's Community page shows what its owner, an admin, or an organizer picks, ADR 0028). Picks belong to the page, a Player's or a Community's, so a Player page kept visible after its account's deletion (ADR 0022) keeps the picks it had (Claude's detail). The page leaves out, picked or not, a standing that rests in any part on a publisher's name link the person has not accepted (ADR 0026; that it covers a standing resting on the link in any part, a team's row included, is Claude's reading of the call, which said a standing the link produced), so a publisher's mistaken link never travels with their name. That link is a bearer slug, as an Event's (ADR 0019, since document ids are not capabilities): anyone holding it opens the Ranking, its Seasons, and their Revisions, final standings included, while the Ranking is unlisted or public, the publisher may regenerate it, and the Ranking's id route needs a relation, as every id route does. Tournaments get a held-on date for window membership (an Event's date where attached; a standalone tournament's creation day, editable by the Organizer), since play can cross midnight and a result recorded a day early must not move a tournament into the wrong month. Only completed tournaments count.

**Rankings feed seeding by copy.** An Organizer seeds a bracket from any Ranking they can view; the standings are copied into the tournament at that instant (the Revision is named), unranked Participants follow in the tournament's other order, and the copy survives the Ranking's later Revisions and its deletion. A Ranking is derived, so its publisher may delete it, Revisions and all, or any one of its Seasons (deleting one Season alone was Claude's choice in an earlier pass; the option the owner accepted kept the document's form, which included it), at any time: a two-phase operation (ADR 0017) whose preview says how many Player and Community pages pick the Ranking and what survives — the seeding copies already taken from it (above), the prior-standing copies other Seasons took from a named Revision (ADR 0026), the tournaments' own results, and the versions the operator keeps for 30 days (ADR 0027); a page that picked it loses the standing with no notice, as it loses a deleted standalone tournament's result. The owner's call (2026-10-22), on the reasoning put to them: a Ranking is a reading of results whose Organizer may delete them after completion, so it is protected no more strongly than they are; the form the owner chose on 2026-10-17 for a deleted Community's Rankings (below) stands; and closing a Ranking instead while a page picks it would add rules it cannot keep, since the publisher switching it to unlisted already takes it off Player pages. The option as sent also dropped the document's two conflicting reasons, "final standings always linkable" and "recomputable, so deletable", which a manual order is not. The one cost, an ended Season's final standings no longer linkable, falls on readers, who can keep their own copy (Claude's note). When its Community is deleted (ADR 0023), a Ranking with a Revision is kept under the Community's frozen name and Visibility, since the Player pages that picked it show its standings as they show the kept Events' results, and nothing that needs its publisher happens again: no setting, manual order, or intervention changes, no recurring Season is created, a rolling window stops moving, and no tournament its last Revision did not count is admitted. Changes to what it already counts still recompute it as they would any Ranking — a corrected result, a claim, a refused name link, a merge, or a tournament handed over out of its scope in a Season not yet ended, made private, made unlisted under a public Ranking, suspended, or deleted — so the deletion of its publisher never holds a person to a link they refused or keeps counting a tournament its Organizer withdrew from it. A Ranking or Season with no Revision is deleted with the drafts, and the owner may delete any Ranking first.

## Considered Options

- **Only the owning Community counts a tournament, and other Communities by consent** (a proposal issued by either side and accepted by the other, revocable). It makes a curator ask every Organizer, which is exactly what UltRank does not do, and it gives an Organizer a veto over readings of public results that forwarding the Share Link already defeats. Visibility is the Organizer's tool — unlisted keeps a tournament out of public Rankings, private out of all — and it already exists.
- **An Organizer's "not for ranking" mark that every Ranking honours.** The mark is a request to publishers, not a fact about the tournament; a publisher may want to count it anyway, or exclude tournaments the mark does not cover. A tournament attribute publishers filter on can be added later if asked for.
- **Any public tournament countable without the publisher having to view it, or unlisted tournaments countable by their own Community in a public Ranking.** The first changes nothing (public is viewable); the second is the leak ADR 0019 closed.
- **Evaluating conditions against the tournament's current owner and tree** (this decision's first form). A club that moved to another league in July took its January-to-June tournaments out of its old league's running Season and put them into its new league's, ended Seasons changed at their next recomputation under an unrelated cause, and a Ranking handed out the link an Organizer had regenerated to shut people out. Freezing only ended Seasons still emptied the running one; recomputing on every move still rewrote the past; letting a child refuse its ancestors' Rankings is the rejected "not for ranking" mark again; and counting descendants by condition only in Rankings an owner or admin publishes keeps a parent's organizer from reading, through a Ranking, unlisted tournaments they cannot open, at the cost of leagues whose ranking an organizer keeps — a trade, not an improvement. Re-counting a handed-over tournament in every Season put tournaments the new owner never ran into its ended Seasons, and fixing the link at a Ranking's first count showed a regenerated link in a Season opened later. Counting descendants always, the publisher excluding a branch's tournaments one by one, and counting the named Community alone, each branch added by hand and added again as branches change, were put beside the switch on 2026-10-17; neither answered the moved branch, since under the second its tournaments stop being counted by condition as well. The owner chose (2026-10-17) the record at first completion above.
- **A person publishes as themselves.** A second ownership path beside ADR 0023 for no gain, since the first Community is the person.
- **A frozen "final" edition per Season, or a publisher's choice between a live Ranking and editions.** Both exist to keep published standings from changing silently; Revisions with diffs and causes do that without contradicting ADR 0005, and remove the choice. A cutoff that computes a Season from results as recorded at an instant is reconstructible from the result log and can be added if a publisher asks.
- **Opt-out, or "hidden player" rows.** Opt-out moves other people's ranks; a hidden row hides nothing the results table shows.
- **Every public Ranking a person appears in on their Player page, the person unable to choose** (written after the grilling, which had left that choice to implementation; the owner chose otherwise on 2026-10-17). Anyone may publish a Ranking, so a harassing or advertising one would reach the page of everyone it lists; letting the person hide one shows it until they notice and needs a hide per publisher that outlasts a re-created Community; showing a default set first (the official Ranking) singles out the operator's Community, which is an ordinary Community.
- **Unique Community handles for Ranking URLs.** Deferred: the Ranking's slug link (above) with the publisher's name on the page serves until a public directory exists, and a handle can be added without breaking anything (nothing is shared yet).
- **Organizers applying to a Ranking** (start.gg's event submission). Not built: publishers find and add tournaments, and a condition already admits matching ones without asking.
- **Handing a Ranking to another Community**, as tournaments are handed over (ADR 0023). Not built: nothing asks for it yet, and the same Grant can carry it when something does.
- **Keeping a deleted Ranking's ended Seasons readable at their final standings** (the audit's alternative of 2026-10-17, withdrawn on 2026-10-18: a Ranking that publishes a past year is "ended" from its start, so one created by mistake could never be deleted), **or closing it instead of deleting it while any page picks it** (the alternative put beside deletion whole on 2026-10-18: read-only, as a deleted Community's Rankings are, its Seasons deletable only before they end, deletion possible again once no page picks it — not recommended, since it adds rules yet cannot hold: the publisher switching the Ranking to unlisted already takes it off Player pages). Announcing the deletion and deleting days later (a waiting mechanism and a notice, for people who can do nothing about it) and copying the person's own standing onto the pages that picked it (a standing without its table, which nobody can check) were named as dropped, with these reasons, in the message of 2026-10-18 that put deletion whole beside the closing, and leaving only a tombstone where the Ranking stood was dropped in the search before the question was put. The copies seeding and prior standing took survive either way, which is what other publishers rely on. Found in that search: Strava lets a segment's creator delete it, and it survives the creator's leaving; GitHub keeps forks when a repository is deleted; FIDE publishes its past rating lists; start.gg shows rankings on profiles, and speedrun.com's boards belong to their communities (start.gg's handling of deletion, and part of speedrun.com's, unconfirmed); and allowing only withdrawal, never deletion, was dropped with the tombstone. The owner chose deletion whole (2026-10-22).
- **A deleted Community's Rankings handed to its parent or a successor, excluded at display time, corrected by the operator by hand, or disposed of by the creator at deletion.** Dropped in the search of 2026-10-17 that strengthened the kept-and-frozen form (Decision) after the owner had answered that the form, put to them as the recommendation, stood unless a better one was found; the search found none, and these were not shown.
- **A back-link from a tournament page to the Rankings that count it.** With anyone able to publish, that list is noise on the Organizer's page; the Ranking lists what it counts.

## Consequences

- Schema: `rankings` (Community, name, Discipline, Method type, Visibility), `rankingPicks` (a Player or a Community, and a Ranking its page shows), `rankingSeasons` (window, timezone, scope conditions, hand-picked and excluded tournaments, Method settings, draft and published states), `rankingRevisions` (instant, cause, standings as Player ids with scores, counted tournaments; per-player breakdowns in their own table), intervention rows with their reason category, `tournaments.heldOn`, and, from a tournament's first completion, its owner and that owner's ancestor ids (recorded again, with the instant, at each later hand-over) and the Share Link slug it had at that completion. Recomputation runs as scheduled internal work, never inside the mutation that records a result, and a Revision says "as of" its instant.
- ADR 0023's capability table gains "Create, edit, publish, and delete Rankings" for owner, admin, and organizer; ADR 0019's `canDiscover` note on Ranking sources is amended to the cap above. Every publisher capability is a public function of a top-level `convex/` module (ADR 0001), so a Ranking is operable from chat.
- `docs/vision.md` closes its "official-ranking eligibility" question (the operator's Community curates, as any publisher does) and its seeding-provenance and season-timezone checklist items; `docs/specs/events.md` records the held-on date and the Player-page link rule.
- [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md) sets 20 Rankings per Community, 1,000 counted tournaments per Season (the first 1,000 in held-on order count, ties broken by completion instant and then id; the rest are listed on the Ranking as over the limit, and a hand-add past it is refused with the reason), and the top 5,000 Players stored per Revision, and treats reason text as user content: reportable, never pre-moderated, redacted by the operator. Rankings skip an operator-suspended Event's tournaments.
- [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) (2026-10-15) decides what a Ranking in a team Discipline stands: Players or teams, chosen at creation, a team being a chain of Entries.
