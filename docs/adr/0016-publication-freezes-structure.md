# Publication freezes a tournament's structure; Entries reach a Roster only until the Organizer pins it

Amended by [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) (2026-10-15): an Entry carries a team name and its Entrants carry their own names. The Organizer's rename of a team name locks the Entry (the lock survives the captain flag passing); the Organizer's rename of an Entrant's name keeps this decision's lock, for that person on that Track. The withdrawn, substituted, and restored Participant below stands for an Entry; its lineup of playing Entrants may change under ADR 0029's swap deadline without touching the bracket, and a restoration re-enters the Entrants as typed-name copies with the Entry's claim link. Undoing a wrong claim is no longer part of the re-point operation below: the reset happens at the Entrant (ADR 0029) — the wrong claimer's row ends and a typed-name row is re-entered in its place — while the Entry, its seat, and its Participant stay, so the re-point primitive keeps substitution and restoration only, and the reset still notifies as before. "Holder" below reads as the Entry's Entrants (ADR 0029).

Once players can see a bracket they prepare for its matchups, so nothing may change them behind their backs. The MVP allowed roster edits, seeding changes, format changes, and regeneration while a tournament was published — each of which either left a published tournament with no bracket or silently reshuffled the one players had seen — because unpublishing did not exist. Tracks make this acute: registrations keep arriving after an Organizer has settled a bracket. We decide:

- **Structure changes only in draft.** Roster membership, seeding, format, and the bracket are editable only while a tournament is a draft; publication fixes the structure players see. Labels — the tournament's name and Discipline, and Participants' display names — stay editable in every status (ADR 0011). Unpublishing (published → draft) exists and is allowed only from published — which, since the first result makes a tournament live, means only while no result exists. The rule applies to standalone tournaments as well.
- **A withdrawn Participant's unplayed matches are walkovers, derived.** The engine treats a match whose one side is withdrawn and unplayed as the other side's walkover, so a withdrawal neither needs the Organizer nor makes a tournament live; the walkover is recorded by the system only when a result that depends on it is recorded, after which it is an ordinary result (amended 2026-09-30). A pinned Participant's withdrawal therefore no longer stops automatic publication, which drops withdrawn Participants it does not refill (below).
- **Substitution is not a structural change.** Re-pointing a withdrawn Participant that has no recorded result to another confirmed Entry — its label taken from the new Entry — leaves every slot, seed, and result where it was (ADR 0005), so it is allowed in draft, published, and live — the way a walk-in takes a no-show's place on the day — as a previewed operation. It checks for results in the same transaction, which serializes it with result recording. A withdrawn Participant that already has a result cannot be substituted: its remaining matches stay derived walkovers unless it is restored: if the person withdrew by mistake, the Organizer re-enters the Entry by name in place — keeping its confirmation instant and Grant link — and sends them a fresh claim link (also left in their inbox if the Entry was their own), so the Participant keeps its results and the person takes it back by claiming. Such re-entries continue the old Entry: they take no new seat and no new Grant use. Substitution, restoration, and undoing a wrong claim are one re-point operation: it updates the last publication's record of who stands in the slot and tells the new holder — and, once published, for substitution and reset, the person replaced and the opponent if what they see changes. Results can still be recorded for withdrawn Participants.
- **Until the Organizer pins it, the draft Roster is a projection.** A Track tournament's draft Roster and bracket are computed when read from the Track's confirmed Entries, the format, and the seeding order — random (each Entry ordered by a hash of a stored salt and its id, so a newcomer never moves anyone else and reshuffling is a new salt), confirmation order, or later a Ranking — plus fixed seeds: chosen Entries held at chosen seeds, so an Organizer can seed the top players by hand and still let everyone else flow in. Changing any of these inputs does not pin, and removing an Entry simply ends it. The Roster pins into stored Participants on an explicit pin or at publication. Either action carries every projection input the Organizer saw; it refuses if anything other than new arrivals changed, pins exactly the Entries shown, drops any that ended meanwhile and says so, and leaves later arrivals as pending changes. Registrations never write tournament documents, and a Track tournament has no Participant without an Entry.
- **After pinning, Entries never change the Roster by themselves.** The difference is derived and shown as pending changes — confirmed Entries not on the Roster and withdrawn Participants (whose Entry is no longer active) — which the Organizer applies in draft all at once, one by one, or as substitutions. Names are labels, not structure: a rename of an Entry writes its Participant's label at once in every status, the Organizer's rename locking it (amended 2026-09-30). Applied additions take the next seeds within the Roster limit. Any Entry id a client names — shown, fixed, substituted, or applied — must be an active confirmed Entry of that Track not already on the Roster. The one exception to "by themselves" is automatic publication, which the Organizer opted into (below).
- **Publication is recorded, without copies.** Each tournament keeps its last publication — the structure, the seed order, and which Entry each Participant stood for, by id — in its own row, overwritten on every publication after the diff against the previous one is taken. Registrant status is derived against it, never against the draft; while an unpublished tournament is being edited, its Share Link by default shows only that the bracket is being updated, or — the Organizer's choice, previewed either way — keeps showing that publication under a "being updated" banner. Names resolve from the current Participant rows only — a Participant removed since shows a neutral placeholder — so an Organizer who renames or removes an offensive name does so everywhere at once. An Organizer's rename, made while a person holds the Entry, locks the name for that person on that Track: their holder cannot rename it back, and a new Entry they make there inherits it.
- **Publication is announced as a diff.** Every publication notifies exactly the Participants whose first match or whose Entry changed since the last one, and shows the Organizer that impact before publishing — including, the first time, withdrawn Participants whose names become visible; the first publication is the diff against nothing.
- **Publication closes public registration.** While automatic publication is armed or failed its time counts as the close (derived, not written, so disarming loses nothing); any publication, and re-arming from failed, writes that effective close into the Track's close, a publication also capping it at its own instant. Unpublishing does not reopen it; moving the close later again is the explicit reopen, allowed only while the Track has no published tournament. Late arrivals come through invite links or the Organizer's own entries and wait as pending changes; when the tournament goes live, those still off the Roster are told they are not in the bracket, as is the Organizer.
- **Automatic publication is opt-in and runs from the Track's due instants.** Enabling it — itself a previewed operation (ADR 0017), because it will publish names nobody has looked at — arms a publish time, refused on a cancelled Event or a tournament that is published, live, or completed. The default time (the earlier of the registration close and the Track's start) is derived, never stored, so it follows later edits to either — closing registration by hand publishes at once, which the close warns of; only a time the Organizer sets explicitly is stored. The time in force is one of the Track's due instants; when it comes, the Track's settle publishes everything confirmed by that time: an unpinned Roster pins the Entries confirmed by then, and on a pinned Roster Entries confirmed by then fill withdrawn Participants' seeds first, in confirmation order, then the next seeds, and withdrawn Participants left over are taken off (amended 2026-10-11, after review: publishing the Roster as pinned showed people who had withdrawn before publication as walkovers, by name, and left Entries confirmed before the close undecided). If fewer than two Participants would be published, or publication is refused for any reason — the Event not published or suspended among them — it pins and publishes nothing, becomes failed — its time still counts as the close, so registration stays closed as Registrants were told, and it is never due again — and tells the Organizer once; the ways out are disarming (the Track's own close applies again), re-arming with a future time (the close is written as the earlier of it and the failed time unless the Organizer also reopens), or publishing. Any publication disarms it; re-arming needs an explicit action and a future time. A reschedule that moves an explicit time into the past sets it to the commit, like any other instant, so the tournament publishes then; disarming instead would have reopened registration until the Event's end. Public registration is also refused while the tournament is published, beside the close, since a registration decides with the clock its function started at and can commit after the publication.

## Considered Options

- **Freeze at bracket generation.** Generation is repeatable and roster edits discard the bracket, so the freeze would silently lift itself.
- **Keep Entries and Roster in sync until publication, in stored rows.** Destroys a bracket an Organizer finished but has not published yet, and resumes on unpublishing — the surprise this ADR exists to prevent.
- **An asynchronous reconcile worker** (`@convex-dev/batch-worker`) keeping stored rows converged with a policy. It needed pings from every registration, debounce, re-reads that conflict with registration bursts, and pause-on-failure semantics the component does not provide; the read-time projection has none of these, at the cost of the Organizer's draft page re-running on each registration (bounded by the Roster limit).
- **A policy field choosing when the Roster pins** (immediately, at the first edit, at publication). Pinning immediately is attaching and pressing "pin"; pinning only at publication either refuses the Organizer's edits or is the same as the default. One rule — the projection lasts until the Organizer acts — covers all three.
- **A switch accepting late Entries after publication, and unpublishing that reopens registration.** Both let newcomers take freed seats ahead of the Waitlist, and every late case is already an invite link or an Organizer entry.
- **Substitution as a new Participant in the slot.** Slots are fixed at generation and results reference them; re-pointing a result-free Participant changes nothing results depend on.
- **Keeping every publication, with names.** Only the last one is ever read, and copied names outlive renames and pseudonymization.
- **Convex Triggers for the settle function; Workflow for offer chains.** Triggers act at a distance and commit a write whose trigger error was caught; Workflow cannot wait for an event with a timeout. Explicit calls from the one module that writes Entries, driven by a cron over due instants, are simpler.

## Consequences

- ADR 0005 is amended: progression is derived from the recorded results and the Participants' withdrawal state, and the system records a derived walkover before any result that depends on it.
- MVP stories 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, and 26 narrow to draft; renaming a Participant widens to every status; unpublishing moves out of the MVP's deferred list; generation under random seeding uses the stored salt (`docs/specs/events.md` records the changes).
- A Registrant's displayed status distinguishes "in the published bracket", "confirmed, awaiting the Organizer's decision", "confirmed, bracket not yet published", and "not in the bracket".
- A Track with a tournament is capped at the Roster limit (128 today) on every path that confirms an Entry or applies a change; larger events need phases or pools, which are not part of the first Events feature line.
