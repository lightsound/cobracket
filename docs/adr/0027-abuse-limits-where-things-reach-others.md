# Abuse limits sit where something reaches other people; the operator decides what is allowed, never who someone is

Amended by [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) and [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) (2026-10-15): new limits — per Community 20 Squads, 100 players per Squad, 10 child Communities, 50 affiliations; team names 40 and labels 20 characters; per account 500 Follows, 50 team invitations a day (requests to join a team included), and 10 affiliation requests a day; one unended join link per Entry; a logo is image-only, 512 KB, hidden while reported and removed as the sanction, since redaction does not apply to images — and the operator can suspend a Community as it suspends a person. Team names, labels, and Community profiles are user content under the rules below. The 90-day absence rule below is replaced: a designated successor (a member; the designation lapses when they leave or ownership moves) may request ownership at any time, a member without a designation after 30 days in which the owner did nothing in a session, and a child's owner last when a root has no other member and no designated successor; every request is a 14-day notice to the owner's address, served first-come; an owner suspended by the operator counts as absent from the first day and cannot cancel; a request that succeeds leaves the former owner a member as an admin, told (ADR 0028); there is no re-application ban. The holdings-cap row below reads "Entry and Entrant rows an anonymous identity holds" (its Entrant rows in every state and each Entry it has one on, once), and an Entry holds at most 32 active Entrant rows. "Block" below reads as a Track-scope sanction, and "holderless" as an Entrant row stripped of its identity and kept as a typed-name row while its Entry stays (ADR 0022's note, ADR 0029). The 10 owned Communities are counted over the nodes a person owns, children included (ADR 0028); a suspended captain's flag moves by ADR 0029's succession rule, never to a suspended person, and does not return when the suspension lifts (a waiting Entry passed over until it has moved, an Offer it holds returned first, its place kept); a suspended captain whose flag no one can take — every captain on a one-player Track — keeps it until someone can (the activation, claim, or lift that lets them moves it), the Entry left to its Organizer meanwhile as below, neither ended (a waiting one keeps its place, passed over by settle until the flag moves or the lift, an Offer it holds returned by the suspension) nor an Entry nobody can act for (ADR 0029). A suspended person receives only notices about their own status, as a sanctioned person does on a Track (a team invitation among them, its acceptance refused with the true reason, ADR 0029), none of the others replayed at the lift. A person's suspension writes its row first, every gate reading it at once, and runs the person-wide work behind it in pages, as a deletion's batches do — suspending the Events of the Communities whose sole member they are, withdrawing their pending requests (ADR 0029), returning the Offers of the waiting Entries whose flag they hold before moving the flags someone can take, and lowering those Tracks' due instants — so the lift below is accepted only once those pages are done, and pages its own work the same way (`docs/specs/events.md`, Suspending a person). A merge into a suspended account is refused as a suspended anonymous identity's is (ADR 0021), so no captain flag passes to a suspended person (ADR 0029). For the robot check below, joining a team through its join link without an email counts as registering without one: the check guards it, and the joiner's anonymous identity is minted there (ADR 0029).

Several decisions left their abuse side open: ADR 0021 set the first sign-in code limits and named the per-identity side as weak, ADR 0022 left the reaping of lost anonymous identities and the operator's workflow, ADR 0023 left what a suspended Event blocks, ADR 0024 left per-account creation limits, and ADR 0025 left the Ranking counts and how reason text is treated. The anti-spam grilling with the owner (2026-10-14/15) settled them together.

Three things are cheap for an abuser: an anonymous identity (free, minted on page load until now), an email account (one mailbox; throwaway addresses and Gmail aliases make it cheap), and a sign-in code email (cobracket pays, to any address). Nothing is discoverable yet (ADR 0019: `canDiscover` is false for everything until a directory exists), so whatever an abuser creates reaches only the people they send its link to; the value left to them is hosting a phishing page on cobracket's domain, sending mail at cobracket's cost, and evading an Organizer's block.

Two principles generate the decisions below:

- **Harm happens when something reaches other people, so the limits sit there**: on the button that mints a free identity or sends an email, and on publishing, issuing invite links, and sending Announcements — not on drafts, which nobody else sees.
- **The operator decides what is allowed, never who someone is.** A report only queues an item for the operator; nothing happens automatically. Accounts are never recovered by identity checks.

**A robot check where free things are minted.** Cloudflare Turnstile (free, usually invisible, on the provider that already serves the app) guards the two buttons that mint something free: registering without an email (including acting on a claim link) and sending a sign-in code. An anonymous identity is minted only then — no longer on page load, so Spectators and visitors never get one; a code request from a browser without an identity mints one behind the same check, since the code is bound to a session. The token is verified inside the sign-in action (Turnstile's siteverify), which Convex Auth runs as an action. Every per-identity limit already decided (code sends, blocks, typed-code attempts, the holdings cap) becomes meaningful, since each identity costs a solved check. No per-IP limit: Convex functions do not see the client's address, the browser talks to Convex directly so a Cloudflare rule never sees the traffic, Japanese mobile carriers and venue Wi-Fi put many real people behind one address, and Turnstile already weighs the address's reputation on Cloudflare's side.

**Sign-in code emails carry no user-typed text.** Fixed wording, the code, and its purpose (sign-in, email change, deletion) — never a display name or an Event's name, so a code request cannot carry an abuser's text to a stranger. There is no global circuit breaker on sending for now: each email costs a Turnstile solve, and the email provider's plan is a ceiling; the operator sets usage alerts in Resend and Convex instead (a configuration step, no code). A breaker that stopped only new addresses was rejected outright, since it would reveal which addresses have accounts.

**Rate limits sit on the account; size limits sit on the container.** The account is the only identity that costs something; a limit on a Community is evaded by making another Community. Size limits (the existing per-Event caps, the Ranking caps) protect one transaction's write limits and belong to the Event, the Community, or the Season. The values are fixed and generous, so a real Organizer never meets them; the operator raises one account's limit on request. A trust tier that grows with an account's history can be added later; disposable-domain blocklists are not used (list upkeep and false positives, for little gain over the check and the limits).

| Limit                                                                 | Value                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Communities an account owns                                           | 10                                                                                      |
| Events and standalone tournaments an account creates per day (drafts) | 100 together                                                                            |
| Events and standalone tournaments an account publishes per day        | 60 together                                                                             |
| Reports an account files per day                                      | 10                                                                                      |
| Rankings per Community                                                | 20                                                                                      |
| Tournaments counted per Season                                        | 1,000                                                                                   |
| Players stored per Revision                                           | the top 5,000 (below that, "unranked"); raising it means splitting the stored standings |
| Announcements per Event per day                                       | 100 in the app, of which at most 3 also go out by email                                 |
| Entries and participant-number rows an anonymous identity holds       | 100 (was a provisional 1,000)                                                           |

The creation limit is loose and the publish limit is the real one, because a draft harms nobody but the storage bill; both allow a year of weekly Events (52) prepared and published at once. The Announcement limit lets the Organizer choose "also by email" per Announcement and shows how many email sends remain today; past three, an Announcement reaches the app only. The ranked-player cap comes from Convex's one-megabyte document limit at about sixty bytes per standing.

**Content is not pre-moderated.** Every page carrying user-written content — an Event, a Community, a Ranking (its reason text included, which is user content like any other), an Announcement, a name — has a report button; the operator reviews and suspends or redacts. Nothing is indexed by search engines while it is not `canDiscover` (ADR 0019 already says search reaches only discoverable subjects; this makes it explicit: `noindex` on every page today), and links in user content carry `rel="nofollow ugc noopener"`. An interstitial for outbound links and a word filter were rejected: the first taxes every Organizer pasting a Discord link, the second loses to paraphrase and blocks legitimate names. Checking saved links against a known-phishing list (Google Web Risk, paid for commercial use) is a candidate once abuse appears.

**Reports.** Only an email account can report; a report names a type (phishing, harassment, other), may add a short text and nothing else — no screenshot, which the operator could not tell from a fabricated one —, and goes to the operator's queue only, never to the reported person or Community; repeated reports by one person on one subject count once; the daily limit above applies; filing reports is an action like any other, so the operator can suspend someone who keeps filing baseless ones. The evidence is the content's own history, kept for the operator: whenever an edit or a deletion replaces content others could see — published or sent, never a draft —, the replaced version is kept for 30 days, shown to the operator only, so an Announcement emailed and then deleted in the app, or a link swapped back before review, leaves its evidence, and nothing has to be frozen, which would tell the reported side. Every field others read keeps versions except the names of people, teams, Squads, labels, and Communities, whose own rules stand (a person who renamed to a pseudonym leaves no trail of the old name) — descriptions, links, logos, Announcements, and Ranking reason text among them. The history belongs to its subject — the Event, standalone tournament, Community, or Ranking — and not to its author, so the author's account deletion leaves it, the author shown as a deleted account, and the subject's own deletion keeps the versions others saw for the same 30 days. A report pins the versions of the past 30 days and those replaced while it is open, marking the one the reporter saw — on a page, the one shown; from an email, the one its report link names —; a report closed without action leaves them to expire as unpinned versions do, and one the operator acts on (a suspension, a redaction, a logo's removal) keeps them with the record of that action. Nothing but the operator's redaction (below) takes a version away early, and where the email service keeps the bodies it sent, they go within the same 30 days. A report keeps no tag of the reported person after their deletion — they cannot know they were reported, so a deletion is never a flight from one, and what they wrote stays with its subject for the operator to act on.

**Suspending a person.** The operator can suspend an account. The person can still sign in and sees only the reason, a route to the operator, their own data, and account deletion; they cannot create, operate, register, report, or accept a Grant. Every Event and standalone tournament of a Community whose sole member they are is suspended with them, and its Rankings are withdrawn as a suspended Community's are (ADR 0028); a Community with other members carries on without them (their membership is inert while suspended). Their Entries in other Communities' Events are left to those Organizers. Deleting the account leaves the suspension dormant under the email tag for twelve months, as ADR 0022 already set for sanctions, and a suspended anonymous identity is refused the merge (ADR 0021). Lifting the suspension restores everything it suspended.

**Suspending an Event stops everything that reaches out and deletes nothing.** Beyond ADR 0019's visibility gate and ADR 0023's refusal of deletion and hand-overs: the pages, its tournaments' Share Links, every Grant, registration, Announcements, email and in-app deliveries, and the Discord webhook stop; the Organizer can view but not edit (so the evidence cannot be rewritten while suspended — what they want changed they tell the operator); Rankings do not count its tournaments while it is suspended. A standalone tournament is suspended the same way. Lifting restores all of it.

**Operator redaction replaces a name with a stable pseudonym.** When a name must go from records the person cannot edit — an Organizer-typed Entry, a name an Organizer renamed and locked, a deleted account's names — and the Organizers concerned cannot (gone, or many Communities), the operator replaces it through the `players` row with a pseudonym fixed per Player ("Player K7Q2") — or on the record itself where no Player carries the name (ADR 0029: a typed name has none until its claim) —, so brackets and Rankings stay readable and the same person reads as the same pseudonym everywhere; redacted text shows "hidden by the operator"; the Organizers concerned are told. Names resolve at read (ADR 0025), so Rankings follow without a recomputation. The operator can extend a redaction of text, or a logo's removal, to every kept version of it, pinned by a report or not (Reports, above), which is how personal data posted by mistake leaves the history before its 30 days.

**No account recovery by identity checks; a long-absent owner can be replaced.** An account whose mailbox is gone stays as it is: the operator does not move an account to a new address on someone's word (GitHub refuses the same; Slack transfers ownership only for legal entities; Discord's support moves a server only past 100 members and a month's absence). The person's own routes are changing the email while still signed in (sessions last thirty days of disuse) and the re-verification prompt a hard bounce raises (ADR 0021). So that a Community does not strand with its owner: when the owner has not signed in for 90 days, the successor they designated (ADR 0023's designation for deletion), or failing one the first organizer to ask, may request ownership; the owner's address is told, and ownership moves after 14 days without objection — signing in and declining is the objection (replaced by ADR 0028, see the note above). A childless root Community whose sole member lost their mailbox is not recoverable, as on the services above (ADR 0028: a child's parent names its new owner, and a root with children passes to a child's owner); its Events end on their dates and keep their results.

**A lost anonymous identity is reaped when its session expires.** An anonymous identity has no credential but its session, and a session ends after thirty days of disuse (ADR 0021), so on the thirty-first day nobody can use the identity again. Reaping at that point needs no new period: the identity is deleted and its Entries in other people's Events are detached (ADR 0022: shown to the Organizer as holderless, never cancelled). The identity records when it was last used, written when its session refreshes.

## Considered Options

- **Per-IP limits, on Convex or through a Cloudflare rule.** Not visible to Convex functions; the traffic does not pass Cloudflare; shared addresses at venues and on mobile carriers stop real people; Turnstile already uses the signal.
- **A proof-of-work challenge instead of Turnstile** (no third party). Weaker against botnets, and the app already runs on Cloudflare.
- **Requiring an email for every registration**, dropping anonymous-allowed Tracks. Removes the free identity but loses the walk-in registration from a QR code that the Organizer opts into.
- **A circuit breaker on code emails now.** Kept for when sending is actually abused; a breaker for new addresses only would leak which addresses have accounts.
- **Limits per Community, or a trust tier from the start.** The first is evaded by creating Communities; the second needs a definition of trust and trips new Organizers.
- **Keeping a reported person's tag after deletion for 90 days** (the candidate in the events spec). The person cannot know they were reported, so there is nothing to flee; dropped.
- **Refusing deletion while a report is open.** Anyone could block anyone's deletion by reporting them.
- **A report's copy of the content as filed** (this decision's first form). Content replaced before anyone reported it — an Announcement emailed and then deleted in the app — left nothing to copy; the replaced versions above keep it. Showing that history to everyone, as X, Facebook, and GitHub do, would bring deleted harassment and personal data back into view; keeping it without a limit would hold personal data with no end; deleting it with its author's account would let a throwaway Organizer erase the evidence by deleting the account; and erasing unpinned versions on request, instead of redacting every version, left a pasted phone number in a pinned version, where refusing to remove it would reveal the report. Forbidding edits to sent Announcements cannot fix a typo and still needs a way to remove a pasted phone number; reporters' screenshots cannot be told from fabrications and miss what was read by email; emailing a link instead of the body loses reading at a venue without a connection. The owner chose (2026-10-17) the 30-day history above.
- **Recovering accounts on proof of identity.** A social-engineering entry point the operator cannot police.
- **Letting a suspended Organizer edit the Event.** It lets the evidence be rewritten during review.
- **Blanking a redacted name, or one shared placeholder.** The first edits records silently against ADR 0005; the second makes a bracket with two redacted people unreadable.

## Consequences

- `docs/specs/events.md`: the sign-in section mints identities at the guarded buttons and drops the "per-identity side to harden" note; the holdings cap becomes 100; the reaping period is the session's; the suspension of a person, Event suspension, redaction, reports, the Announcement limit, and the ownership request are specified; the anti-spam pending question is replaced by what remains (below). Slice 1 builds Turnstile, lazy minting, the holdings cap, the code-email wording, and the operator's suspension of a person (the merge refusal needs it); slice 2 the Community limit and the ownership request; slice 3 the creation and publish limits, `noindex`, `nofollow`, reports with the content versions they read, and the operator's Event suspension and redaction with the pages they act on; slice 6 reaping (it detaches Entries, which slice 6 brings); slice 9 the Announcement limit; the Ranking caps come with Rankings.
- ADR 0021's "a browser's identity is minted before cobracket knows who the person is" now holds only for a browser that registered without an email or asked for a code; ADR 0013's and ADR 0019's notes that every per-user limit waits on anti-spam measures are answered here; ADR 0022's reaping period and operator workflow, ADR 0023's suspended-Event question and its owner succession, ADR 0024's per-account limits, and ADR 0025's Ranking counts and reason text point here.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains Report and Suspension.
- Operator tooling (a queue of reports with the versions each pins, the suspend and redact actions) is internal functions plus a minimal operator page; it is not an Organizer capability, so it does not join the operations API. Versions are rows of their own (subject, field, the replaced value, its author, the instant it was replaced, and the reports or operator actions holding it), deleted by an internal job once 30 days have passed and nothing holds them.
- Still open, for later: a trust tier, the phishing-list check, trusted flaggers, and the professional legal review (which also covers how long report records and the operator's action records keep what they hold, and suspension tags).
