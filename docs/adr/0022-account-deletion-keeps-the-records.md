# Deleting an account removes the person at once and keeps the records; sanctions survive as dormant rows keyed by a tag of the email

Slice 4 is the first to store personal data — a verified email address, a display name, and the former names an Organizer sees (ADR 0013) — so how a person deletes it had to be decided before the owner shares the URL (`docs/specs/events.md`). The grilling of 2026-10-13/14 settled it on one principle: **the account belongs to the person; the records belong to the Event.** Deletion removes everything that exists only for the person and cuts the pointers from records to them, and edits no record. The one future an Organizer, a Registrant, and a Ranking all rely on — a bracket that still names who won — is the one deletion must not touch, which is why the first proposal, pseudonymizing names on deletion, was withdrawn by the owner: a Ranking whose first place turns into "deleted player" is a broken record, and a name in a published bracket is an official record of the competition, as it is for FIDE, athletics federations, and quiz leagues. Deletion is also never wider than what the person could already have done themselves: cancel their own Entries, cancel their own Events.

**What is removed, in the first transaction.** The `users` row (display name, former-name history, settings) and the verified email; sessions die with them, and a missing `users` row is the deletion marker every later write or delivery to that person checks. Everything that follows runs in batches behind that marker, as an Event's deletion runs behind its tombstone: an email account has no holdings cap, so each Event is cancelled as its own Event-wide operation and the rest is paged. The operation is two-phase (ADR 0017): a preview lists the impact, and a fresh email code confirms it (an anonymous identity confirms with its session); there is no grace period, since a cancelled Event cannot be revived anyway. The core keys accounts by user id (ADR 0021), so the same address can sign up again as a new user.

**What stays.** A non-personal `players` row, created at a person's first Entry and written on it, survives deletion as the competitor key Rankings and Player pages aggregate on; names in Entries, Participants, brackets, results, and notices stay as they were at deletion (a person may rename themselves before deleting, which is their choice, and the history of former names goes with the account; names were never copied into notices, which resolve them through the Entry at read). Every Event content stays under its frozen Visibility — description, area, exact address, online link, Announcements and their inbox copies — because a living Organizer's past Events keep them too, nobody new gains sight of anything, and a cancellation or a VOD link is part of the record; the preview offers to clear content first, and a content-based request after deletion is for the operator to redact.

**The Registrant side.** Active Entries in Events not yet over end as ordinary cancellations through the one Entry-state module (the seat moves on, derived walkovers follow, the Organizer is told); every other Entry of theirs — in an Event that is over, in a completed tournament, or already ended — loses its holder and keeps everything else; the claim Grants of that person's Entries are revoked, since the old link must not let a stranger take over the record. Settle skips an Entry whose holder row is missing, so an Offer cannot confirm a deleted person.

**The Organizer side.** Deletion removes only the Organizer's capabilities — the host attribution becomes "the host's account was deleted", the Discord webhook is deleted after the cancellation notices went out, and their Grants are revoked. Drafts are deleted. An Event that is over is kept whole, results or not: attendance is a record even without a competition. An Event not yet over is cancelled by the system with the notices ADR 0017 prescribes; if any result was recorded it is kept forever, otherwise it is removed on ADR 0017's existing terms. A standalone tournament with a recorded result is kept (a live one stays as it stands, which the preview says); one without is deleted (ADR 0011). No "archived" state is added: a finished Event without an Organizer is just that.

**Sanctions survive.** Deleting the email would turn a ban into a two-minute reset, easier than finding a new address — and reachable-only became the default precisely so that bans bite (ADR 0013). So, for people under an Organizer ban or an operator suspension at the moment of deletion and for nobody else, the existing ban or suspension row goes dormant: its user id is cleared and it carries a tag, `HMAC-SHA256(server secret, normalized email)`, the key's version, and an expiry twelve months from deletion. When any email is verified, its tag is looked up and the dormant rows re-attach to the new user. A dormant row keeps the Organizer-scoped participant number the person had, and numbers follow the merge's rule (ADR 0021): a number the verifying identity already holds with that Organizer wins, and the dormant row's number stays on the ban as a former number the Organizer recognises. Per-Track blocks are not carried. The key lives only in a deployment environment variable, versioned so rotation keeps the old rows until they expire; losing it is a general amnesty. An Organizer's own deletion ends their bans (they have no future Events), not the operator's suspensions. A request to erase a tag is the operator's call: a suspension's tag stays for other users' safety, an Organizer-only tag is erased after the person is identified. The tag is pseudonymous data, which the privacy notice and the ban notice itself state.

## Considered Options

- **Delete everything the person touched.** Breaks other people's brackets and results (ADR 0005).
- **Refuse deletion while anyone relies on the person, or deactivate without erasing.** A completed bracket relies on its names forever, so the first can never delete and the second never erases.
- **Pseudonymize names on deletion** (the first proposal). Withdrawn: see above.
- **Strip the exact address, the online link, Announcements and the description from a deleted Organizer's Events** (the second proposal). Withdrawn by the owner: the address is where the tournament was held, Announcements are what people read when they look back, and neither is seen by anyone who could not already see it.
- **Show both the name used at the time and the current name in past brackets**, or freeze names in completed records against renames. Withdrawn: the stalker case it served is an edge case a stable Player page URL defeats anyway, and offensive renames are a ban matter; the account-rename rule of 2026-09-30 stands, and the Player page gets a switch to hide itself in its own feature line.
- **A grace period of 14–30 days, a transfer to a co-organizer, data export, detection of a cleared browser.** No revival exists to make the grace period worth it; co-organizers do not exist yet; the rest adds nothing the preview does not.
- **Keeping nothing for sanctions; refusing deletion while banned; banning the `players` row; keeping the plain email of banned people; a shorter retention.** The first makes deletion the ban reset; the second holds the right to erasure hostage to a third party's ban; a new sign-up is a new `players` row; the plain email exposes more for the same effect; and a ban has no natural end, so six or twelve months is a policy number either way.
- **Refusing to merge, or ending the ban on deletion of the Organizer.** The first was adopted for suspended identities only; the second is what "Organizer's deletion ends their bans" is.

## Consequences

- The privacy notice, linked from sign-in and registration, states what is kept (verified email, display name, former names), that names in records persist after deletion, and that a tag of the email is kept for twelve months only for people under a sanction; the ban notice says the same. A professional legal review of the legitimate-interest basis for both is due before the URL is shared, since the owner's reading (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f), 17(3), 21; APPI Art. 35; the ICO's suppression-list precedent) is not legal advice.
- A lost anonymous identity (a cleared browser) is not a deletion: it is reaped after long inactivity, and the reaping only detaches that identity's Entries in other people's Events, never cancels them, since the person may still turn up.
- ADR 0014's exception for display data copied into notices covers subject data (an Event's name after its deletion) and never a person's name, which resolves through the Entry — the Entry outlives the account, so no copy is needed.
- The operator workflow (suspensions, redaction, tag erasure, cancelling an Event whose Organizer lost their account) is part of the anti-spam grilling.
