# Deleting an account removes the person at once and keeps the records; sanctions survive as dormant rows keyed by a tag of the email

Amended by [ADR 0023](./0023-communities-own-events-and-roles.md) and [ADR 0024](./0024-organizer-side-requires-an-account.md) (2026-10-14): "the Organizer side" below applies when the deleted account was the sole member of a Community — otherwise the Community keeps its Events and the ownership passes to the designated or longest-standing member; Grants belong to the Event and are revoked only when the Community ends; bans are the Community's, so a member's deletion ends none; and the anonymous-Organizer paths no longer arise, since Organizers are accounts. Slice numbers follow ADR 0024's re-sequencing. Amended by [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) and [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) (2026-10-15): a deleted person's active Entrant rows in Events not over, unless their Track's tournament has completed, end as left (the team's Entry is cancelled only when they were its last playing Entrant, a forming Entry only when they were its last Entrant; pending and declined rows, not records, are deleted), rows in Events over or in a completed tournament lose their identity and stay, as the body splits them, no longer offered by their Entry's claim link (revoked only when no claimable typed-name row remains), and either way a deleted captain's flag passes by ADR 0029's succession rule; Squad memberships are kept as left rows, and the deletion of a Community's sole member's account ends the links to its child Communities, which stand as roots (ADR 0028, revised 2026-10-22); a `players` row is created at a person's first Entry or Squad membership, whichever comes first, written on their Entrant or Squad membership row, and never deleted — a merge keeps the merged identity's row as an alias of the account's, since stored Revisions hold Player ids. Codes are derived (ADR 0028), so a dormant sanction row stores the code the person had, and nothing is reconciled at re-attachment. Reaping an abandoned anonymous identity keeps this ADR's rule: the identity is removed from its active Entrant rows (on one-player Tracks, since team Tracks require accounts from 2026-10-22), which stay as typed-name rows the Organizer can act on, and no Entry is cancelled (ADR 0029 follows it), the captain flag staying on the row; deletion, unlike reaping, ends the rows as left and deletes the person's Follows (ADR 0028). "Block" below reads as a Track-scope sanction, and an Entry's "holder" as its Entrants (ADR 0029). Revised 2026-10-22 (the owner's call): the deletion preview may hand the person's competition history to another account of theirs (below), built last of all.

Slice 1 is the first to store a person's own contact data — a verified email address, beside a display name and the former names an Organizer sees (ADR 0013); the names Organizers type for others are personal data already, and the privacy notice covers them too — so how a person deletes it had to be decided before the owner shares the URL (`docs/specs/events.md`). The grilling of 2026-10-13/14 settled it on one principle: **the account belongs to the person; the records belong to the Event.** Deletion removes everything that exists only for the person and cuts the pointers from records to them, and edits no record. The one future an Organizer, a Registrant, and a Ranking all rely on — a bracket that still names who won — is the one deletion must not touch, which is why the first proposal, pseudonymizing names on deletion, was withdrawn by the owner: a Ranking whose first place turns into "deleted player" is a broken record, and a name in a published bracket is an official record of the competition, as it is for FIDE, athletics federations, and quiz leagues. Deletion is also never wider than what the person could already have done themselves: cancel their own Entries, cancel their own Events.

**What is removed, in the first transaction.** The `users` row (display name, former-name history, settings), the verified email, and the account's MCP tokens; sessions die with them at the app layer (`resolveSession` answers a missing user as signed out, since the core keeps rotating tokens for thirty days), and a missing `users` row is the deletion marker every later write or delivery to that person checks. The batches also delete what exists only for the person: in-app deliveries addressed to them (under the account's id and under its aliases', which the merge left in place), code rows, rate-limit rows, stored previews, and participant-number rows; the alias rows go in the last batch, once nothing is keyed by them, and until then an alias whose account row is missing reads as deleted. Everything that follows runs in batches behind that marker, as an Event's deletion runs behind its tombstone: an email account has no holdings cap, so each Event is cancelled as its own Event-wide operation and the rest is paged. The operation is two-phase (ADR 0017): a preview lists the impact, and a fresh email code confirms it (an anonymous identity confirms with its session); there is no grace period, since a cancelled Event cannot be revived anyway, but a deletion is refused while an email change's revert window is open — seven days from the first change of a chain — so the revert link (ADR 0021) cannot be outrun. The core keys accounts by user id (ADR 0021), so the same address can sign up again as a new user.

**What stays.** A `players` row holding no contact data, created at a person's first Entry and written on it, survives deletion as the competitor key Rankings and Player pages aggregate on; names in Entries, Participants, brackets, results, and notices stay as they were at deletion (a person may rename themselves before deleting, which is their choice — a rename to a pseudonym is the privacy move deletion exists for, which is why no rename is ever reverted on deletion; a rename to an offensive name is the Organizer's to fix, since their rename and lock work on an Entry whose holder is gone; the history of former names goes with the account; names were never copied into notices, which resolve them through the Entry at read). The Player page, a profile that aggregates one person's results, is hidden once the account is deleted unless the person chose in the preview to keep it visible; the `players` row stays as the Rankings key either way. Every Event content stays under its frozen Visibility — description, area, exact address, online link, Announcements and their inbox copies — because a living Organizer's past Events keep them too, nobody new gains sight of anything, and a cancellation or a VOD link is part of the record; the preview offers to clear content first, and a content-based request after deletion is for the operator to redact.

**Handing the history to another account of the person's** (the owner's call, 2026-10-22; built last of all, since the case is rare — the slot after the Player page and Rankings feature lines is Claude's). One email is one person (ADR 0021) and two email accounts are never merged in place; the one place they meet is here. In the deletion preview the person may choose "hand my history to another account of mine": they type the other address, a code bound to that purpose goes to it (it signs nobody in, and the request answers the same for every address, as a sign-in request does), and the confirmation screen lists what moves — every tournament the deleted account's Player is credited in, its Squad memberships as left rows, its "as a member of" rows — and what that means: it all shows on the receiving account's Player page, a Ranking that counted both shows one row with the public reason "account merge", other Communities can tell the two were one person, the receiver can detach it, one email stays one person, and the person may rename before handing over. The deletion then proceeds exactly as above — memberships end, ownership passes, active Entrant rows end, the `users` row goes — and only one mark is written: the deleted account's `players` row becomes an alias of the receiving account's Player, with the instant, so reads resolve it as they resolve a merge's alias (ADR 0030); no record is edited, and Entrant and Squad-membership rows carry a Player index so the resolution is one lookup. The receiver is told and may detach once, from their settings; the deleted address receives a link, valid 30 days, that detaches as well; a detach removes the mark, the history returns to the deleted Player (its page hidden, as a deleted account's is), and the Seasons that counted either recompute. The hand-over is refused when the deleting account is under a ban or an operator suspension, when the receiving account is suspended, mid-deletion, or inside an email change's revert window (the last two Claude's details), and when the two share a tournament — both credited in a completed one, or both active in one not over — since one person cannot have played twice; afterwards a name link or claim that would credit the aliased Player in a tournament where the receiver is already credited is refused, and any overlap that remains counts once, at the better placement, matches between the two not counted (the merge rule of `docs/specs/events.md`). The deleted side's Ranking picks and page settings go with it; its refusals of name links bind through the alias; its pending acceptances lapse; and the aliased Player counts as the original for "takes no new link" (ADR 0026). Choosing the hand-over removes the "keep the Player page visible" choice, since the history has a page. Stored Revisions keep their keys: an old Revision shows the alias row under the name the record carries, marked "merged on <date>"; copies (seeds, prior standing) use the better row; and the Seasons that counted either, or read the receiver's prior standing, recompute as scheduled internal work. The overlap check is one range per Player and Track, up to about 10,000 rows in one transaction, else paged before the preview and re-checked at commit on new rows and live tournaments only; the receiver's state is checked before the confirmation screen and again at commit.

**The Registrant side.** Active Entries in Events not yet over end as ordinary cancellations through the one Entry-state module (the seat moves on, derived walkovers follow, the Organizer is told); every other Entry of theirs — in an Event that is over, in a completed tournament, or already ended — loses its holder, is marked holder-deleted so that no new claim Grant can be issued for it, and keeps everything else; the claim Grants of that person's Entries are revoked, since neither the old link nor a new one must let a stranger take over the record. Settle skips an Entry whose holder row is missing, so an Offer cannot confirm a deleted person.

**The Organizer side.** Deletion removes only the Organizer's capabilities — the Organizer attribution becomes "the Organizer's account was deleted", the Discord webhook is deleted after the cancellation notices went out, and their Grants are revoked. Drafts are deleted. An Event that is over is kept whole, results or not: attendance is a record even without a competition. An Event not yet over is cancelled by the system with the notices ADR 0017 prescribes, in their own wording; if any result was recorded it is kept forever, otherwise the cancellation writes a removal instant (the later of its end and seven days after cancelling) that an internal function acts on, since no Organizer remains to delete it. Events already over at deletion are the ones kept whole. A standalone tournament with a recorded result is kept (a live one stays as it stands, which the preview says); one without is deleted (ADR 0011). No "archived" state is added: a finished Event without an Organizer is just that.

**Sanctions survive.** Deleting the email would turn a ban into a two-minute reset, easier than finding a new address — and reachable-only became the default precisely so that bans bite (ADR 0013). So, for email accounts under an Organizer ban (or an operator suspension, ADR 0027) at the moment of deletion and for nobody else — an anonymous identity's ban ends with it — the existing row goes dormant: its user id is cleared and it carries a tag, `HMAC-SHA256(server secret, canonical email)` (lowercased, NFC-normalized, `+suffix` stripped, Gmail dots removed), the key's version, and an expiry twelve months from deletion; an email change made under a sanction tags the old address too. When any email is verified, its tag is looked up and the dormant rows re-attach to the new user, who is told and given a route to the operator. A dormant row keeps the Organizer-scoped participant number the person had, and numbers follow the merge's rule (ADR 0021): a number the verifying identity already holds with that Organizer wins, and the dormant row's number stays on the ban as a former number the Organizer recognises. Per-Track blocks are not carried: a block is one Organizer's judgment about one Track, escalated to a ban when it should follow the person. The key lives in a deployment environment variable with a copy outside the deployment, versioned so rotation keeps the old rows until they expire; the deploy never generates it once dormant rows exist, so a lost key is noticed rather than a silent amnesty, and a leaked version's rows are dropped. An Organizer's own deletion ends their bans (they have no future Events), not the operator's suspensions. A request to erase a tag is the operator's call: a suspension's tag stays for other users' safety, an Organizer-only tag is erased after the person is identified — outside the sign-in flow, since verifying in cobracket would re-attach the rows. The tag is pseudonymous data, which the privacy notice and the ban notice itself state.

## Considered Options

- **Delete everything the person touched.** Breaks other people's brackets and results (ADR 0005).
- **Refuse deletion while anyone relies on the person, or deactivate without erasing.** A completed bracket relies on its names forever, so the first can never delete and the second never erases.
- **Pseudonymize names on deletion** (the first proposal). Withdrawn: see above.
- **Strip the exact address, the online link, Announcements and the description from a deleted Organizer's Events** (the second proposal). Withdrawn by the owner: the address is where the tournament was held, Announcements are what people read when they look back, and neither is seen by anyone who could not already see it.
- **Show both the name used at the time and the current name in past brackets**, or freeze names in completed records against renames. Withdrawn: the stalker case it served is an edge case a stable Player page URL defeats anyway, and offensive renames are a ban matter; the account-rename rule of 2026-09-30 stands, and the Player page gets a switch to hide itself in its own feature line.
- **A grace period of 14–30 days, a transfer to a co-organizer, data export, detection of a cleared browser.** No revival exists to make the grace period worth it; co-organizers do not exist yet; the rest adds nothing the preview does not.
- **Reverting renames made in the days before deletion, and keeping a tag for every account that ever published an Event (for abuse reports that arrive after deletion).** The first re-exposes the real name of a person who renamed to a pseudonym before deleting; the second retains data on nearly every Organizer for a purpose that rarely arises — a report pending at deletion is the narrow hold to consider, in the anti-spam grilling.
- **Keeping nothing for sanctions; refusing deletion while banned; banning the `players` row; keeping the plain email of banned people; a shorter retention.** The first makes deletion the ban reset; the second holds the right to erasure hostage to a third party's ban; a new sign-up is a new `players` row; the plain email exposes more for the same effect; and a ban has no natural end, so six or twelve months is a policy number either way.
- **Refusing to merge, or ending the ban on deletion of the Organizer.** The first was adopted for suspended identities only; the second is what "Organizer's deletion ends their bans" is.
- **Two email accounts merged in place, or no path at all between them** (the in-place merge the audit's alternative, put on 2026-10-17 and refined on 2026-10-18; no path the document's form until then). The in-place merge folds two Organizer sides for a case the owner judges rare; no path leaves a person who registered under two addresses with two Player pages forever. The hand-over above is one mark on one row; the owner chose it on 2026-10-22, to be built last of all.
- **An operator-side detach of a handed-over history.** Not built (2026-10-18): the receiver can detach it themselves, and collecting two people's history under one account is met by the Organizer's exclusion and the operator's suspension.

## Consequences

- The privacy notice, linked from sign-in and registration, states what is kept (verified email, display name, former names), that names in records persist after deletion, that at deletion the person may hand their competition history to another account of theirs — after which other Communities can tell the two were one person —, and that a tag of the email is kept for twelve months only for people under a sanction; the ban notice says the same. A professional legal review of the legitimate-interest basis for both is due before the URL is shared, since the owner's reading (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f), 17(3), 21; APPI Art. 35; the ICO's suppression-list precedent) is not legal advice.
- A lost anonymous identity (a cleared browser) is not a deletion: it is reaped once its session has expired, thirty days after its last use ([ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md)), and the reaping only detaches that identity's Entries in other people's Events, never cancels them, since the person may still turn up.
- A request about a name in records after deletion is the operator's to redact, through the `players` row across every record of that person; the legal review also covers the processors and their countries, APPI Art. 32's publication duties, retention in logs and backups, minors, the Organizer's role as controller of names they type, a contact and deadline for data-subject requests, a retention criterion for Events without an Organizer, and the lawful basis for the hand-over of a deleted account's history, its withdrawal by the detach, and the publisher's role on the rows it links (`docs/specs/events.md`, pending questions).
- ADR 0014's exception for display data copied into notices covers subject data (an Event's name after its deletion) and never a person's name, which resolves through the Entry — the Entry outlives the account, so no copy is needed.
- The operator workflow is [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md)'s: suspending a person or an Event, redaction as a pseudonym fixed per Player, and reports, which keep no tag of a deleted person; content the person cleared before deleting stays with the operator for 30 days, as every version others saw does, since versions belong to the Event, standalone tournament, Community, or Ranking (2026-10-17). Cancelling an Event whose Organizer lost their account needs no operator: a Community loses its last member only by that member's deletion, which already cancels its Events. Tag erasure stays as the events spec describes it.
