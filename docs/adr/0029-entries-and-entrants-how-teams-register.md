# An Entry takes a seat and Entrants stand on it: solo play is an Entry with one Entrant, teams form before they register, and a team's lineup is part of the published result

Every rule about people on the registration side — gates, notices, cancellation, claims, blocks, bans, codes, Players, deletion — was written on the Entry, and every rule about seats and states — Capacity, Waitlist, Offer, confirmed and ended states, Participant, publication, substitution — on the same Entry, because the unit of registration was always one person. Teams split the two. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 (rounds 11–15, 18 and 20 of its record) tried per-person Entries bundled into a team and a team Entry with members as data, and settled on the split itself: an **Entry** is the unit that takes a seat, an **Entrant** is a person on it, and a one-player Track is an Entry with exactly one Entrant, so the code has one path. [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) decides what a persistent team is; this decision is how a team — a Squad's, or one formed for the day — enters a Track.

**Entry and Entrant.** An Entry belongs to a Track and carries the seat-side facts: state (`forming` while it has no seat; then `confirmed`, `waitlisted`, or `offered`; ended as `cancelled`, `removed`, or `lapsed`), its team name (the Community's and Squad's name by default for a Squad-linked Entry, the Entrants' names joined for an ad-hoc one; at most 40 characters; the Organizer's rename locks the Entry, as it locks a person's Entry today), an optional link to a Squad, its Participant once it is on a Roster, a denormalized count of active playing Entrants, and whether it is listed as recruiting. An Entrant belongs to an Entry and carries the person-side facts: an identity (an account, an anonymous identity, or a typed name with none), an optional Player (set at once for an account, at claim for a typed name — no ghost Players from typos), a display name under the existing name rules, a `playing` flag, a `captain` flag, a label copied from the Squad membership (ADR 0028; editable by whoever may rename, reaching records at once like a name), the Grant it came through, a former code where a merge changed it, and a state: `invited` or `requested` before consent, `active`, then `declined`, `left` (with a pointer to the Entry the person moved to, when they moved), or `removed`. Entrant rows are never deleted while they are records; `invited`, `requested`, and `declined` rows are not records and go with the account. The Track declares the number of playing Entrants an Entry needs (a minimum, and an optional maximum, the platform cap being 32 rows per Entry); a solo Track is 1/1 and shows none of this. There is no mixed Track: a Track is for Entries of one size range, and a person who wants to play alone on a team Track is an Entry of one, forming until it finds teammates. The maximum bounds playing Entrants; non-playing Entrants (coach, manager) exist only on Squad-linked Entries and count against the row cap alone.

**Three invariants, one place each.** (1) A person is an active playing Entrant on at most one Entry per Track, forming Entries included — checked by every act that activates a person (opening a join link, accepting an invitation, a request being accepted, claiming, turning `playing` on, an Organizer's lineup change), and "leave A and join B" is offered as one step. (2) A person has at most one unended Entrant row per Entry; a `declined` row blocks only re-invitation from that Entry. (3) An Entry needs someone who can act for it: an ad-hoc Entry has exactly one captain while any account Entrant is active (the captain must be a playing Entrant; the flag passes to the longest-standing active account Entrant, else to an anonymous one on an anonymous-allowed Track); a Squad-linked Entry has no captain — the Squad's staff act for it (ADR 0028's capability). An Entry nobody can act for ends while it is waiting and stays while confirmed, the Organizer told. An Entry whose last active playing Entrant leaves ends as cancelled (on a solo Track "leave" is "cancel"); when the Organizer removes the last one, the preview asks whether to remove the team.

**Teams form, then register.** An Entry starts `forming`: no seat, any number of Entrants, outside the seat equation, the Grant use counts, the Roster limit, and the Waitlist. Registering is always an explicit act of the captain or the staff, allowed only once the Entry has at least the minimum of playing Entrants, and it runs the existing decision function once (confirmed, waitlisted, refused with the reason) — idempotent, and the first team to press "register" takes the last seat. The Waitlist asks ADR 0015's test of those who act for the Entry: it waits while its captain is reachable or it is Squad-linked (the staff always are) — an anonymous captain, or one without a channel, takes a free seat or is told to add an email or pass the captain flag to a reachable teammate (the captain hands the flag to any active account Entrant; a suspension moves it to the longest-standing one) — and an Offer is accepted or declined with the captain's or the staff's session, a non-captain Entrant only leaving. The minimum is the one entry gate asked again at the Waitlist: a waiting Entry that fell below it (an Entrant left, or a sanction ended their row) keeps its place but is passed over when a seat frees, and an Offer it holds can be taken only once it is back at the minimum, so a team below the minimum is never confirmed by the queue — not by settle's walk when a seat frees, and not by the fill at the close — while the Organizer's by-hand confirmation may still seat it, warned as short-handed; its captain is told to add a player to keep the place, and crossing the minimum in either direction is a seat input that schedules settle, so a team that restocks while a seat is already free is confirmed at once. When the count reaches the minimum the captain is told "you can register", and an acceptance that reaches it offers "accept and register" in one tap. Entries still forming when the tournament goes live (or the Event ends, on a Track without one) end as lapsed, "no team formed". A Track may list its forming Entries as recruiting (an Organizer switch, off where the maximum is 1): the list shows team names and counts to the Organizer and to viewers who hold an active Entry on that Track, never on the registrant list; it is sendou.ink's "looking for group" without a second mechanism. An Organizer's "make teams from the forming list" moves whole Entries, names them Team 1…N, and makes the longest-standing account Entrant captain. An Organizer's own entries (pasted names: one team per line, the first name the captain's row) skip the gates as they do today, short of the minimum included; the Organizer adds people by name or from the recruiting list, never by code.

**Short-handed is a fact about a seated Entry, not a state.** An Entry that took a seat and then fell below the minimum is "short-handed" in the UI; the format engine has one predicate, _absent_ = the Entry has ended or its playing Entrants are below the minimum, with ADR 0016's derived walkover behind it: nothing is recorded until a result depending on it is, the opponent is not told automatically (counts move), the Organizer is warned, and an automatic publication includes short-handed Entries and lists them in its preview. A swap is one act — A out, B in — so no instant of short-handedness is created when B's consent already exists; otherwise it is a removal and an invitation with a short-handed interval between.

**Consent is the Entrant's own act, or standing consent given on joining a Squad.** A person becomes `active` only by opening a join link, accepting an invitation, having their request accepted, claiming a typed name — or, for a Squad member, by the standing consent they gave when they joined the Squad ("the staff may enter you in tournaments; you are told every time and may leave any time"; a Squad larger than the Track's maximum gets a "choose up to N" step, and "last time's team" the same). A person who left an Entry returns to it only by their own acceptance; leaving the Squad ends the standing consent. Promotion of an ad-hoc team into a Squad is an invitation to each account Entrant, never an automatic add. The Track's registration requirement applies to every Entrant and is a ladder of one setting: _anonymous allowed_ (anonymous Entrants, and a captain may type a name with a claim link so a four-player team is not stranded at 3 on the day) ⊂ _reachable only_ (the default: every Entrant a reachable account; a typed name only by the Organizer, whose entries are exempt as today) ⊂ _Squad-linked teams only_ (every Entry links a Squad, which already means accounts). A Squad-linked Entry's Entrants come from the Squad; staff may add nobody else — the Organizer may.

**A join link is a register Grant scoped to one Entry.** The scope ladder is Event > Track > Entry. An Entry-scope Grant is issued by the captain (an account, or an anonymous captain on an anonymous-allowed Track — narrower than a public QR code) or by the staff; one unended per Entry (re-issuing replaces); it stores no end and ends with the Entry or the swap deadline; it admits a person through the activation function (the per-person gates, the maximum, the deadline), not the registration decision, so it works on a Track whose tournament is live; it admits while playing Entrants are below the maximum; a removal does not free its use (re-issue to admit another); it appears in the Organizer's Grant list and can be revoked there; it cannot be issued on an invite-only or hidden Track or in a private Event (there the Organizer's claim link, the Squad's standing consent, and invitations to recruiting Entries remain). An invitation is an Entrant row in state `invited`, a request one in state `requested` created by the person — the same row from the other side. Invitations go to a closed set (members of recruiting Entries on that Track, last time's Entrants, the ad-hoc team's own Entrants at promotion), carry fixed wording with the Event's and Track's names (the team name on opening, with a report button), need an account to send (pushing a notice at someone is an account's act), count 50 a day per account, and are withdrawn when the Entry reaches its maximum. An invitation gives view of the Entry and Track as a Grant does. A claim is the existing claim Grant scoped to an Entry — "which one are you?" — and claiming a captain's row grants the captain's powers; a wrong claim is reset at the Entrant (a captain's: the name restored, links issued since revoked, removals since listed). Restoration (ADR 0016) re-enters the Entrants as typed-name copies with claim links.

**The swap deadline is the Organizer's, with three settings and no new instant.** Lineups change freely until the deadline: lock at the bracket's publication; lock at this team's first match result, a recorded walkover included (the default); never lock — under which, once the Track has its first result, a person who was on any Entry of the Track cannot join another (the ringer rule; the Organizer may override, and such a change is recorded on the bracket's activity). Entrants may always leave; the Organizer may always change a lineup ("the Organizer's lineup change", distinct from Substitution, which re-points a Participant to another Entry — the way a no-show team is replaced by the next waiting one). After the deadline the Organizer adds people by typed name or from the recruiting list only.

**One sanction row replaces the block and the ban.** A sanction has a person, a scope (a Track, or a Community and so its descendants), the Track or Community, the Entrant row it was made from, and an instant; the gate is one lookup per scope kind. A Track-scope sanction ends the person's active and invited rows on that Track and their view (the team may become short-handed); a typed-name Entrant has no person and so cannot be sanctioned, only removed. The person is told the true reason when they act themselves (joining, accepting); a captain or staff adding someone is told only "X cannot be included" — the one wording for a ban, a sanction, suspension, "already active on this Track", and "already on another Squad's Entry here" (an Entry in the same tree may be named) — because sanctions are never disclosed to third parties; a Community ban on the team itself is said plainly, since the staff are the party. The removal preview offers sanctions on each account Entrant and says typed names cannot be sanctioned. A Community ban is evaded by the same five people entering ad hoc, which the preview says; the person ban is what follows people. The operator's redaction (ADR 0027) covers team names.

**A team's lineup is part of the published result.** A one-player bracket shows the person's name; a team bracket shows the team's name and the registrant list shows the Entrants under ADR 0019's audience setting — which would let a registrant-list setting of "organizers only" hide, after the fact, who played, while Rankings read lineups (ADR 0030) and Player pages credit them. The owner decided (2026-10-15) that the playing lineup of a completed tournament is part of its published result: who played is a public fact of a public tournament, as the name in a one-player bracket is, and the registrant-list audience governs the view before and during the Event only. Registration says so ("your name is visible to everyone who can see this tournament's results"), and a Player hides their name by hiding their Player page, as everywhere. Concretely, a match's lineup is the active playing Entrants of each side at the instant its result is recorded, and a team's placement is credited to every person who was on the lineup of at least one of its matches (not "active at completion", which drops a semi-finalist who left before the final); a bench player who never played still shares the placement — a known imprecision that per-match lineups, if ever recorded, refine without changing the definition; the Organizer's maximum is the cap on it. The Participant stores those credited Player ids when the tournament completes.

**The rest, by rule.** Visibility: the bracket shows team names; the registrant list shows team and active Entrants; forming Entries' Entrants are in the Registrants audience, not the confirmed one; Waitlist names stay hidden. Notices for an Entry reach the captain and the account Entrants (the staff, folded per Entry per day), paged by Entrant count; an Entrant's own changes reach the person and the captain. A team's placement appears on each credited Entrant's Player page "as a member of <team>". "Last time's team" is offered to the previous captain or staff: Squad-linked → active rows for those still on the Squad; ad-hoc → invitations; typed names listed for re-entry. Deletion of an account: unended Entrant rows in Events not over end as `left` (the Entry cancelled if it was the last playing one); rows in Events over lose their identity and stay; reaping does the same; a suspension moves the captain flag. A merge ends the duplicate Entrant row where one person held two on a Track (the other team told). Short-handed Entries and the "no team formed" notice are the only new wording; "withdrawn" keeps its meaning. Default team names, rename limits (7 days), and the 120-character person name are unchanged.

## Considered Options

- **One Entry per person, bundled into a team record** (the grilling's first proposal). Every seat operation would move N rows (a Waitlist of teams, Offers to teams), and the team record would still need a name, a state, and a Participant — the Entry in all but name.
- **A team Entry whose members are data (an array of Player ids).** Loses the per-person gates, notices, claims, sanctions, and Players; a second code path beside the person Entry.
- **A holder (the person who created the Entry) as the team's owner.** An uncancellable account deletion would cancel the whole team's seat; a departed holder could cancel out of spite; a banned person could field a team as a non-playing holder. The captain flag on a playing Entrant, and the staff for Squad-linked Entries, dissolve all three.
- **Registering automatically when the minimum is reached.** It registers into a closed window when a Squad fills before it opens, lets a stranger's joining force the seat, and races the captain's own press; a notice at the minimum does the job.
- **Seating short-handed Entries** ("3 of 4, incomplete"). A one-person Entry would hold a seat; the minimum stops meaning anything.
- **A separate "waiting for a team" pool and a "mixed" Track.** Both are the forming Entry with one Entrant and the recruiting switch.
- **Substitutes as a concept, per-match lineups now.** The minimum–maximum range is the substitute; per-match records are an additive refinement the crediting rule already anticipates.
- **Flat teams in which every member can act** (one person could expel everyone), **ad-hoc teams as real Communities** (garbage against the 10-Community limit), **replacing the swap deadline with the registration close** (day-of substitutions would depend on the Organizer), **no deadline** (a Squad could add a professional who never registered).
- **Non-playing captains on ad-hoc teams.** One anonymous identity could field name-typed teams until the Track was full; requiring the captain to play makes the one-active-Entrant invariant stop it.
- **Reading lineups only where the registrant list is public** (the alternative to the lineup decision above). Team Rankings would silently skip most tournaments, players would not be credited, and the Organizer setting would be doing a job the Player-page switch already does.

## Consequences

- Schema: `entries` (Track, state with `forming` as its own value, team name and lock, optional Squad, Participant, `activePlayingCount`, listed flag, outcome notices as today); `entrants` (Entry, Track denormalized, user or anonymous identity or none, Player, name, `playing`, `captain`, label, state, moved-to pointer, Grant, former code; indexes by Entry and state, by Track, user, and state, by Track and state, by Grant and state); `sanctions` (person, scope kind, Track or Community, source Entrant, instant; indexes by user and Community, by Track and user, by email tag); Grants gain the Entry scope; Participants gain `creditedPlayerIds`; Tracks gain minimum, maximum, the recruiting switch, the swap-deadline setting, and the registration requirement as a three-step ladder. The Entry row is the pivot of optimistic concurrency; registration is idempotent; daily caps use the rate limiter; no operation scans a Track's Entrants unbounded.
- The settle function, the decision function, Capacity, the Waitlist, Offers, publication, substitution, restoration, and derived walkovers keep their rules with "Entry" meaning this Entry; the "one active Entry per user per Track" gate becomes invariant (1) above; the anonymous holdings cap counts Entry and Entrant rows together; the per-Event notice caps count Entrants.
- ADR 0013, 0015, 0016, 0018, and 0019 carry amendment notes reading Entry and Entrant in place of "an Entry and its holder" (who may wait and accept an Offer for a team, which name the Organizer's rename locks, the Entry scope of Grants and the Entrant a claim takes); ADR 0019's registrant-list audience is amended to govern the view before and during the Event, the completed lineup being public; ADR 0027 gains the join-link and invitation caps.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains Entrant and Lineup and rewrites Entry and Registrant; `docs/specs/events.md` gains the team sections, the lineup rule, and slices for Squads, team Entries, and the forming list.
- Rankings read `creditedPlayerIds` and the Entry chain ([ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md)).
