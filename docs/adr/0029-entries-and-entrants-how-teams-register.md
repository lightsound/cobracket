# An Entry takes a seat and Entrants stand on it: solo play is an Entry with one Entrant, teams form before they register, and a team's lineup is part of the published result

Every rule about people on the registration side — gates, notices, cancellation, claims, blocks, bans, codes, Players, deletion — was written on the Entry, and every rule about seats and states — Capacity, Waitlist, Offer, confirmed and ended states, Participant, publication, substitution — on the same Entry, because the unit of registration was always one person. Teams split the two. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 (rounds 11–15, 18 and 20 of its record) tried per-person Entries bundled into a team and a team Entry with members as data, and settled on the split itself: an **Entry** is the unit that takes a seat, an **Entrant** is a person on it, and a one-player Track is an Entry with exactly one Entrant, so the code has one path. [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) decides what a persistent team is; this decision is how a team — a Squad's, or one formed for the day — enters a Track.

**Entry and Entrant.** An Entry belongs to a Track and carries the seat-side facts: state (`forming` until it registers; then `confirmed`, `waitlisted`, or `offered`; ended as `cancelled`, `removed`, or `lapsed`), its team name (the Squad's display name of ADR 0028 by default for a Squad-linked Entry, the Entrants' names joined for an ad-hoc one; at most 40 characters; the Organizer's rename locks the Entry, as it locks a person's Entry today), an optional link to a Squad, an optional pointer to the Entry it was copied from (set when "last time's team" is used; team Rankings read it as a tie-breaker only, [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md)), its Participant once it is on a Roster, a denormalized count of active playing Entrants, and whether it is listed as recruiting. An Entrant belongs to an Entry and carries the person-side facts: an identity (an account, an anonymous identity, or a typed name with none), an optional Player (set at once for an identity, account or anonymous; at claim for a typed name — no ghost Players from typos), a display name under the existing name rules, a `playing` flag (on by default; the staff turn it off on a Squad-linked Entry), a `captain` flag, a label copied from the Squad membership (ADR 0028; editable by whoever may rename, reaching records at once like a name), the Grant it came through, a former code where a merge changed it, the instant of each state change and of each change of `playing`, and a state: `invited` or `requested` before consent, `active`, then `declined`, `left` (with a pointer to the Entry the person moved to, when they moved), or `removed`. Entrant rows are never deleted while they are records; `invited`, `requested`, and `declined` rows are not records, never appear on the Organizer's list, and go with the account; while pending, `invited` and `requested` rows count against the Entry's maximum apart from its active rows (an anonymous invitee's row also against that identity's holdings cap), and every invitation or request counts against its sender's daily limit when it is sent, whatever becomes of it. The Track declares the number of playing Entrants an Entry needs (a minimum, and an optional maximum — unset, it reads as the platform cap wherever it is a gate — the platform cap being 32 active rows per Entry); a solo Track is 1/1 and shows none of this. There is no mixed Track: a Track is for Entries of one size range, and a person who wants to play alone on a team Track is an Entry of one, forming until it finds teammates. The maximum bounds playing Entrants; non-playing Entrants (coach, manager) exist only on Squad-linked Entries and count against the row cap alone.

**Three invariants, one place each.** (1) A person is an active playing Entrant on at most one Entry per Track, forming Entries included — checked by every act that activates a person (forming an Entry, opening a join link, accepting an invitation, a request being accepted, the staff adding a Squad member, claiming, turning `playing` on, an Organizer's lineup change), and "leave A and join B" is offered as one step. (2) A person has at most one unended Entrant row per Entry; a `declined` row blocks only a repeat of what was declined — re-invitation from that Entry after an invitee declined, a new request to it after the captain declined one. (3) An Entry needs someone who can act for it: an ad-hoc Entry has exactly one captain while any Entrant the flag can pass to is active (the captain must be a playing Entrant; the flag passes to the longest-standing active account Entrant, else to an anonymous one on an anonymous-allowed Track, and never to a suspended person — no succession, hand-over, or "make teams" gives it to one, and a suspension moves it when anyone can take it, while a suspended captain whose flag no one can take keeps it and the Entry is left to its Organizer, ADR 0027); a Squad-linked Entry has no captain while its Squad link is live — the Squad's staff act for it (ADR 0028's capability), and the succession rule gives it one once the link is inert (its Community suspended or deleted). An Entry nobody can act for ends while it is waiting and stays while confirmed, the Organizer told, as long as its Event is not over and its Track's tournament has not completed; after either, an Entry is a record that needs no one to act for it, and nobody is told. An Entry that has registered and whose last active playing Entrant leaves ends as cancelled (a forming Entry ends as cancelled when its last Entrant leaves or is moved out by "make teams" — the moved Entrants are `left` with a pointer to their new Entry — and cannot be listed as recruiting while it has no playing Entrant) (on a solo Track "leave" is "cancel"); when the Organizer removes the last one, the preview asks whether to remove the team.

**Teams form, then register.** An Entry starts `forming`: no seat, any number of Entrants (playing ones up to the maximum, every row up to the 32-row cap, fewer than the minimum included), outside the seat equation, the Grant use counts, the Roster limit, the Waitlist, and the registration window (a forming Entry may be created and filled before the Track opens: forming passes the decision function's gates other than the window, the publication guard, Capacity, a Grant's use count, the Waitlist's, the minimum, and the Roster limit, so no Entry forms once the Track's tournament is live or completed), and not reliance that keeps an Event from returning to draft (ADR 0017). Registering is always an explicit act of the captain or the staff, allowed only once the Entry has at least the minimum of playing Entrants, and it runs the existing decision function once (confirmed, waitlisted, refused with the reason — a refused registration leaves the Entry forming, to register again once the reason is gone) — idempotent, and the first team to press "register" takes the last seat. The Waitlist asks ADR 0015's test of those who act for the Entry: it joins the Waitlist only while its captain is reachable or its Squad link is live (the staff always are; asked when it joins, so a waiting Entry whose captain flag later passes to an anonymous Entrant keeps its place, its Offer reaching that captain in-app) — an anonymous captain, or one without a channel, takes a free seat or is told to add an email or pass the captain flag to a reachable teammate (the captain hands the flag to any active account Entrant who is not suspended, a refusal giving no reason; a suspension moves it by the succession rule) — and an Offer is accepted or declined with the captain's or the staff's session, a non-captain Entrant only leaving. The minimum is the one entry gate asked again at the Waitlist: a waiting Entry that fell below it (an Entrant left, or a sanction ended their row) keeps its place but is passed over when a seat frees, and an Offer it holds can be taken only once it is back at the minimum, so a team below the minimum is never confirmed by the queue — not by settle's walk when a seat frees, and not by the fill at the close — while the Organizer's by-hand confirmation may still seat it, warned as short-handed; its captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry) is told to add a player to keep the place, and crossing the minimum in either direction is a seat input that schedules settle, so a team that restocks while a seat is already free is confirmed at once. An Entry whose suspended captain keeps the flag (below) is passed over the same way while the suspension lasts — no Offer, no automatic acceptance, and an Offer it holds returned by the suspension itself, its place kept, as an Event's suspension returns its Offers — and the suspension and its lift are seat inputs on each Track where the person captains a waiting Entry. When the count reaches the minimum while registration is open — or registration opens while the count is at the minimum or above — the captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry) is told "you can register" and the other Entrants see "waiting for the captain to register" ("waiting for the staff to register" on a Squad-linked Entry), and the captain's acceptance of a request that reaches it while registration is open offers "accept and register" in one tap (an invitee's acceptance registers nothing; before the opening the Entry shows the opening time instead). Lowering the minimum or the maximum is always allowed and registers nothing by itself (a forming Entry that reaches the minimum because it was lowered is told "you can register" under the same rule — only while registration is open); raising either is two-phase under ADR 0017, and the preview of any Track-setting change lists the forming Entries it affects. Entries still forming when the tournament goes live (or the Event ends, on a Track whose tournament never went live or that has none) end as lapsed, "no team formed". A Track may list its forming Entries as recruiting (an Organizer switch, off where the maximum is 1): the list shows each recruiting Entry's team name, its Entrants' display names (nothing else: no code, no reachability) and its count against the minimum to the Organizer and to viewers who hold an active Entry on that Track (anyone else sees only how many Entries are recruiting), never on the registrant list, which shows no forming Entry; a captain lists their Entry (never a Squad-linked one, whose Entrants come from its Squad, which is also why it has no join link), and listing it is consent to be placed on a team by the Organizer and to appear on that list, both of which the listing screen says; it is sendou.ink's "looking for group" without a second mechanism. An Organizer's "make teams from the recruiting list" makes the number of teams the Organizer asks for, named Team 1…N, and moves each recruiting Entry's Entrants together into one of them, at random or in an order the Organizer sets — an Organizer act, skipping the gates as the Organizer's own entries do (below) rather than going through the activation function, and like them never past the maximum or the 32-row cap (a recruiting Entry that fits no team stays on the list, and a team that receives none is not made); each team is an ad-hoc forming Entry (fewer than the minimum included) for its captain to register, the captain being the longest-standing account playing Entrant, else the first row placed, never a suspended person — a row "make teams" creates keeps the start of the row it came from, so standing on a made team, for its captain and for later succession alike, counts from joining the recruiting Entry. An Organizer's own entries (pasted names: one team per line, the team's name and then, after a delimiter, its Entrants' names, the first of them the captain's row) skip the gates as they do today, short of the minimum included; the Organizer adds people by name or from the recruiting list, never by code.

**Short-handed is a fact about a seated Entry, not a state.** A confirmed Entry below the minimum is "short-handed" in the UI; the format engine has one predicate, _absent_ = the Entry has ended or its playing Entrants are below the minimum, with ADR 0016's derived walkover behind it: nothing is recorded until a result depending on it is, the opponent is not told automatically (counts move), the pending match is not shown as a walkover while it is only derived — once a dependent result records it, the walkover is labelled "short-handed", and the UI word for _absent_ is "short-handed, cannot play" —, the Organizer is warned and the captain (the staff, for a Squad-linked Entry) is told that the Entry is short-handed, and an automatic publication includes short-handed Entries and lists them in its preview, and publishing by hand warns about them. A swap is one act — A out, B in — so no instant of short-handedness is created when B's consent already exists (a Squad's standing consent, or an invitation B accepted, which made B active already) or B is a name typed on an anonymous-allowed Track; otherwise it is a removal and an invitation with a short-handed interval between.

**Consent is the Entrant's own act, or standing consent given on joining a Squad or on listing an Entry as recruiting.** A person becomes `active` only by forming an Entry, opening a join link, accepting an invitation, having their request accepted, claiming a typed name — or, for a Squad member, by the standing consent they gave when they joined the Squad ("the staff may enter you in tournaments; you are told every time and may leave any time"; a Squad larger than the Track's maximum gets a "choose up to N" step, and "last time's team" the same) — or, for an Entrant of an Entry listed as recruiting, by the Organizer's placement, which the listing consents to. A person who left an Entry returns to it only by their own acceptance (on a Squad-linked Entry, of the staff's invitation: the standing consent does not re-add them); leaving the Squad ends the standing consent. Promotion of an ad-hoc team into a Squad is an invitation to each account Entrant — a single-use "join this Squad" Grant delivered as a notice (ADR 0028), each counting against the sender's 50 team invitations a day — never an automatic add. The Track's registration requirement applies to every Entrant and is a ladder of one setting: _anonymous allowed_ (anonymous Entrants, and a captain may type a name with a claim link so a four-player team is not stranded at 3 on the day) ⊃ _account required_ (the default: every Entrant is an account with a verified email, that is, reachable; a typed name only by the Organizer, whose entries are exempt as today) ⊃ _Squad-linked teams only_ (every Entry links a Squad, which already means accounts). A Squad-linked Entry's Entrants come from the Squad; staff may add nobody else — the Organizer may.

**A join link is a register Grant scoped to one Entry.** The scope ladder is Event > Track > Entry. An Entry-scope Grant is issued by the captain (an account, or an anonymous captain on an anonymous-allowed Track — narrower than a public QR code) — a Squad-linked Entry has none, since its Entrants come from its Squad and a guest joins the Squad first; one unended per Entry (re-issuing replaces), link-only — it is never given a code — and outside the Event's register-Grant cap (Event-wide Grant operations are paged); it stores no end and ends with the Entry, the swap deadline, or a change that makes its Track invite-only or hidden or its Event private (listed in that change's preview); it admits a person through the activation function (the per-person gates, the Track's registration requirement among them, the maximum, the deadline), not the registration decision, so it works on a Track whose tournament is live; it admits while playing Entrants are below the maximum and so are its uses — the rows it admitted that are still active or were removed, so a leaving Entrant frees a use and a removal does not (re-issue to admit another); it appears in the Organizer's Grant list and can be revoked there, and the Entry's captain may revoke it outright; it cannot be issued on an invite-only or hidden Track or in a private Event (there the Organizer's claim link, the Squad's standing consent, and invitations to recruiting Entries remain). An invitation is an Entrant row in state `invited`, a request one in state `requested` created by the person — the same row from the other side, which the captain accepts or declines one at a time (a Squad-linked Entry takes no requests: its Entrants come from its Squad); a request from someone active on another Entry of the Track is their "leave A and join B" — the request says so, and the captain's acceptance moves them — and a person's pending requests on a Track are withdrawn once they become active on an Entry other than the one a request names for leaving, so an acceptance never moves anyone out of an Entry they did not agree to leave. Invitations go to a closed set (Entrants of recruiting Entries on that Track, last time's Entrants, and a Squad member who left the Squad-linked Entry inviting them) and are sent by a captain (on a Squad-linked Entry, whose Entrants come from its Squad, only by the staff, and only to such a member), carry fixed wording with the Event's and Track's names (the team name on opening, with a report button), need an account to send (pushing a notice at someone is an account's act), and none at the recipient, who is reached in-app as an existing Entrant (by email where they have one), count 50 a day per account (a person's requests count there too), are capped while pending at the Entry's maximum (counted apart from active rows), and are withdrawn — pending requests too — when the Entry reaches its maximum. An invitation gives view of the Entry and Track as a Grant does. A claim is the existing claim Grant scoped to an Entry — "which one are you?" — and claiming a captain's row grants the captain's powers; a wrong claim is reset at the Entrant (a captain's: the name restored, links issued since revoked, removals since listed). Restoration (ADR 0016) re-enters the Entrants as typed-name copies with the Entry's claim link, keeping the Entry's Squad link, so a Squad-linked team's staff act for the re-entry.

**The swap deadline is the Organizer's, with three settings and no new instant.** Lineups change freely until the deadline: lock at the bracket's publication; lock at this team's first match result, a recorded walkover included (the default); never lock — under which, once the Track has its first result, any lineup change is notified to the Organizer and recorded on the bracket's activity. Under every setting, once the Track has its first result, a person who was on any Entry of the Track cannot join another (the ringer rule, which the Organizer may override), since under the default a player could otherwise leave a team that has played for one that has not. Turning `playing` on goes through the activation function as an addition does (the per-person gates, the maximum, the deadline) and counts as joining for the ringer rule. Until the deadline the captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry) may also remove an Entrant, whose row ends as `removed`, alone or as a swap's "A out". Entrants may always leave; the Organizer may always change a lineup ("the Organizer's lineup change", distinct from Substitution, which re-points a Participant to another Entry — the way a no-show team is replaced by the next waiting one). After the deadline the Organizer adds people by typed name or from the recruiting list only.

**One sanction row replaces the block and the ban.** A sanction has a scope (a Track, or a Community and so its descendants), the Track or Community, a target — a person, with the Entrant row it was made from when one exists (a Squad member banned from the ban preview has none), or, at Community scope only, a Community, with neither — and an instant; a person-target row is set by the Organizer on a Track or by the banning Community's owner or admin (from an Entrant row, the banning Community is the Event's own or, chosen in the preview, an ancestor of it where the actor holds one of those roles — never a Community outside the Event's tree), a Community-target row by that owner or admin from the ban preview or the Community page; the gate is one lookup per scope kind. A sanction can be lifted — a Track-scope one by any Organizer of the Event, a Community-scope one by the banning Community's owner or admin — which sets an `ended` instant on the row (rows are kept), reinstates nothing (ended Entrant rows stay ended), and is told to nobody. A Track-scope sanction ends the person's active and invited rows on that Track as `removed` and their view (the team may become short-handed; the person is told only that they were removed from the team); a typed-name Entrant has no person and so cannot be sanctioned, only removed. The person is told the true reason when they act themselves (joining, accepting), and the captain and staff are told nothing; a captain or staff adding someone — or pressing register for an Entry that holds such a person — is told only "X cannot be included" — the one wording for a ban, a sanction, suspension, "already active on this Track", and "already on another Squad's Entry here" (an Entry in the same tree may be named), checked once when the addition is submitted, never while names are typed, and chooses to go on without X or to stop, while X is told the true reason privately — because sanctions are never disclosed to third parties; a Community ban on the team itself is said plainly ("this team cannot register with this host"), since the staff are the party. The removal preview offers a sanction on each Entrant with an identity (an account or an anonymous one) and says typed names cannot be sanctioned. A Community ban is evaded by the same five people entering ad hoc, which the preview says; the person ban is what follows people. The operator's redaction (ADR 0027) covers team names.

**A team's lineup is part of the published result.** A one-player bracket shows the person's name; a team bracket shows the team's name and the registrant list shows the Entrants under ADR 0019's audience setting — which would let a registrant-list setting of "organizers only" hide, after the fact, who played, while Rankings read lineups (ADR 0030) and Player pages credit them. The owner decided (2026-10-15) that the playing lineup of a completed tournament is part of its published result: who played is a public fact of a public tournament, as the name in a one-player bracket is, and the registrant-list audience governs the registrant list, never the published result. Registration says so ("your name is visible to everyone who can see this tournament's results; your teammates, anyone invited to this team, and anyone holding this team's join or claim link see your name before the Event, and so, while this team is listed as recruiting, does everyone holding an active Entry on this Track"), and a Player's lever is hiding their Player page, which leaves the name in the result without a link, as everywhere. Concretely, a match's lineup is the active playing Entrants of each side at the instant its result is recorded, and a team's placement is credited to every person who was on the lineup of at least one of its matches (not "active at completion", which drops a semi-finalist who left before the final); a bench player who never played still shares the placement — a known imprecision that per-match lineups, if ever recorded, refine without changing the definition; the Organizer's maximum is the cap on it. The Participant stores those credited Player ids once, when the tournament first completes; a credited typed-name Entrant claimed afterwards (within ADR 0018's window) adds its Player id to the set, which is one of ADR 0030's recomputation triggers, and a wrong-claim reset undoes a claim here too: lineups read the typed-name row re-entered in its place instead of the row it ended, so the wrong claimer's Player id leaves the set, a later claim of that row adds the right one, and the reset is a trigger like the claim it undoes.

**The rest, by rule.** Visibility: the bracket shows team names; the registrant list shows team and active Entrants, never a forming Entry; forming Entries' Entrants are in the Registrants audience, not the confirmed one; Waitlist names stay hidden. Notices for an Entry reach the captain and the account Entrants (and the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry), paged by Entrant count, and a notice to an Event's or a Track's Registrants reaches every Registrant and the staff of its active Squad-linked Entries; an Entrant's own changes (added, left, removed, moved, or claimed, and the Entry falling below the minimum through one of them) reach the person and the captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry, folded into one line per Entry per day). The captain's and the staff's view of the lineup shows each Entrant's name, state, and whether they are reachable — never the participant code or an address; the Organizer's list shows every active Entry, forming ones included, listed as recruiting or not, with each Entrant's name, code, and reachability. "Staff", throughout, means the Community's members acting for its Squad-linked Entries, whatever their role (ADR 0023's `staff` role included). A team's placement appears on each credited Entrant's Player page "as a member of <team>". "Last time's team" is offered to the previous captain or staff: Squad-linked → active rows for those still on the Squad; ad-hoc → invitations; typed names listed for re-entry. Deletion of an account: active Entrant rows in Events not over, unless their Track's tournament has completed, end as `left` (the Entry cancelled if it was the last playing one, a forming Entry only if it was the last one); rows in Events over or in a completed tournament lose their identity and stay; either way a deleted captain's flag passes by the succession rule. Reaping an abandoned anonymous identity (ADR 0022) never cancels an Entry: it removes the identity from its active Entrant rows, which stay as typed-name rows the Organizer can act on and a claim link the Organizer issues can claim, its pending and `declined` rows going with it, and the Entry stays. A suspension moves the captain flag by the succession rule — to the longest-standing active account playing Entrant who is not suspended, else to such an anonymous one on an anonymous-allowed Track — and the flag does not pass back when the suspension is lifted; a suspended captain whose flag no one can take (every captain on a one-player Track) keeps it, and the Entry is left to its Organizer as ADR 0027 leaves a suspended person's Entries: it does not end (a waiting one keeps its place, passed over by settle until the lift, above), it is not an Entry nobody can act for, and its captain acts for it again once the suspension is lifted. Entries of a suspended or deleted Community keep their name and Entrants; the Squad link is inert while the Community is suspended or after its deletion, so the Entry takes a captain by the succession rule (the longest-standing active account playing Entrant, else an anonymous one on an anonymous-allowed Track, never a suspended person), and only one with none falls under the "Entry nobody can act for" rule. A merge ends the duplicate Entrant row where one person held two active playing rows on a Track (the other team told), or two active rows on one Entry, and deletes a pending row that would duplicate another on its Entry (the merged identity's, when both are pending). Short-handed Entries and the "no team formed" notice are the only new outcome wording; "withdrawn" keeps its meaning. Default Entrant names (following the account name, as today) and the 120-character person name are unchanged; a team name may be renamed once every seven days, as an account name may, and the Organizer's rename lock is on the Entry and survives a captain-flag hand-over.

## Considered Options

- **One Entry per person, bundled into a team record** (the grilling's first proposal). Every seat operation would move N rows (a Waitlist of teams, Offers to teams), and the team record would still need a name, a state, and a Participant — the Entry in all but name.
- **A team Entry whose members are data (an array of Player ids).** Loses the per-person gates, notices, claims, sanctions, and Players; a second code path beside the person Entry.
- **A holder (the person who created the Entry) as the team's owner.** An uncancellable account deletion would cancel the whole team's seat; a departed holder could cancel out of spite; a banned person could field a team as a non-playing holder. The captain flag on a playing Entrant, and the staff for Squad-linked Entries, dissolve all three.
- **Registering automatically when the minimum is reached.** It registers into a closed window when a Squad fills before it opens, lets a stranger's joining force the seat, and races the captain's own press; a notice at the minimum does the job.
- **Seating short-handed Entries** ("3 of 4, incomplete"). A one-person Entry would hold a seat; the minimum stops meaning anything.
- **Returning a waiting Entry that fell below the minimum to forming.** It would lose its Waitlist place.
- **A separate "waiting for a team" pool and a "mixed" Track.** Both are the forming Entry with one Entrant and the recruiting switch.
- **Substitutes as a concept, per-match lineups now.** The minimum–maximum range is the substitute; per-match records are an additive refinement the crediting rule already anticipates.
- **Flat teams in which every member can act** (one person could expel everyone), **ad-hoc teams as real Communities** (garbage against the 10-Community limit), **replacing the swap deadline with the registration close** (day-of substitutions would depend on the Organizer), **no deadline** (a Squad could add a professional who never registered).
- **Non-playing captains on ad-hoc teams.** One anonymous identity could field name-typed teams until the Track was full; requiring the captain to play makes the one-active-Entrant invariant stop it.
- **Reading lineups only where the registrant list is open to everyone who can view** (the alternative to the lineup decision above). Wherever an Organizer narrowed the list, the lineup would stay hidden — those Entries would join chains only through a Squad or an intervention, and their players would not be credited — and a team's result would hide who played, where a one-player bracket shows the name.

## Consequences

- Schema: `entries` (Track, state with `forming` as its own value, team name with its former names and its lock, optional Squad, the pointer to the Entry it was copied from, Participant, `activePlayingCount`, listed flag, outcome notices as today plus "no team formed", and whether the opening's "you can register" was sent); `entrants` (Entry, Track denormalized, user or anonymous identity or none, Player, name, `playing`, `captain`, label, state and `playing` with the instant of each change, moved-to pointer, Grant, former code; indexes by Entry and state, by Track, user, and state, by Track and state, by Grant and state); `sanctions` (scope kind, the Track or Community, target kind — person or Community —, the person and, when one exists, the source Entrant for a person target, the target Community for a Community target, instant, `ended` instant, origin on a detach's copies; indexes by user and Community, by Community and user, by Track and user, by email tag); Grants gain the Entry scope; Participants gain the Squad of a Squad-linked Entry and `creditedPlayerIds`; Tracks gain minimum, maximum, the recruiting switch, the swap-deadline setting, and the registration requirement as a three-step ladder. The Entry row is the pivot of optimistic concurrency; registration is idempotent; daily caps use the rate limiter; no operation scans a Track's Entrants unbounded.
- The settle function, the decision function, Capacity, the Waitlist, Offers, publication, substitution, restoration, and derived walkovers keep their rules with "Entry" meaning this Entry, save where the minimum enters or a suspended captain keeps the flag: the Waitlist asks the minimum again (settle's walk and the close-time fill pass a waiting Entry below it over, and its Offer cannot be taken until it is back at the minimum, its deadline lapsing it otherwise), it passes over a waiting Entry whose suspended captain keeps the flag until the lift (the suspension returning its Offer, its place kept), and a short-handed Entry is _absent_, its unplayed matches derived walkovers, which publication keeps and lists; the "one active Entry per user per Track" gate becomes invariant (1) above; the anonymous holdings cap counts Entry and Entrant rows together (the identity's Entrant rows in every state and each Entry it has one on, once); Event-wide notices, publication diffs among them, are paged by Entrant count.
- ADR 0013, 0014, 0015, 0016, 0017, 0018, 0019, 0022, and 0027 carry amendment notes reading Entry and Entrant in place of "an Entry and its holder" (who may wait and accept an Offer for a team, which name the Organizer's rename locks, the Entry scope of Grants and the Entrant a claim takes, the team notices with their audiences and the staff fold); ADR 0023's note reads "block" as a Track-scope sanction and its table's lineup row as naming Entrants; ADR 0019's note makes the completed lineup part of the published result, which the registrant-list audience never governs; ADR 0027 gains the join-link and invitation caps, the 32-row cap per Entry, and the suspended captain's flag.
- ADR 0017's two-phase list gains the team-era operations: raising a Track's minimum or maximum, re-parenting or releasing a child Community, deleting a child, banning a Community, removing a player from a descendant's Squad, the Organizer's removal of an Entrant, the Organizer's lineup change after the swap deadline, and the captain-row reset. ADR 0021, 0022, and 0024 carry amendment notes (participant codes on Entrants, the merge acting on Entrant rows, the anonymous holdings cap counting Entry and Entrant rows, the `players` alias, and a merge's conflicting Entrant row ending as `left`).
