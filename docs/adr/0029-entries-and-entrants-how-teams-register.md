# An Entry takes a seat and Entrants stand on it: solo play is an Entry with one Entrant, teams form before they register, and a team's lineup is part of the published result

Revised 2026-10-22 (the owner's call): _anonymous allowed_ exists on one-player Tracks only — every Entrant of a team Entry is an account or a name the Organizer typed, which only an account claims — so the captain-typed name with a claim link, the anonymous captain, and the anonymous invitee are gone (the first form is in Considered Options); and the recruiting list's names follow the Event's registrant-names audience; on the same day the owner set the swap deadline's default to the Track's first result and dropped the per-team setting, kept the ringer rule under every setting, and required the maximum on every team Track, prefilled at the minimum plus two, and had a Squad-derived team name follow renames as a person's name does — into completed tournaments when the renamer so chooses — instead of freezing at completion (the per-team default, the optional maximum, and the freeze are in Considered Options). Every rule about people on the registration side — gates, notices, cancellation, claims, blocks, bans, codes, Players, deletion — was written on the Entry, and every rule about seats and states — Capacity, Waitlist, Offer, confirmed and ended states, Participant, publication, substitution — on the same Entry, because the unit of registration was always one person. Teams split the two. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 (rounds 11–15, 18 and 20 of its record) tried per-person Entries bundled into a team and a team Entry with members as data, and settled on the split itself: an **Entry** is the unit that takes a seat, an **Entrant** is a person on it, and a one-player Track is an Entry with exactly one Entrant, so the code has one path. [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) decides what a persistent team is; this decision is how a team — a Squad's, or one formed for the day — enters a Track.

**Entry and Entrant.** An Entry belongs to a Track and carries the seat-side facts: state (`forming` until it registers; then `confirmed`, `waitlisted`, or `offered`; ended as `cancelled`, `removed`, or `lapsed`), its team name (the Squad's display name of ADR 0028 by default for a Squad-linked Entry, which the team name follows through every change of that display name — a rename of the Community or the Squad, or the Community's going from one Squad to several or back, the latter Claude's extension — under the rule by which a person's name follows an account rename, the owner's call of 2026-10-22 so that people and teams have one name rule (the spared Entry and the ended Squad below are Claude's details; the former names and the Event-renamed team were in the option as sent): each change asks whoever makes it whether it applies to future Entries only or to every Entry still following the Squad, past ones included — an Entry spared by "future only" still follows, one whose Squad has ended follows nothing more — so a completed tournament's bracket shows the new name when the renamer so chooses, the former names are the Organizer's history and never displayed (the cost put with the option: the name a team played under survives only there, and the rename screen gains one question), and a team renamed for its Event by its captain or staff, or by the Organizer, stays as it is; the freeze at the tournament's completion is in Considered Options; the Entrants' names joined for an ad-hoc one; at most 40 characters; the Organizer's rename locks the Entry, as it locks a person's Entry today), an optional link to a Squad, an optional pointer to the Entry it was copied from (set when "last time's team" is used; team Rankings read it as a tie-breaker only, [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md)), its Participant once it is on a Roster, a denormalized count of active playing Entrants, and whether it is listed as recruiting. An Entrant belongs to an Entry and carries the person-side facts: an identity (an account, an anonymous identity, or a typed name with none), an optional Player (set at once for an identity, account or anonymous; at claim for a typed name — no ghost Players from typos), a display name under the existing name rules, a `playing` flag (on by default; the staff turn it off on a Squad-linked Entry), a `captain` flag, a label copied from the Squad membership (ADR 0028; editable by whoever may rename the team, reaching records at once like a name), the Grant it came through, a former code where a merge changed it, the instant of each state change and of each change of `playing`, and a state: `invited` or `requested` before consent, `active`, then `declined`, `left` (with a pointer to the Entry the person moved to, when they moved), or `removed` (with a pointer to the typed-name row a wrong-claim reset re-entered in its place, when it ended so, which lineups and credits read instead). Entrant rows are never deleted while they are records; `invited`, `requested`, and `declined` rows are not records, never appear on the Organizer's list, and go with the account; while pending, `invited` and `requested` rows count against the Entry's maximum apart from its active rows, and every invitation or request counts against its sender's daily limit when it is sent, whatever becomes of it. The Track declares the number of playing Entrants an Entry needs (a minimum and a maximum, both required on a team Track — the maximum prefilled at the minimum plus two when the Track is created, the Organizer's to change, and never above the platform cap of 32 active rows per Entry nor below the minimum — a change that would put the minimum above the maximum is refused, the maximum raised first, and the prefill is clamped at the cap; the owner's call of 2026-10-22 on the reasoning put to them, that an unset maximum left the bench imprecision below uncapped — the owner chose a required maximum prefilled at the minimum plus two, the two matching the bench of two that other rules allow, a convention put as open to another number, at the cost put of one more field on the Track's creation screen and the MCP create call; the floor at the minimum, the refusal of a crossing change, and the clamp are Claude's details); a one-player Track is 1/1 and shows none of this, and whether a Track is one-player or a team Track is set at its creation and changes afterwards only through the maximum (Claude's reading; the ladder paragraph below says what raising or lowering it past 1 does). There is no mixed Track: a Track is for Entries of one size range, and a person who wants to play alone on a team Track is an Entry of one, forming until it finds teammates. The maximum bounds playing Entrants; non-playing Entrants (coach, manager) exist only on Squad-linked Entries and count against the row cap alone.

**Three invariants, one place each.** (1) A person is an active playing Entrant on at most one Entry per Track, forming Entries included — checked by every act that activates a person (forming an Entry, opening a join link, accepting an invitation, a request being accepted, the staff adding a Squad member, claiming, turning `playing` on, an Organizer's lineup change), and "leave A and join B" is offered as one step. (2) A person has at most one unended Entrant row per Entry; a `declined` row blocks only a repeat of what was declined — re-invitation from that Entry after an invitee declined, a new request to it after the captain declined one. (3) An Entry needs someone who can act for it: an ad-hoc Entry has exactly one captain while any Entrant the flag can pass to is active (the captain must be a playing Entrant; the flag passes to the longest-standing active account playing Entrant, and never to a suspended person — no succession, hand-over, or "make teams" gives it to one, and a suspension moves it when anyone can take it, while a suspended captain whose flag no one can take keeps it until the activation, claim, or lift that lets someone take it moves it, and the Entry is left to its Organizer meanwhile, ADR 0027); a Squad-linked Entry has no captain while its Squad link is live — the Squad's staff act for it (ADR 0028's capability), and the succession rule gives it one once the link is inert (its Community suspended or deleted). An Entry nobody can act for (one with neither a captain's row nor a live Squad link; a typed-name row carrying the captain flag, such as the first Entrant name on a pasted line, is a captain's row, the Organizer acting on it until its claim grants the claimer the captain's powers) ends as lapsed while it is waiting, its Entrants told, and stays while confirmed, the Organizer told, as long as its Event is not over and its Track's tournament has not completed; after either, an Entry is a record that needs no one to act for it, and nobody is told. Standing, which succession and the choice of a made team's captain read, counts from when a row became active, never from its claim, and rows one act made active stand in the order that act entered them, as a pasted line lists them. An Entry that has registered and whose last active playing Entrant leaves ends as cancelled (a forming Entry ends as cancelled when its last Entrant leaves or is moved out by "make teams" — the moved Entrants are `left` with a pointer to their new Entry — and cannot be listed as recruiting while it has no playing Entrant) (on a one-player Track "leave" is "cancel"); when the Organizer removes the last one, the preview asks whether to remove the team, and when the staff remove the last one or turn its `playing` off, they are asked whether to cancel the team.

**Teams form, then register.** An Entry starts `forming`: no seat, any number of Entrants (playing ones up to the maximum, every row up to the 32-row cap, fewer than the minimum included), outside the seat equation, the Grant use counts, the Roster limit, the Waitlist, and the registration window (a forming Entry may be created and filled before the Track opens: forming passes the decision function's gates other than the window, the publication guard, Capacity, a Grant's use count, the Waitlist's, the minimum, and the Roster limit, so no Entry forms once the Track's tournament is live or completed), and not reliance that keeps an Event from returning to draft (ADR 0017). Registering is always an explicit act of the captain or the staff, allowed only once the Entry has at least the minimum of playing Entrants, and it runs the existing decision function once (confirmed, waitlisted, refused with the reason — a refused registration leaves the Entry forming, to register again once the reason is gone) — idempotent, and the first team to press "register" takes the last seat. The Waitlist asks ADR 0015's test of those who act for the Entry: it joins the Waitlist only while its captain is reachable or its Squad link is live (the staff always are; asked when it joins, so a waiting Entry whose captain flag later passes to an Entrant without a deliverable channel keeps its place, its Offer reaching that captain in-app) — a captain without a deliverable channel takes a free seat or is told to fix the email or pass the captain flag to a reachable teammate (the captain hands the flag to any active account playing Entrant who is not suspended, a refusal giving no reason; a suspension moves it by the succession rule) — and an Offer is accepted or declined with the captain's or the staff's session, a non-captain Entrant only leaving; queueing again at the back after its Offer is declined or lapses (ADR 0015; never offered from an Entry whose Squad has ended, ADR 0028) is theirs too, the one-tap invitation reaching them, and copies the lapsed Entry as "last time's team" does (those still on its Squad active again, invitations to the rest of an ad-hoc team, typed names listed), registering the copy in the same act if it holds the minimum, like any registration, a copy below it staying forming as any forming Entry does. The minimum is the one entry gate asked again at the Waitlist: a waiting Entry that fell below it (an Entrant left, or a sanction ended their row) keeps its place but is passed over when a seat frees, and an Offer it holds can be taken only once it is back at the minimum, so a team below the minimum is never confirmed by the queue — not by settle's walk when a seat frees, and not by the fill at the close — while the Organizer's by-hand confirmation may still seat it, warned as short-handed; its captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry) is told to add a player to keep the place, and crossing the minimum in either direction is a seat input that schedules settle, so a team that restocks while a seat is already free is confirmed at once. An Entry whose captain is suspended is passed over the same way while they hold its flag — until it moves to someone who can take it (by the suspension, or later by the activation, claim, or lift that lets someone take it, below) or the suspension is lifted — with no Offer and no automatic acceptance, an Offer it holds returned by the suspension before any flag moves, its place kept, as an Event's suspension returns its Offers; the suspension, its lift, and such a later move are seat inputs on the Tracks concerned, so the Entry is offered a free seat again once its flag has moved or the suspension is lifted. When the count reaches the minimum while registration is open — or registration opens while the count is at the minimum or above — the captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry) is told "you can register" and the other Entrants see "waiting for the captain to register" ("waiting for the staff to register" on a Squad-linked Entry), and the captain's acceptance of a request that reaches it while registration is open offers "accept and register" in one tap (an invitee's acceptance registers nothing; before the opening the Entry shows the opening time instead). Lowering the minimum is always allowed, and lowering the maximum is allowed down to the minimum; neither registers anything by itself (a forming Entry that reaches the minimum because it was lowered is told "you can register" under the same rule — only while registration is open); raising either is two-phase under ADR 0017, and the preview of any Track-setting change lists the forming Entries it affects. Entries still forming when the tournament goes live (or the Event ends, on a Track whose tournament never went live or that has none) end as lapsed, "no team formed". A Track may list its forming Entries as recruiting (an Organizer switch, off where the maximum is 1 and on by default elsewhere, the Organizer of a Track for Squad teams only being the one to turn it off, as the option said): the list shows each recruiting Entry's team name, its Entrants' display names (nothing else: no code, no reachability) and its count against the minimum to the Organizer and to the Event's registrant-names audience (everyone who can view, by default; Registrants; organizers — ADR 0019; the one setting that says who sees names before the Event, the owner's call of 2026-10-22), anyone outside it seeing only how many Entries are recruiting, never on the registrant list, which shows no forming Entry; a captain lists their Entry (never a Squad-linked one, whose Entrants come from its Squad, which is also why it has no join link), and listing it is consent to be placed on a team by the Organizer and to appear on that list, both of which the listing screen says; it is sendou.ink's "looking for group" without a second mechanism. An Organizer's "make teams from the recruiting list" makes the number of teams the Organizer asks for, named Team 1…N, and moves each recruiting Entry's Entrants together into one of them, at random or in an order the Organizer sets — an Organizer act, skipping the gates as the Organizer's own entries do (below) rather than going through the activation function, and like them never past the maximum or the 32-row cap (a recruiting Entry that fits no team stays on the list, and a team that receives none is not made); each team is an ad-hoc forming Entry (fewer than the minimum included) for its captain to register, the captain being the longest-standing account playing Entrant, else the first row placed, never a suspended person — a row "make teams" creates keeps the start of the row it came from, so standing on a made team, for its captain and for later succession alike, counts from joining the recruiting Entry. A made team comes through no Grant, so its registration passes its Track's invite-only or hidden setting as the Organizer's own entries do — the Organizer made it —, every other gate applying as to any team; otherwise no made team could register on an invite-only or hidden Track, where the recruiting list remains. An Organizer's own entries (pasted names: one team per line, the team's name and then, after a delimiter, its Entrants' names, the first of them the captain's row) skip the gates as they do today, short of the minimum included; the Organizer adds people by name or from the recruiting list, never by code.

**Short-handed is a fact about a seated Entry, not a state.** A confirmed Entry below the minimum is "short-handed" in the UI; the format engine has one predicate, _absent_ = the Entry has ended or its playing Entrants are below the minimum, with ADR 0016's derived walkover behind it: nothing is recorded until a result depending on it is, the opponent is not told automatically (counts move), the pending match is not shown as a walkover while it is only derived — once a dependent result records it, the walkover is labelled "short-handed", and the UI word for _absent_ is "short-handed, cannot play" —, the Organizer is warned and the captain (the staff, for a Squad-linked Entry) is told that the Entry is short-handed, and an automatic publication includes short-handed Entries and lists them in its preview, and publishing by hand warns about them. A swap is one act — A out, B in — so no instant of short-handedness is created when B's consent already exists (a Squad's standing consent, or an invitation B accepted, which made B active already); otherwise it is a removal and an invitation with a short-handed interval between.

**Consent is the Entrant's own act, or standing consent given on joining a Squad or on listing an Entry as recruiting.** A person becomes `active` only by forming an Entry, opening a join link, accepting an invitation, having their request accepted, claiming a typed name — or, for a Squad member, by the standing consent they gave when they joined the Squad ("the staff may enter you in tournaments; you are told every time and may leave any time"; a Squad larger than the Track's maximum gets a "choose up to N" step, and "last time's team" the same) — or, for an Entrant of an Entry listed as recruiting, by the Organizer's placement, which the listing consents to. A person who left an Entry returns to it only by their own acceptance (on a Squad-linked Entry, of the staff's invitation: the standing consent does not re-add them); leaving the Squad ends the standing consent. Promotion of an ad-hoc team into a Squad is an invitation to each account Entrant — a single-use "join this Squad" Grant delivered as a notice (ADR 0028), each counting against the sender's 50 team invitations a day — never an automatic add. The Track's registration requirement applies to every Entrant row — a joiner, an invitee, and a requester alike, and a claimer on a team Track, a one-player Track's claim links staying exempt (ADR 0021) — and is a ladder of one setting: _anonymous allowed_ (anonymous Entrants; on a one-player Track only, the owner's call of 2026-10-22, on the reasoning put to them: the people who act for a team — registering it, swapping, taking an Offer — must be reachable, so a team Track's requirement is never _anonymous allowed_ — the three transition rules that follow are Claude's — a Track created with a maximum above 1 under an Event default of _anonymous allowed_ stores _account required_, raising a one-player Track's maximum above 1 is refused while an anonymous Entrant is active on it and otherwise sets its requirement to _account required_, which the change's preview says, and lowering a maximum to 1 leaves the requirement as it is, the Organizer choosing _anonymous allowed_ afterwards if they want it; a typed name comes only from the Organizer, whose "add by name" keeps a four-player team from being stranded at 3 on the day) ⊃ _account required_ (the default: every Entrant is an account with a verified email, that is, reachable; a typed name only by the Organizer, whose entries are exempt as today) ⊃ _Squad-linked teams only_ (every Entry links a Squad, which already means accounts). A Squad-linked Entry's Entrants come from the Squad; staff may add nobody else — the Organizer may.

**A join link is a register Grant scoped to one Entry.** The scope ladder is Event > Track > Entry. An Entry-scope Grant is issued by the captain (an account — narrower than a public QR code) — a Squad-linked Entry has none, since its Entrants come from its Squad and a guest joins the Squad first; one unended per Entry (re-issuing replaces), link-only — it is never given a code — and outside the Event's register-Grant cap (Event-wide Grant operations are paged); it stores no end and ends with the Entry, the swap deadline, or a change that makes its Track invite-only or hidden or its Event private (listed in that change's preview); it admits a person through the activation function (the per-person gates, the Track's registration requirement among them, the maximum, the deadline), not the registration decision, so it works on a Track whose tournament is live; it admits while playing Entrants are below the maximum and so are its uses — the rows it admitted that are still active or were removed, so a leaving Entrant frees a use and a removal does not (re-issue to admit another); it appears in the Organizer's Grant list and can be revoked there, and the Entry's captain may revoke it outright; it cannot be issued on an invite-only or hidden Track or in a private Event (there the Organizer's claim link, the Squad's standing consent, and invitations to recruiting Entries remain). An invitation is an Entrant row in state `invited`, a request one in state `requested` created by the person — the same row from the other side, which, like an invitation, needs an account to send and reaches the captain as an invitation reaches its invitee (fixed wording, the requester's name on opening, with a report button), and which the captain accepts or declines one at a time (a Squad-linked Entry takes no requests: its Entrants come from its Squad); a request from someone active on another Entry of the Track is their "leave A and join B" — the request says so, and the captain's acceptance moves them — and a pending request never stands while its sender is active on an Entry of its Track other than the one it names for leaving, or is barred there: whatever brings that about — an activation, a merge, a ban, a sanction on that Track, or a suspension — withdraws it, the captain told nothing (a suspension's pages write it, a request counting as withdrawn until then), so an acceptance never moves anyone out of an Entry they did not agree to leave and never meets a bar. Invitations go to a closed set (Entrants of recruiting Entries on that Track, last time's Entrants, and a Squad member who left the Squad-linked Entry inviting them) and are sent by a captain (on a Squad-linked Entry, whose Entrants come from its Squad, only by the staff, and only to such a member), carry fixed wording with the Event's and Track's names (the team name on opening, with a report button), need an account to send (pushing a notice at someone is an account's act), and none at the recipient, who is reached in-app as an existing Entrant (by email where they have one), count 50 a day per account (a person's requests count there too), are capped while pending at the Entry's maximum (counted apart from active rows), and are withdrawn — pending requests too — when the Entry reaches its maximum or ends. An invitation gives view of the Entry and Track as a Grant does. A claim is the existing claim Grant scoped to an Entry — "which one are you?" — and claiming a captain's row grants the captain's powers; the Entry has one unended claim link (re-issuing replaces it), issued by the Organizer, whose typed names are the only ones (above); a wrong claim is reset at the Entrant (a captain's: the re-entered row being the captain's row again, so no succession runs, the name restored, join links issued since revoked, removals since listed), the reset re-issuing the Entry's claim link so that the link the wrong claimer used ends at once. Restoration (ADR 0016) re-enters the Entrants as typed-name copies with the Entry's claim link, keeping the Entry's Squad link, so a Squad-linked team's staff act for the re-entry.

**The swap deadline is the Organizer's, with three settings and no new instant.** Lineups change freely until the deadline: lock at the bracket's publication; lock at the Track's first match result, a recorded walkover included (the default, the owner's call of 2026-10-22; the per-team instant it replaces is in Considered Options); never lock — under which, once the Track has its first result, any lineup change is notified to the Organizer and recorded on the bracket's activity. Under every setting, once the Track has its first result, a person who was on any Entry of the Track cannot join another (the ringer rule, which the Organizer may override — the refusal says the person has already played on this Track for another team and points them to the Organizer; kept under every setting, the owner's call of 2026-10-22) — a no-op for captains and staff where lineups are locked (there only the Organizer's lineup change meets it, as a recorded override) and the one guard under _never lock_, where a player could otherwise leave a team that has played for one that has not. Turning `playing` on goes through the activation function as an addition does (the per-person gates, the maximum, the deadline) and counts as joining for the ringer rule. Until the deadline the captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry) may also remove an Entrant, whose row ends as `removed`, alone or as a swap's "A out". Entrants may always leave; the Organizer may always change a lineup ("the Organizer's lineup change", distinct from Substitution, which re-points a Participant to another Entry — the way a no-show team is replaced by the next waiting one). After the deadline the Organizer adds people by typed name or from the recruiting list only.

**One sanction row replaces the block and the ban.** A sanction has a scope (a Track, or a Community), the Track or Community, a target — a person, with the Entrant row it was made from when one exists (a Squad member banned from the ban preview has none), or, at Community scope only, a Community, with neither — and an instant; a person-target row is set by the Organizer on a Track or by the banning Community's owner or admin (from an Entrant row, the banning Community is the Event's own), a Community-target row by that owner or admin from the ban preview or the Community page; the gate is one lookup per scope kind. A sanction can be lifted — a Track-scope one by any Organizer of the Event, a Community-scope one by the banning Community's owner or admin — which sets an `ended` instant on the row (rows are kept), reinstates nothing (ended Entrant rows stay ended), and is told to nobody. A Track-scope sanction ends the person's active and invited rows on that Track as `removed` and their view, and withdraws their requests there (the team may become short-handed; the person is told only that they were removed from the team; what it ends shows others only what an ordinary act would — an active row as the Organizer's removal, a request as its sender's withdrawal, an invitation as one deleted with its invitee's account, the inviting captain and staff told nothing), and a ban on a person, when it is set, ends and withdraws the same rows in the same way in the Events it applies to that are not over, unless the Track's tournament has completed (ADR 0028); a typed-name Entrant has no person and so cannot be sanctioned, only removed. The person is told the true reason when they act themselves (joining, accepting), and the captain and staff are told nothing; a captain or staff adding someone — or pressing register for an Entry that holds such a person — is told only "X cannot be included" — the one wording for a ban, a sanction, suspension, "already active on this Track", and "already on another Squad's Entry here" (the one wording never answers sending an invitation or accepting a request, since X's own act, accepting the invitation or making the request, is where X alone is told a refusal's true reason and offered "leave A and join B"), checked once when the addition is submitted, never while names are typed — and chooses to go on without X (at a registration, by removing X, whose row ends as `removed`) or to stop, while X is told the true reason privately, because sanctions are never disclosed to third parties; a Community ban on the team itself is said plainly ("this team cannot register with this host"), since the staff are the party. The removal preview offers a sanction on each Entrant with an identity (an account or an anonymous one) and says typed names cannot be sanctioned. A Community ban is evaded by the same five people entering ad hoc, which the preview says; the person ban is what follows people. The operator's redaction (ADR 0027) covers team names.

**A team's lineup is part of the published result.** A one-player bracket shows the person's name; a team bracket shows the team's name and the registrant list shows the Entrants under ADR 0019's audience setting — which would let a registrant-list setting of "organizers only" hide, after the fact, who played, while Rankings read lineups (ADR 0030) and Player pages credit them. The owner decided (2026-10-15) that the playing lineup of a completed tournament is part of its published result: who played is a public fact of a public tournament, as the name in a one-player bracket is, and the registrant-list audience governs the registrant list, never the published result. Registration says so ("your name is visible to everyone who can see this tournament's results; your teammates, anyone invited to this team, and anyone holding this team's join or claim link see your name before the Event, and, while this team is listed as recruiting, so does everyone this Event shows registrant names to"), and a Player's lever is hiding their Player page, which leaves the name in the result without a link, as everywhere. Concretely, a match's lineup is the active playing Entrants of each side at the instant its result is recorded, and a team's placement is credited to every person who was on the lineup of at least one of its matches (not "active at completion", which drops a semi-finalist who left before the final); a bench player who never played still shares the placement — a known imprecision that per-match lineups, if ever recorded, refine without changing the definition; the Organizer's maximum, required on every team Track, is the cap on it. The Participant stores those credited Player ids once, when the tournament first completes; a credited typed-name Entrant claimed afterwards (within ADR 0018's window) adds its Player id to the set, which is one of ADR 0030's recomputation triggers, and a wrong-claim reset undoes a claim here too: lineups read the typed-name row re-entered in its place instead of the row it ended, so the wrong claimer's Player id leaves the set, a later claim of that row adds the right one, and the reset is a trigger like the claim it undoes. Once the tournament completes, its Share Link shows each team's lineup — the credited Entrants, typed names included, under their current names — through an identity-free query of its own over the Participants' Entries, as the registrant list is the Event page's own (ADR 0019), so the bracket's queries stay Entry-free.

**The rest, by rule.** Visibility: the bracket shows team names; the registrant list shows team and active Entrants, never a forming Entry; forming Entries' Entrants are in the Registrants audience, not the confirmed one; Waitlist names stay hidden. Notices for an Entry reach the captain and the account Entrants (and the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry), paged by Entrant count, and a notice to an Event's or a Track's Registrants reaches every Registrant and the staff of its active Squad-linked Entries; an Entrant's own changes (added, left, removed, moved, claimed, or made captain — never saying why —, and the Entry falling below the minimum through one of them) reach the person and the captain (the staff, on a Squad-linked Entry, folded into one line per Entry per day). The captain's and the staff's view of the lineup shows each Entrant's name, state, and whether they are reachable — never the participant code or an address; the Organizer's list shows every active Entry, forming ones included, listed as recruiting or not, with each Entrant's name, code, and reachability. "Staff", throughout, means the Community's members acting for its Squad-linked Entries, whatever their role (ADR 0023's `staff` role included). A team's placement appears on each credited Entrant's Player page "as a member of <team>". "Last time's team" is offered to the previous captain or staff (never from an Entry whose Squad has ended, ADR 0028): Squad-linked → active rows for those still on the Squad; ad-hoc → invitations; typed names listed for re-entry. Deletion of an account: active Entrant rows in Events not over, unless their Track's tournament has completed, end as `left` (the Entry cancelled if it was the last playing one, a forming Entry only if it was the last one); rows in Events over or in a completed tournament lose their identity and stay; either way a deleted captain's flag passes by the succession rule. Reaping an abandoned anonymous identity (ADR 0022) never cancels an Entry: it removes the identity from its active Entrant rows — on one-player Tracks, since team Tracks require accounts —, which stay as typed-name rows the Organizer can act on and a claim link the Organizer issues can claim, and the Entry stays, the flag staying on the row, now a typed-name captain's row (above). A suspension moves the captain flag by the succession rule — to the longest-standing active account playing Entrant who is not suspended — and the flag does not pass back when the suspension is lifted; a suspended captain whose flag no one can take (every captain on a one-player Track) keeps it until someone can — the activation, claim, or lift that lets them moves it by the succession rule — and the Entry is left to its Organizer meanwhile as ADR 0027 leaves a suspended person's Entries: it does not end (a waiting one keeps its place, passed over by settle until the flag moves or the lift, above), it is not an Entry nobody can act for, and its captain acts for it again once the suspension is lifted. Entries of a suspended or deleted Community keep their name and Entrants; the Squad link is inert while the Community is suspended or after its deletion, so the Entry takes a captain by the succession rule (the longest-standing active account playing Entrant, never a suspended person), and only one with none falls under the "Entry nobody can act for" rule. A merge ends the duplicate Entrant row where one person held two active playing rows on a Track (the other team told), or two active rows on one Entry, and deletes a pending row that would duplicate another on its Entry (the merged identity's, when both are pending). Short-handed Entries and the "no team formed" notice are the only new outcome wording; "withdrawn" keeps its meaning. Default Entrant names (following the account name, as today) and the 120-character person name are unchanged; a team name may be renamed once every seven days, as an account name may, and the Organizer's rename lock is on the Entry and survives a captain-flag hand-over; a Community and a Squad are renamed under the same limit (once every seven days, plus one correction within an hour), since their renames reach team names, and each keeps its former names with their instants, which its rename limit reads; a change that reaches an Entry writes the Entry's own former name with its instant, which the Organizer's history reads (the names a team carried while its Entry was on their Event) and which counts against no captain's limit.

## Considered Options

- **One Entry per person, bundled into a team record** (the grilling's first proposal). Every seat operation would move N rows (a Waitlist of teams, Offers to teams), and the team record would still need a name, a state, and a Participant — the Entry in all but name.
- **A team Entry whose members are data (an array of Player ids).** Loses the per-person gates, notices, claims, sanctions, and Players; a second code path beside the person Entry.
- **A holder (the person who created the Entry) as the team's owner.** An uncancellable account deletion would cancel the whole team's seat; a departed holder could cancel out of spite; a banned person could field a team as a non-playing holder. The captain flag on a playing Entrant, and the staff for Squad-linked Entries, dissolve all three.
- **Registering automatically when the minimum is reached.** It registers into a closed window when a Squad fills before it opens, lets a stranger's joining force the seat, and races the captain's own press; a notice at the minimum does the job.
- **Seating short-handed Entries** ("3 of 4, incomplete"). A one-person Entry would hold a seat; the minimum stops meaning anything.
- **Returning a waiting Entry that fell below the minimum to forming.** It would lose its Waitlist place.
- **A separate "waiting for a team" pool and a "mixed" Track.** Both are the forming Entry with one Entrant and the recruiting switch.
- **Substitutes as a concept, per-match lineups now.** The minimum–maximum range is the substitute; per-match records are an additive refinement the crediting rule already anticipates.
- **Flat teams in which every member can act** (one person could expel everyone), **ad-hoc teams as real Communities** (garbage against the 10-Community limit), **replacing the swap deadline with the registration close** (day-of substitutions would depend on the Organizer), **no deadline** (a Squad could add a professional who never registered), **the bracket's publication as the default** (published the night before, every day-of swap fell to the Organizer), **no default, the Organizer always choosing, and changes after the start subject to the Organizer's approval** (weighed in the search: more work for the Organizer and nothing else), **lock at this team's first match result as the default** (the default from 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22: the one locking setting under which, after the Track's first result, a team that has not yet played can still take a player — one who left a team that has — so the ringer rule had to bite under the default; the owner restored the Track-wide instant, which every team reads off the bracket, a late-scheduled team asking the Organizer — the form Battlefy, Tonamel, Challengermode, and competitive communities' rulebooks use, fixed at the start and changed through staff after), **an optional maximum, unset reading as the 32-row cap** (the first form: it left the bench imprecision uncapped, a nine-member Squad credited for a 5v5 — the owner required the maximum on 2026-10-22), **a required maximum prefilled at the minimum, raised only when the Organizer allows a bench** (put beside it: no substitute could register until the Organizer noticed, and the day's sixth player became the Organizer's chore), **an unset maximum read as the minimum plus a fixed bench** (weighed in the search: it keeps the unset state the required number removes), **a Squad-derived team name frozen at the tournament's completion** (the first form, 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22: the bracket kept the name the team played under, while a person's name follows their account rename into past rows when they choose; the owner aligned the two on 2026-10-22, one rule being easier for users to understand — Claude's example beside it: a first Community is named after its person, so a frozen team name could have kept a name its person had changed everywhere else), **copying the name at registration and following nothing after** (two teams of one Community told apart only by a rename of their own), **locking at the bracket's publication** (a typo fixed after publication never reaching the bracket), **and following forever without asking** (a past bracket showing today's name, the name played under lost) — the three put beside the frozen form on 2026-10-22 and again beside the aligned one; renaming past results on request after completion, "now X" beside a frozen name, and locking the name with the lineup at the swap deadline, weighed in the search, rebuilt what Rankings and the Community page already show.
- **The recruiting switch off by default, or a separate consent to being placed** (the forms put beside the written one on 2026-10-22). Off by default ends a Track whose Organizer never noticed the switch with a lone participant who found nobody; a second checkbox adds a step where nearly everyone answers the same way and leaves the Organizer unable to do anything for the one who declined — listing is the wish to play with someone, and placement the Organizer's only tool for it. The owner kept the written form.
- **The ringer rule under "no lock" only, or never refusing but telling the Organizer and recording it** (the forms put beside the rule under every setting on 2026-10-22, the first the document's earlier form). Under the per-team lock then the default, the first let an eleven o'clock invitation through with nobody told; the second widened the no-lock treatment to every setting. The owner kept the rule under every setting: a rule Organizers write for themselves ("a helper plays for one team only"), idle where the lineup is locked and biting only where it is not, a legitimate day-of regrouping taking the Organizer's override; confining it to people who played a recorded match was weighed in the search and dropped, since it waves a substitute's move through.
- **Non-playing captains on ad-hoc teams.** One anonymous identity could field name-typed teams until the Track was full (a captain could type names until 2026-10-22, when team Tracks came to require accounts); requiring the captain to play makes the one-active-Entrant invariant stop it.
- **_Anonymous allowed_ on team Tracks** (the first form, 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22). A captain could type a teammate's name with a claim link, an anonymous person could join by link, invitation, or claim, an anonymous captain took a free seat or was told to add an email, the flag could pass to an anonymous Entrant, and reaping reached team rows — about ten rules for a case the default never showed, since a team Track started at _account required_ anyway. The owner confined _anonymous allowed_ to one-player Tracks on 2026-10-22, on the reasoning put to them: the people who act for a team — registering it, swapping, taking an Offer — must be reachable, and the Organizer's typed names cover the walk-in teammate; what goes is a casual team's members joining without an account, a path start.gg does not offer either. A captain-only account requirement, members anonymous, was put beside it on 2026-10-22 and not chosen: it keeps the member rules and leaves more Entries with nobody to take the flag. Letting an anonymous person request to join a team, fifty a day per identity, or request without a notice, seen only when the captain opens the team's screen (the forms put on 2026-10-22 beside the refusal, before team Tracks came to require accounts): an identity costs one robot check, so the first let throwaway identities press notices on captains and fill a team's unaccepted-request room against real requests and invitations, and the second cut the harm but added an exception where delivery depends on the sender and buried the request; an anonymous person could instead form a team of one and list it, reaching the same result through the Organizer's placement.
- **Recruiting-list names to the Organizer and to that Track's active Entrants only** (the first form). It drew a second line beside the registrant-names audience (ADR 0019), which already says who sees names before the Event and whose default is everyone who can view, start.gg's default too; the list follows that audience since 2026-10-22 (the owner's call), so an Organizer who narrows the registrant list narrows the recruiting list with it, where a fixed "everyone who can view" would have left the recruiting list open on an Event whose registrant list is narrowed; names to the Organizer and the captains alone, the other form put, would only have shut out the teammates who help recruit.
- **Reading lineups only where the registrant list is open to everyone who can view** (the alternative to the lineup decision above). Wherever an Organizer narrowed the list, the lineup would stay hidden — those Entries would join chains only through a Squad or an intervention, and their players would not be credited — and a team's result would hide who played, where a one-player bracket shows the name.

## Consequences

- Schema: `entries` (Track, state with `forming` as its own value, team name with its former names and its lock, optional Squad, the pointer to the Entry it was copied from, Participant, `activePlayingCount`, listed flag, whether "make teams" made it, outcome notices as today plus "no team formed", and whether the opening's "you can register" was sent); `entrants` (Entry, Track denormalized, user or anonymous identity or none, Player, name, `playing`, `captain`, label, state and `playing` with the instant of each change, moved-to pointer, the pointer to the row a wrong-claim reset re-entered in its place, Grant, former code; indexes by Entry and state, by Track, user, and state, by Track and state, by Grant and state); `sanctions` (scope kind, the Track or Community, target kind — person or Community —, the person and, when one exists, the source Entrant for a person target, the target Community for a Community target, instant, `ended` instant; indexes by user and Community, by Community and user, by Track and user, by email tag); Grants gain the Entry scope; Participants gain the Squad of a Squad-linked Entry and `creditedPlayerIds`; Tracks gain minimum, maximum, the recruiting switch, the swap-deadline setting, and the registration requirement as a three-step ladder. The Entry row is the pivot of optimistic concurrency; registration is idempotent; daily caps use the rate limiter; no operation scans a Track's Entrants unbounded.
- The settle function, the decision function, Capacity, the Waitlist, Offers, publication, substitution, restoration, and derived walkovers keep their rules with "Entry" meaning this Entry, save where the minimum enters, a suspended captain holds the flag, or a team "make teams" made registers: the Waitlist asks the minimum again (settle's walk and the close-time fill pass a waiting Entry below it over, and its Offer cannot be taken until it is back at the minimum, its deadline lapsing it otherwise), it passes over a waiting Entry while a suspended captain holds its flag (until the flag moves to someone who can take it or the suspension is lifted; the suspension returning its Offer, its place kept), the decision function passes a made team through its Track's invite-only or hidden setting as the Organizer's own entries do, every other gate applying, and a short-handed Entry is _absent_, its unplayed matches derived walkovers, which publication keeps and lists; the "one active Entry per user per Track" gate becomes invariant (1) above; the anonymous holdings cap counts Entry and Entrant rows together (the identity's Entrant rows in every state and each Entry it has one on, once); Event-wide notices, publication diffs among them, are paged by Entrant count.
- ADR 0013, 0014, 0015, 0016, 0017, 0018, 0019, 0022, and 0027 carry amendment notes reading Entry and Entrant in place of "an Entry and its holder" (who may wait and accept an Offer for a team, which name the Organizer's rename locks, the Entry scope of Grants and the Entrant a claim takes, the team notices with their audiences and the staff fold); ADR 0023's note reads "block" as a Track-scope sanction and its table's lineup row as naming Entrants; ADR 0019's note makes the completed lineup part of the published result, which the registrant-list audience never governs; ADR 0027 gains the join-link and invitation caps, the 32-row cap per Entry, and the suspended captain's flag. On 2026-10-22, with _anonymous allowed_ confined to one-player Tracks, the notes on ADR 0003, 0013, 0015, 0018, 0019, 0021, 0022, 0026, and 0027, and ADR 0024's body, lost their anonymous-captain, captain-typed-name, and join-link-without-email clauses.
- ADR 0017's two-phase list gains the team-era operations: raising a Track's minimum or maximum, banning a Community, the Organizer's removal of an Entrant, the Organizer's lineup change after the swap deadline, and the wrong-claim reset at any Entrant (a captain's row included). ADR 0021, 0022, and 0024 carry amendment notes (participant codes on Entrants, the merge acting on Entrant rows, the anonymous holdings cap counting Entry and Entrant rows, the `players` alias, and a merge's conflicting Entrant row ending as `left`). Story 24 reads "sanction a person on this Track, or lift it" in place of "block or unblock", story 17 "optionally sanction the person on that Track — or, as my Community's owner or admin, ban them from the Community". ADR 0017's note also says a forming Entry is not reliance for returning an Event to draft.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains Entrant, Lineup, Registration requirement, and Sanction and rewrites the entries the team rules change (Entry, Registrant, Waitlist, Offer, Access Grant, Participant, Reachable, and Account among them); `docs/specs/events.md` gains the team sections, the lineup rule, slice 11 (team Entries, the recruiting list among them), and the team stories, and rewrites the earlier stories and slices the team rules change.
- Rankings read `creditedPlayerIds` and the Entry chain ([ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md)).
