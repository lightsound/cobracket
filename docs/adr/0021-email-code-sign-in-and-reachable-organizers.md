# Sign-in is an email one-time code; publishing an Event needs a reachable Organizer; a known email merges the anonymous identity into the account

Amended by [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md), [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md), and [ADR 0030](./0030-team-rankings-chains-of-entries.md) (2026-10-15): participant numbers are derived codes, so the merge re-points no number rows — the former code is stored on the identity's Entrant rows; the holdings cap counts Entry and Entrant rows; the merged identity's `players` row is kept as an alias of the account's, not deleted (ADR 0030); Y's "Entries" below, where the merge re-points them, names them, or keeps them through the bans' union, read as its Entrant rows (an Entry carries no identity); and the per-Track conflict row, where one person held two active playing Entrant rows on one Track, ends as `left`, cancelling its Entry only when it was the last playing Entrant, a forming Entry only when it was the last Entrant (ADR 0029); where re-pointing would leave the person two unended rows on one Entry, a pending row goes (the merged identity's, when both are pending) and two active rows are asked about in the same way; a pending request the merge leaves against the request rules is withdrawn (ADR 0029); the merge preview counts the Communities that will see the code change (ADR 0028, which made the code per Community on 2026-10-22). Revised 2026-10-22 (the owner's call): two email accounts are never merged in place, but at the deletion of one of them the person may hand its competition history to the other (ADR 0022), built last of all. The registration form's "no pending Entry state" below is the sign-in's: on a team Track an Entry exists as `forming` before it registers, holding no seat, and "sign in to wait" is a one-player Track's question, since a team Track requires an account of every Entrant (ADR 0029, 2026-10-22).

Account sign-in was decided to ship with Events (ADR 0013) and left to a grilling of its own (`docs/specs/events.md`, pending questions). That grilling, 2026-10-13/14 with the owner, settled the three questions that shape the data model, and this ADR records them. The amendments to earlier decisions are marked in those ADRs.

Amended by [ADR 0024](./0024-organizer-side-requires-an-account.md) (2026-10-14): the whole Organizer side requires an email account, so the publish gate and the reachable-Organizer invariants below are moot, and the merge's Organizer half no longer arises — an anonymous identity holds only Entries, their `players` link, and the participant-number rows Communities assigned it, which is what the preview lists and the holdings cap counts. Amended by [ADR 0023](./0023-communities-own-events-and-roles.md): "Organizer" below means a member of the Community that owns the Event, and the ownership transfer the last consequence wished for exists as the hand-over Grant.

Amended by [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md) (2026-10-15): an anonymous identity is minted only when a browser registers without an email or asks for a code, both behind a Turnstile check, so "a browser's identity is minted before cobracket knows who the person is" holds for those browsers only; the code email carries no user-typed text; the holdings cap is 100.

**One sign-in method: a six-digit one-time code sent to an email address.** No password. The person types their email, receives a code, types it back, and from then on is reachable (ADR 0015) under that address. A code is bound to the session that asked for it and to its purpose (sign-in, email change, deletion, a history's hand-over), expires after ten minutes, is single-use, allows five wrong attempts, and is superseded by the next code for the address; sending is limited per address and per requesting identity, once a minute and five times an hour, and the request answers alike for every address (the first anti-spam limits; the per-identity side, which free anonymous identities weaken, is the anti-spam grilling's). Convex Auth v2 has no such provider (2.0.0-alpha.2 ships anonymous, password, passkey, Google, GitHub, and Apple; the unreleased email-password method verifies by link, not by code), so it is built in `convex/auth.ts` on the core's `bindProvider` (documented as not for application code — an accepted deviation, since the seam is one file), `completeSignUp`, and `completeSignIn`, with cobracket's own table of hashed codes and Resend for the message, sent from a dedicated sending domain verified before slice 1. Three things about the core are deliberate choices, not givens: the provider keys the core's account by the user id (`USE_USER_ID_AS_ACCOUNT_ID`, which the core marks as a mechanism it may replace — preview risk, ADR 0003) while the verified email lives in cobracket's own table, so an account is deleted by deleting those rows and the same address can sign up again afterwards (ADR 0022); upgrading an anonymous identity in place means the `createUser` callback returns the current anonymous user's id, passed through the provider's profile, instead of inserting a row; and the core exposes no way to end a user's sessions, so an alias or a deleted user is signed out at the app layer, in `resolveSession`. The package's own email component (`verifiedEmails` keyed by user id, with no write path in alpha.2) was considered and not adopted, so the table stays cobracket's. Providers that yield a verified email (Google, Discord) can be added later behind the same entry point; a sign-in that yields none (a passkey) would not make a person reachable and is not planned. An account holds one email address; changing it verifies the new one and then removes the old, and a change to an address another account holds is refused — two email accounts are never merged in place; the one path that joins them is the deletion of one, whose preview offers to hand the deleted account's Player history to another account of the person's, verified by a code to that address (ADR 0022; built last, after the Player page and Rankings feature lines — the owner's call, 2026-10-22). The old address is told and holds a seven-day revert link (the address stays reserved meanwhile — no sign-up, upgrade, or change can verify it; reverting restores it and ends every session; a further change inside the window opens no new one, the first address stays reserved and its link reverts the chain), and deletion is refused while the window is open — otherwise a session left signed in on a shared computer, or stolen, would take the account for good with one change; a code to the old address instead would lock out everyone whose old mailbox is dead, the usual reason to change. A sign-in from a new device already sends a code to the inbox, so it needs no notice of its own. A session already signed in under an email account is not offered the sign-in form (it signs out first, or changes its email). Sessions persist on the device until thirty days of disuse, and a signed-in email account can sign out; an anonymous identity cannot, since signing out would be losing it.

**Publishing an Event requires a reachable Organizer.** Drafts and standalone tournaments stay anonymous, so ADR 0003's zero-friction promise — create a tournament in seconds — is kept where it was made. The reason is ADR 0015's own rule applied to the other side of the table: a published Event is a state many people wait on, and an Organizer with no channel cannot be told of a withdrawal, a claim, or a failed automatic publication, and cannot cancel from another device when their browser is lost — leaving Registrants with a page nobody can take down, while the Waitlist and automatic publication run on. It also narrows the fake-page and phishing paths that an anonymous Organizer sending email from cobracket's domain (slice 9) would open (a disposable address still passes; the rest is the anti-spam grilling's). Sign-in happens inside the publish flow, before `publishEvent`, which refuses an unreachable Organizer — publishing an Event is not two-phase — and the draft is carried along; while a published Event someone relies on exists, its Organizer's email can be changed but not removed; a hard bounce prompts re-verification and never unpublishes anything. The MCP server likewise issues tokens only to accounts with a verified email, which replaces the device-link flow once pondered for anonymous Organizers (ADR 0003, 0009).

**Signing in with an email that another account already holds merges the anonymous identity into that account.** A browser's identity is minted before cobracket knows who the person is (`src/lib/auth`), so the same person can hold an anonymous identity Y on one device and an email account X from another. There is one entry point — email, then code — and the server decides: an unknown email attaches to the current identity in place (the row is upgraded, every Entry and tournament follows); a known one merges Y into X. The verification spends the code and yields a short-lived proof the merge commit requires; two browsers verifying one new address serialize on the verified-email table, and the loser lands in the merge path; with nothing to merge the empty Y is deleted, not aliased. The merge previews Y's holdings — tournaments, Events, Entries, ended ones included — and the two things it cannot undo later: the bans on Y that X takes on, and how many Organizers will learn through the participant numbers that Y and X were one person; two choices, bring them or cancel: "sign in without them" was dropped because a browser holds one identity at a time, so leaving Y behind strands it forever (a colleague's live bracket on a shared venue PC), and only cancelling changes nothing; the cancel screen points to a private window or another browser profile. The merge is one transaction, which an anonymous identity's holdings cap (about 1,000 tournaments, Events, Entries, participant-number rows, and bans it issued — the rows the merge re-points; the value is fixed in the anti-spam grilling) keeps inside Convex's limits: those rows re-point to X and Y's Entries get X's `players` row (Y's is deleted; when X has none yet — a browser that only published — Y's row becomes X's), while rows an Event owns stay with the Event and deliveries resolve through the alias; X's participant numbers win; bans are unioned in both directions without removing an existing Entry; Y's Entries that followed its account name get that name as their Track-specific name, so nothing changes on screen; and Y's row stays as an alias — stripped to the mark — merged into X so recorded actor ids need no rewrite; it never authenticates again and grants no access. Where X and Y both hold an active Entry on one Track, the preview asks per conflict with no default, showing the further-along Entry (results, then published, then pinned, then confirmed); the one not kept ends as an ordinary cancellation. Once the operator can suspend a person (the anti-spam grilling's operator workflow; today only an Event can be suspended), a suspended identity is refused the merge.

## Considered Options

- **Magic links instead of codes.** A link opens in whichever browser the mail client picks, which on a phone is often not the one holding the anonymous identity to upgrade; a code is typed where the person already is.
- **Passkeys or OAuth first.** Neither makes a person reachable by itself (ADR 0015); OAuth providers that return a verified email are an additive second provider later.
- **Convex Auth v1's email OTP.** v1 is incompatible with the v2 core ADR 0003 adopted, and the provider is small to build.
- **Anonymous Organizers may publish, with email recommended** (the first proposal). Checked and withdrawn: every finding above holds, and reachable-only Registrants handing their email to an unknown Organizer inverts the trust the default asks for.
- **Requiring sign-in to create an Event, or to open registration.** A draft affects nobody, so friction there protects nothing; a registration-time rule leaves an information-only page anonymous and the fake-page path open.
- **Recovery keys for anonymous Organizers.** Rejected in ADR 0003; it solves only a lost browser.
- **A merge with a third choice, "sign in without bringing them", or a separate "link email" button, a reverse merge (the account into the browser's identity), a partial or deferred merge, or keeping the anonymous identity as a second id forever.** Each either strands or loses data, leaves a half-merged state, or makes every read multi-identity.
- **Merging two email accounts in place, both addresses verified** (the audit's alternative, 2026-10-18). It folds two Organizer sides — memberships, ownerships, Entries on both sides of one tournament — for a case the owner judges rare; the hand-over at deletion (ADR 0022) is one mark on one row, chosen on 2026-10-22 at the lowest implementation priority.

## Consequences

- Slice 1 (`docs/specs/events.md`) builds the provider, the merge, sessions, the holdings cap, and — since it is the first slice to store a person's own contact data — account deletion and the privacy notice (ADR 0022); MCP tokens, when built, go to email accounts only. Resend moves from slice 9 into slice 1 for the codes; the Organizer's own notices (withdrawals, claims, failed automatic publication) reach their email from slice 9.
- The registration form embeds the sign-in: email, code, name, register, on the Event page with no navigation, with no pending Entry state and no seat held while a code is in flight (a seat belongs to a reachable person, never to a form in progress). On an anonymous-allowed Track the email field is optional, with one line saying what registering without it gives up; a full anonymous-allowed Track makes it required, since the only outcome left is the Waitlist (ADR 0015). A code arriving after the close completes the verification and the registration is refused with the reason. Claim links stay exempt from the requirement on a one-player Track (on a team Track a claim is an account's — ADR 0029, 2026-10-22); a session signed in under another email is shown "not you?".
- `convex/auth.ts` stays the single auth seam (ADR 0003): the provider, the merge, and deletion are its functions, and the rest of the app keeps asking only who the current user is.
- The merge's one residual cost — a shared-device mistake making a colleague's tournament the signer's — is recoverable once ownership can be transferred (co-organizers, out of scope here).
