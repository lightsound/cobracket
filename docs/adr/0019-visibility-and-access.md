# Visibility only narrows; links grant viewing, Access Grants grant actions, and one predicate decides who sees what

Amended by [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) (2026-10-15): the registrant-names audience governs the view before and during the Event; the playing Lineup of a completed team tournament is part of its published result. The registration requirement is a three-step ladder — anonymous allowed, account required (the default), Squad-linked teams only — and applies to every Entrant of an Entry. [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) adds a Visibility to Community pages (unlisted by default, private = members only, public = listed in the directory). The Organizer relation behind id-addressed routes also includes an owner or admin membership of an ancestor of the owning Community ([ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md)); a block below is a Track-scope sanction row (ADR 0029), which the Organizer may lift; the ladder's middle rung is named "account required" everywhere; an Entrant row, an `invited` one included, is a relation that gives view of the Entry and its Track as a Grant does; an Entrant of a forming Entry belongs to the Registrants audience, not the confirmed one; on a team Track the bracket carries the team name, and the person's name appears in the completed result's lineup, on the registrant list as configured, and — before the Event — to teammates, invitees, and join-link holders, which registration says, and, while the Entry is listed as recruiting, to the Organizer and to holders of an active Entry on that Track, which the listing screen says (ADR 0029).

Events bring the first content that is not simply "anyone with the link": invitationals, early-access windows, secret betas. A first design added per-level exceptions and stored grant redemptions; review rounds showed each addition opened a leak (a private Event's context exposed through an unlisted tournament, forwarded single-use invites becoming permanent view rights, notices unreadable by the people they were for). The settled model removes those mechanisms instead of patching them. Amended by [ADR 0023](./0023-communities-own-events-and-roles.md) (2026-10-14): the Organizer relation behind id-addressed routes is membership of the Community that owns the subject — the co-organizer seam the options below reserved.

- **Visibility only narrows, Event → Track → tournament.** An Event is unlisted (default) or private; public — findable without a link — is stored for later and hidden until cobracket has a directory. A Track is open, invite-only (visible; registering needs an Access Grant), or hidden (private within its Event; hidden implies invite-only). A tournament attached to a Track has no Visibility or Discipline of its own while attached; both follow the Track. A standalone tournament keeps its own field (unlisted; private means Organizer only). A streamed invitational is an unlisted Event with an invite-only Track, so its Share Link stays open to spectators.
- **Two kinds of access.** A bearer link — an Event slug or a tournament Share Link, opened _by slug_ — lets anyone holding it view while the effective Visibility is unlisted or public, and needs no identity. Routes addressed by document id (notification links, inboxes, overlays, MCP, APIs) require a relation instead — the Organizer, or a person on an Entry in scope — because document ids are not capabilities. An Access Grant (ADR 0018) lets its presenter view its scope only while it can still be acted on — not revoked, inside its window, uses left (a claim Grant: while a claim would pass, and after the Event's end only its bracket) — and nothing is stored when it is opened; once its holder acts, the resulting Entry grants access. An Entry or Track Grant in a private Event shows the Event, its visible Tracks, and that Track, never other hidden Tracks. A withdrawn person keeps view of the bracket they are in and nothing more: ended Entries grant no view of a private Event page and belong to no Registrant audience, and a block ends even that relation-based bracket view.
- **Two predicates.** `canView(subject, viewer, how it was resolved)` answers whether someone may open it; `canDiscover(subject)` answers whether it may be surfaced to people without the link (public only — nothing today). Anything reaching people indirectly — a Player's feed, profile pages, listings, APIs, bots, search — uses `canDiscover`, so unlisted participation never leaks through a Player. A Ranking is the one exception, under the same intent (amended by [ADR 0025](./0025-rankings-are-a-publishers-reading-of-public-results.md), 2026-10-14): its publisher may count any non-private tournament they can view, and the Ranking's own Visibility is capped at the narrowest counted tournament, so a public Ranking counts public tournaments only and an unlisted one reveals no more than forwarding the counted Share Links would. Both take a role for the Organizer's "view as" preview, never a user id, and only on the caller's own Event.
- **Identity-free bearer reads.** Convex shares a cached query result across clients only when the execution never read the caller's identity. `canView` therefore settles not-found and bearer-unlisted before reading identity; identity-dependent parts (hidden Tracks a person may see, "your status") are separate overlay queries, and displayed counts come from a sharded counter per Track so no registration contends on a count, and "your next match" is derived on the client from the shared bracket and the person's own Participant. The Event page shell reads no Entries; counts and the registrant list are their own queries (an aggregate count, a paginated list), so a registration re-runs only those. Visibility and lifecycle fields live on documents that registrations never write. Time-dependent facts (an Offer lapsed, a Grant's seats released) are materialized when their instant is processed by settle, because a query does not re-run as time passes — and a query that grants view through a Grant compares its end (or its Event's end) with the server's time — a deliberate exception to reading time only in mutations, since a client-supplied time could be spoofed — so a stalled settle fails closed (an open subscription keeps its last answer until re-evaluated); clients display open or closed using a server-time offset rather than their own clock.
- **A suspension state reserved for the platform operator** is an absolute gate in `canView` from the start (a suspended Event is visible to its Organizer only): a private Event is invisible to everyone else, so it could otherwise host abusive content nobody can report.
- **Field audiences** reuse ADR 0014's audiences: registrant names show to everyone who can view the page (default), to Registrants, or to organizers only (the count always shows); an in-person Event shows a public area, and its exact address to confirmed people by default or to everyone who can view; an online link shows to confirmed people only; waitlist names are never shown (a count, and each person's own position).

## Considered Options

- **Every secret URL as an Access Grant, including slugs and Share Links.** Viewing needs none of what makes a Grant (limits, reserved seats, windows), spectators have no identity to redeem to, and public Events still need slugs. Keeping bearer links separate is simpler.
- **An "anyone with the link" override on tournaments inside private Events.** It exposed the Event's context through the Share Link and stayed open when an Event later went private; the invite-only Track covers the real case.
- **Stored redemptions for viewing through a Grant.** Every opened link minted a user and a row, forwarded single-use links became permanent view rights, and the rows outlived revocation and removal.
- **One relation table (user, subject, kind) behind every predicate.** Only different, not better, once redemptions are gone; the predicates are the seam where it can be introduced with co-organizers.

## Consequences

- Regenerating an Event's slug regenerates its attached Share Links too unless the Organizer keeps them, and Share Link pages never reveal the Event's slug otherwise; regeneration does not revoke Grants.
- On a private Event the UI calls invite links "people with an invite link", because an unlimited Grant differs from an unlisted link only in being revocable.
- Removing someone's Entry may also block them on that Track (the Organizer's choice, off by default, stored as a flag on the ended Entry); a block ends every view of that Track that rests on a relation, on a Grant, or on the Track being hidden or its Event private (the rest of the Event is unaffected) — an unlisted bracket stays open to anyone holding its bearer link, as for any spectator — and the preview says exactly that. Anonymous identities were free to mint, so every per-user limit depended on a cost to minting one, which [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md)'s robot check provides.
- Registration tells the person every place their display name will appear: the registrant list (as configured), the published bracket, and the Organizer's views.
- Discipline suggestions list a curated set and the caller's own Disciplines only, since anything wider either leaks unlisted Events or lets anyone plant names in everyone's suggestions.
- Every `/e`, `/s`, and `/g` response carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` and an explicit `Referrer-Policy`; the future OGP Worker unfurls unlisted subjects with their own fields only and returns a generic card for private ones, with short cache lifetimes. Organizer-authored text renders as untrusted plain text, links carry `rel="ugc nofollow"`, and Event, Share Link, and Grant pages say who hosts them.
