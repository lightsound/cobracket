# Communication is an activity log, subscriptions, and delivery — contact data lives on users, never on Entries

Amended by [ADR 0023](./0023-communities-own-events-and-roles.md) (2026-10-14): the co-organizers anticipated below are the members of the Community that owns the subject, and the audience "the subjects' organizers" resolves to those members when an Activity is read or sent, so a hand-over to another Community or a membership change rewrites nothing. Slice numbers follow [ADR 0024](./0024-organizer-side-requires-an-account.md)'s re-sequencing. Amended by [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) (2026-10-15): the first explicit Follow is of a Community — an Account's subscription row delivering four fixed kinds of public activity; Follows of Events, Tracks, and Players stay later. The Community Follow ships as slice 12 of the Events feature line. "Holder" and "the person" below read, for a notice about an Entry, as the Entry's Entrants — the captain and the account Entrants, each on their own channels — and, on a Squad-linked Entry, its staff (who get its Entrants' changes folded per Entry per day), and "a person blocked from a Track" as a Track-scope sanction; a notice to an Event's or a Track's Registrants — a cancellation or deletion among them — reaches every Registrant there, anonymous ones in-app, and the staff of each Squad-linked Entry among its active Entries (ADR 0029); "the subjects' organizers" is sent to the owning Community's members only, while reading includes the owners and admins of its ancestors, who are Organizers of its Events and learn by looking (ADR 0028); the team notices ("you can register", "no team formed", an invitation, a lineup change, "short-handed", "below the minimum") join the person-addressed list; an Entrant of a forming Entry belongs to the Registrants audience, not the confirmed one (ADR 0029). The Community Follow's four kinds — an Entry confirmed on a public Event, a public tournament completed with the team's placement, a Squad member joined or left, a move in a public Ranking's standings — are the one push of a confirmation and a completion to people who are neither the Organizer nor an Entry's Entrants or staff, so the fixed-audience and pull-only sentences below are qualified by it (ADR 0028).

Events bring the first communication needs — Organizer announcements, waitlist Offers, schedule changes — and the roadmap brings many more: following a Player's results and entries, telling co-organizers that someone changed a permission, match calls, Discord bot and MCP "what happened since", community webhooks and a public API, and the audit trail co-organizing requires. We build all of them on three layers instead of a notification feature:

1. **Activity log.** Every change a person must learn of — whether an Organizer's or Registrant's operation, or the system acting on a policy or a due instant — appends Activities in the same transaction: actor (a user, or the system on someone's behalf), kind, the most specific subjects involved (Event, Track, Entry, tournament, Participant), an audience, and time. It is written by explicit calls, not by table triggers, because a row change does not say who acted or why. Writes that merely follow from others — materializing a projected Roster, for instance — are not recorded.
2. **Subscriptions.** Who is interested in a subject is a function over relations — a Registrant in their Track and Event, an Organizer in what they run — not a stored recipient list, so a future relationship model for co-organizers, and explicit Follows of Players, Events, and Tracks, plug into the same function.
3. **Delivery.** Fan-out from Activities to recipients is asynchronous and recorded per recipient and channel, so it is retryable and never fans out twice; an external post (a Discord webhook) is at-least-once, since a retried send can repeat. Destinations are either places (the Event page, a Discord channel webhook) or people (in-app inbox, email, Discord DM, push — resolved Entry → holder → the user's verified channels). What arrives is the intersection of what the sender chose for that Activity and what the recipient accepts.

This mirrors ADR 0005: one recorded source, everything else derived. Recorded results remain the source of truth for brackets; the Activity log records that something happened and is never used to rebuild state.

Contact data belongs to the person, not to an Entry: an unverified email typed into a registration form would let anyone make cobracket mail a stranger, and a verified one is already an account. Email and other personal channels therefore arrive after account sign-in (ADR 0013), both within the Events feature line (amended 2026-10-11; `docs/specs/events.md`, slices 1 and 9). Sign-in codes are the first email cobracket sends, in slice 1; Organizers' own notices reach their email from slice 9 (ADR 0021).

**Audiences.** An audience is always relative to the Activity's subjects and never wider than their Visibility (ADR 0019): everyone who can view all of its subjects; the subjects' organizers; the people on their confirmed Entries; all their Registrants, waitlisted ones included; or named people. Dynamic audiences are re-checked when read and when sent. Person-addressed notices — joining the Waitlist, an Offer, its return or lapse, a confirmation, a withdrawal, a claim (to the Organizer), a removal, a substitution or restoration, "not in the bracket" or "did not get a place", a publication diff, a cancellation or deletion — are instead written straight into the named recipients' in-app deliveries at commit time, self-contained with the display data they need (names resolve by id while their subject exists), and are checked by membership only — so the people losing access still read them, even after the Event itself is deleted. Entry-lifecycle notices (confirmed, waitlisted, offered, withdrawn, removed) have fixed audiences: the Organizer and the person, never a place and never the Event's viewers. A person blocked from a Track receives only notices about their own status there, never bracket content. Places take only Activities whose subjects are all visible at Event level and whose audience is everyone who can view them. List Activities — someone joined, someone cancelled — are pull-only: feeds filtered when read, never fanned out per person and never pushed to a place, which would be quadratic in a Track's size and would spread names further than the registrant list shows them.

## Considered Options

- **A notification pipeline only** (announcements and system notices, no log): cannot serve following a Player's results, co-organizer audit, or "what happened since" consumers, and anything not sent is lost.
- **Full event sourcing** of all state: at odds with Convex's document model and already done where it matters (results, ADR 0005).
- **Deriving Activities from table changes** (convex-helpers Triggers, change capture): loses the actor and intent, and runs outside the operations API's explicit capabilities.
- **A hosted activity-feed or notification service**: lock-in against a project that exists to exercise Convex; it can still become one delivery destination later.
- **Convex components**: none covers the log, subscriptions, and fan-out end to end (checked 2026-09-27), so those are hand-rolled and small. Delivery is assembled from official components — Workpool for jobs and retries, Resend for email, Rate Limiter for throttling — with plain `fetch` for Discord.

## Consequences

- Activities store ids and minimal payloads and resolve names when read, so pseudonymizing a Player never leaves copies behind (deletion no longer pseudonymizes — ADR 0022 — but the operator's redaction on request rests on the same property). The exception carries content that must outlive its subject: the display data inside person-addressed notices — subject data such as an Event's name, never a person's name, which resolves through the Entry, since Entries outlive accounts (amended 2026-10-14, ADR 0022). An announcement is its own document, which its Activity references, so the Organizer's edits and deletions reach every in-app copy; what already went to an external place cannot be recalled. Field values never ride in payloads ("the location changed"); they are resolved when read under that field's audience.
- Subjects are stored one row per (Activity, subject key), since Convex cannot index array fields. A Player's history is resolved at read time through the Participants and Entries they hold or have claimed, so a later claim pulls past Activities in without rewriting them.
- Activities about a deleted subject survive only when attached to a subject that remains (a Track's deletion is recorded on its Event, a tournament's on its Track), and keep the Visibility the deleted subject had, so a hidden Track's deletion does not surface through its Event. A standalone tournament's deletion leaves no notice behind; deleting an Event deletes its Activities (ADR 0011, 0017).
- External consumers (webhooks, public API, MCP) get a versioned public event format mapped from Activities, never the internal shape, and are served from per-consumer delivery records rather than time cursors, which concurrent commits can reorder.
- Unread counts are capped, only the displayed page re-checks access, and an operation that narrows access schedules the removal of deliveries its losers can no longer read. Sent emails, webhook posts, and link unfurls cannot be recalled, and warnings say so.
- Explicit Follows are designed for but not part of the first Events feature line; implicit relations cover its stories.
