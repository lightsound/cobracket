# A team Ranking stands chains of Entries: a team is identified by its Squad or by shared players, and a team's placement is credited to the players who played

ADR 0025 left open whom a Ranking in a team Discipline stands, and ADR 0026 anticipated a fourth knob family for what a team's players inherit. The team-competitions grilling of 2026-10-15 (rounds 16–18 and 20 of its record) found that real rankings identify a team three ways and that cobracket needs all three: by lineup (Valve's CS2 regional standings and RLCS treat a team as "the majority of the players", the organization's name a label — so an organization cannot buy a standing by buying a roster), by organization (clubs, franchises, schools: the same team though every player changes; HLTV and ESL blend the two, carrying points while three of five stay), and by exact lineup (ITTF and BWF pairs, Smash doubles: one change is a new pair). Both ADRs' readings hold: a Ranking is still a publisher's reading of public results, and identifying a team is one more part of that reading, explained row by row.

**A Ranking's subject is chosen at creation: Players or teams.** A Season's scope gains a condition on lineup size (the Track's minimum), and the Discipline plus that size give the subject's default — Players for a size of one (no team subject there: no K satisfies the bound below), teams otherwise — and the default of K below; mixed sizes raise a warning. A team Ranking stands chains; a Player Ranking in a team Discipline stands Players credited through their teams (below). The three Method types and their knobs are unchanged; a team Ranking's manual order ranks chains. A team Ranking counts Track tournaments only: a standalone tournament has no Track and no Entries, so a chain cannot read it, and the Season's preview names the tournaments skipped for that reason; a Player Ranking in a team Discipline credits through Entries and so skips standalone tournaments likewise.

**A team is a chain of Entries, one per counted tournament, linked by a predicate with two modes.** "Chain" is an internal word, never shown in the UI. Entries of the Season's counted tournaments are taken tournament by tournament in held-on order (ties by completion instant, then id). Within a tournament, every (Entry, open chain) pair is scored by the largest number of players the Entry's lineup shares with any of the chain's last three lineups (one recent bench-heavy appearance must not orphan a history; a chain stays open for the rest of the Season however many counted tournaments it misses, so these are its last three appearances and no consecutive appearance is needed), and pairs are assigned greedily in descending score, each Entry and each chain at most once in that tournament, so two Entries of one tournament are never one team (two Entries of one Squad on two Tracks the same day are two tournaments, so one chain may take both and carry two placements that day — the explanation says "the organization is the team"); an unassigned Entry opens a chain. The predicate that makes a pair eligible:

- _Shared-players mode_: at least K shared players, K an absolute count the publisher sets between 2 and the smallest counted minimum (shown as "at least N shared players"), defaulting to a majority of the most common minimum (5 → 3, 3 → 2, 2 → 2, that is exact pairs); the setting is hidden when only one value is possible. An optional contribution filter (default off, Valve's rule) counts a tournament for a chain only when its lineup shares K with the chain's current lineup, so a lineup that turned over one player at a time does not carry a history none of its players earned.
- _Squad mode_ (the default): shared-players mode plus one rule — Entries of the same Squad are the same team whatever they share, and that rule ranks above any shared count in the greedy order (so a Squad that replaced everyone is not pulled into another chain by count). A chain carries at most one Squad, set by its first Squad-linked Entry and following the latest; an Entry of a different Squad, or an ad-hoc Entry, joins by K like any other (which is what stops a Community from shedding a record by fielding a second Squad; two legitimate units sharing K are split by intervention). The publisher may set K' > 0 to require that many players to remain for the Squad rule to apply.

Ties in the greedy order go to the Entry's declared "last time's team" pointer (ADR 0029's copy; only where it also satisfies the predicate), then to the better placement, then to the Entry id, and between two chains tied for one Entry to the chain that appeared most recently, then to the chain key, so the result does not depend on processing order. An Entry whose lineup has fewer than K identity-backed players (typed names unlinked or refused) opens its own chain, marked "lineup not linked", and joins others only through a Squad or an intervention; the publisher links typed names in bulk, on one screen per Entry. A chain's key is its first Entry's id — the key of diffs between Revisions ("joined X", "split from X" join the vocabulary), of manual orders, of interventions, and of the Community page's lookup. The lineup a chain reads for an Entry is the lineup credited at the tournament's first completion (ADR 0029's `creditedPlayerIds`, read once and stored, extended by a later claim of a credited Entrant and corrected by a wrong-claim reset, both among the recomputation triggers below). Greedy assignment is the definition, not an approximation of one: there is no objective it optimizes, and the row's explanation ("3 shared players with the previous appearance") is what a reader judges.

**Interventions are keyed by Entries, and adjustments may target a Player.** Chain keys vanish on recomputation, so the publisher's hands name Entries: "treat as a new team" (always allowed) and "the same team as Entry X" (X earlier, sharing at least one player — unrelated teams cannot be merged), one per Entry, a later one of either kind replacing it. Both are listed on the Ranking with a reason as every intervention is. A point adjustment or an exclusion may target a chain or a Player — "every team this person is on" — so a team that re-forms under a new name does not shed a sanction. Interventions are reportable like reason text.

**Displayed name, Community pages, Player pages.** A chain with a Squad is shown under the Squad's Community (and Squad) name, and an ad-hoc Entry it absorbed is noted "as <team name>" on that tournament; a chain without a Squad shows its latest Entry's name with "formerly <name>" where it changed, and two rows with one display name carry their active periods. A Community page lists every chain containing an Entry of one of its Squads, with its standing, saying "now continuing as <Team Y>" when the chain's current Squad is another; a Player page lists, from a public team Ranking, each chain one of whose Entries counted the person (the Player ids its row stores), with its standing, "as a member of <team>". Explanations name shared players under the Player page's rules (a hidden page is a name without a link, a redacted one its pseudonym), counts by default and names on expanding. Seasons report the share of Squad-linked Entries among what they count, and suggest a Player Ranking where it is low.

**A Player Ranking in a team Discipline credits every player who played.** A team's placement counts for every person on the lineup of at least one of its matches (ADR 0029), substitutes equal to starters, coaches and managers not at all; placement points are multiplied by a weight knob (default 1, "how much a team's results count for its players"); a rating updates per match for each player on the lineup against the opposing lineup's average. The entrants multiplier counts teams.

**Carry-over and seeding use the same predicate.** A rating Season that inherits from the previous one matches each new chain, when it first appears, against the previous Season's final chains with the same predicate, each previous chain seeding at most one. Seeding a bracket from a team Ranking (ADR 0025's copy) matches each registered Entry's current playing lineup against each row's final lineup the same way (Squad, then K, then the pointer), stores the basis on the Participant ("same Squad", "3 shared: A, B, C"), gives a row claimed by two Entries to the one sharing more and leaves the other unranked, and says so in the preview. Seeding pairs by the sum of two individual standings is a later, separate seeding source.

**Inputs are stored, chains are separate from scores, and recomputation is scheduled work.** Every Revision stores, per chain row, the Entries it links with the Player ids counted for each and the rule that joined it (Squad, K, pointer, intervention), the Entries a placement tie-break decided, its latest Entry and Squad, standing, score, and the diff from the previous Revision; its header stores the Season's settings as applied and the result versions of the counted tournaments. So a later change of definition, link, merge, or deletion leaves every published Revision explicable and replayable. Chain assignment is kept apart from scoring: a placement enters it only as a tie-breaker, and a chain row records the Entries a placement tie-break decided, so a result correction — the most common trigger — rescores on the existing chains, and rebuilds them only when it changed a placement one of those tie-breaks read; chains are recomputed when the counted set, a claim or its reset, a name link or refusal, a merge, an intervention, the mode, K, or K', a held-on date, or a tournament's existence changes. Recomputation is an internal action that pages the counted tournaments, builds chains and scores in memory, and writes rows in batches, one run per Season at a time with a dirty flag for triggers that arrive meanwhile. Rows live in their own table keyed by (Revision, chain key), with an index from Entry to Season rows (Community and Player pages, the merge trigger) and from (Revision, Player) to rows (seeding). A chain holds at most one Entry per counted tournament, so ADR 0027's 1,000 tournaments per Season bound it, under Convex's array limits.

**A `players` row is never deleted; a merge aliases it.** The sign-in decision had a merge delete the merged identity's `players` row after re-pointing its Entries. Rankings, Revisions, and chains now hold Player ids as stored inputs, so the row stays as an alias of the surviving one (reads resolve the alias, as `users` aliases resolve), reaping keeps it too, and a merge triggers recomputation of the Seasons that counted either. ADR 0025's "a deleted account is a new Player" stands and is fatal for pairs — a re-registered partner makes a new pair — so the "same team as X" intervention ships with the first team Ranking, as does the refusal of a name link (a refused name is not counted, so a lineup left with fewer than K identity-backed players — a pair after one refusal — stays unlinked; the explanation says so).

## Considered Options

- **Teams identified by name.** "Team A" would be a new team every tournament, and a rename a split.
- **Teams identified by Squad only.** Ad-hoc teams would never accumulate a Season, and pairs could not stack results; the squad-only reading survives as the Squad rule inside the predicate.
- **Teams identified by lineup only** (Valve's rule for everyone). Right for a publisher guarding against bought rosters, wrong for a club whose fans follow the club through every roster; it stays as a mode.
- **A platform-wide team entity that Rankings stand.** Against ADR 0025: a Ranking is one publisher's reading, and two publishers may legitimately disagree about whether Red Reborn is Team Red.
- **The registration pointer ("last time's team") as the identity signal.** A captain could claim any history; it is a tie-breaker only where the predicate already holds, and a join only through an intervention that requires a shared player.
- **Comparing with the chain's latest lineup only, or merging chains that later share K.** The first orphans a history on one bench-heavy appearance; the second dilutes K into transitivity.
- **Crediting players active at the tournament's completion.** Drops a semi-finalist who left before the final; "on the lineup of at least one match" is the definition per-match records would refine.
- **Hiding a chain from a Community page once its latest Entry is another Squad's.** A single ad-hoc appearance would make a team's standing vanish from its page; listing every chain that contains the Squad's Entries, with "now continuing as", keeps the page honest.
- **An objective-optimizing assignment** (maximum weight matching). Harder to explain than the greedy order it would rarely improve on.
- **Deleting the merged `players` row** (the sign-in decision's rule). Stored Revisions would point at a missing key.

## Consequences

- Schema: `rankings` gain the subject, fixed at creation; `rankingSeasons` gain the lineup-size condition, mode, K, K', and the contribution filter; `rankingRevisions` headers gain the Season's settings as applied and the counted tournaments' result versions; `revisionRows` (Revision, chain key, standing, score, chain Entries with Player ids and join rule, the Entries a placement tie-break decided, latest Entry and Squad, diff), with the Entry → Season rows and (Revision, Player) → row indexes; intervention rows gain the two team kinds and a Player target; `players` gains an alias mark; Participants carry `creditedPlayerIds` (ADR 0029) and the basis a team Ranking seeded them by.
- ADR 0025's `rankingRevisions` consequence and its "a deleted account is a new Player" paragraph are amended for the alias and the stored inputs, and its intervention list for the team kinds and the Player target, and its seeding paragraph for the team basis stored on the Participant; ADR 0026 gains the weight knob, the team manual order, the mode with K and K', and the contribution filter as its fourth family, its entrants multiplier counting teams and its rating updating each lineup player against the opposing lineup's average (the lineup-size condition is ADR 0025's scope rule), and the per-lineup name links, the unlinked chain below K, the refusal shipping with the first team Ranking, and a Season started from the previous Season's ratings matching each new chain against the previous Season's final chains by the same predicate, each previous chain seeding at most one; ADR 0025's note also carries the Season's lineup-size condition and the Track-only count with its preview, and the team recomputation triggers with the result-correction rule; ADR 0021's merge rule for `players` rows becomes the alias, and ADR 0021 and ADR 0022 carry notes saying so.
- `CONTEXT.md`: Ranking stands Players or teams; Intervention gains the team kinds; Player keeps a merged row as an alias and says Rankings never attach to Participants; Method ranks chains in a team Ranking; Lineup is defined (ADR 0029).
- `docs/specs/events.md`: the merge paragraph aliases the `players` row; the Rankings note records what team Rankings read and what this spec owes them; the slice belongs to the Rankings spec when it is written.
- `docs/vision.md` closes its "Team competitions" open question.
