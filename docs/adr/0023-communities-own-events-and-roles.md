# Every Event and tournament belongs to a Community; roles and sanctions are per Community

Amended by [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) (2026-10-15): a competing team _is_ a Community — one that carries a Squad; Communities nest under one parent, three deep, the owner and admin roles applying downward and bans inherited downward (an inherited ban binds a member's own registration on their Community's Tracks, ADR 0028); a hand-over's preview counts the active Entries with an Entrant — or a Squad's Community — that the receiving Community or its ancestors ban, and "its bans are not applied automatically" below reads "its bans and its ancestors' withdraw the pending requests they bar (ADR 0029) and are not otherwise applied automatically"; the capability table gains the rows ADR 0028 lists; participant numbers become derived codes; the operator can suspend a Community; and "the subjects' organizers" below is sent to the owning Community's members only, while reading includes the owners and admins of its ancestors, who are Organizers of its Events and learn by looking. The admin role is built in slice 2, the Communities slice, since the tree's governance and member management must not depend on one owner; staff stays defined, not built. A child Community's owner succession resolves upward — designated successor, then longest-standing member, then the parent's owner — and the absence rule of ADR 0027 is replaced by ADR 0028's; whenever ownership moves while the former owner is still a member — the transfer below, a parent's naming, or a successor's request — the former owner stays a member as an admin and is told; deleting a Community is also refused while it has children (a root's sole member's account deletion detaches them as roots); the derived code is shared by a whole Community tree, so "different for every other Community" below reads "different for every other tree"; and "block" below reads as a Track-scope sanction, and the table's lineup row names Entrants (ADR 0029). Deleting a Community also ends its Squads (memberships kept as left rows), affiliations, and Follows (ADR 0028). The invitation screen below also says that the Community page lists members with their roles unless the owner hides that section (ADR 0028). The hand-over's notice to Registrants below reaches every Registrant, anonymous ones in-app, and the staff of each Squad-linked Entry among its active Entries (ADR 0014's note, ADR 0029).

Today a tournament is owned by one user (`tournaments.organizerId`), every permission check asks "is the caller that user", and `docs/specs/events.md` scopes participant numbers and bans to that one person. Co-organizing was anticipated — ADR 0014's subscription function, ADR 0018's "join an Event's organizers" Grant, ADR 0019's relation seam — but never designed, and the owner judged on 2026-10-14 that it is the one decision whose rework cost grows with every slice built on single ownership: a running team organizes twenty Events, people organize across several teams, and a Ranking needs a publisher. The grilling of 2026-10-14 settled the shape below.

**A Community owns everything an Organizer operates.** Events, standalone tournaments, bans, participant numbers, and — when they exist — Rankings belong to a Community, never to a user. "The Organizer" of an Event means the members of the Community that owns it, and every authorization is one question: is the caller a member of the owning Community with a role that allows this capability. There is no second ownership path: a person's first Community is created with their account, named after them, and is not special — it can be renamed, take members, and be left; a person creates as many as they like, and one with none gets a new one when they next create something. So "I ran this alone and now want help" is one invitation, not a migration, and a person who organizes for several teams is simply a member of several Communities. Members see everything their Community runs — drafts, registrant lists, past Events included — and the invitation screen says so (Organizers never see a Registrant's email address, ADR 0013). A member may register as a Registrant on their own Community's Tracks and record their own results; ADR 0005's actor record is the conflict-of-interest control, as it already is for a solo Organizer.

**Roles are the unit of permission, and a Community has exactly one owner.** A membership row carries a role; a capability table maps roles to capabilities, so finer permissions are rows and columns added to the table, never per-person switches (which make "who can delete this Event" unanswerable without reading every member). Two roles ship first; two more are defined so the table does not move when they are built:

The owner is one person, so a co-owner can never remove the founder and delete the team's Community (Slack's primary owner and Discord's server owner guard the same thing; GitHub's peer owners do not). The owner transfers ownership to another member; on the owner's account deletion it passes to the member they designated, else to the longest-standing member, and a Community with no other member follows ADR 0022. An owner leaves only after transferring. A one-off helper — someone recording results at one tournament — is not a member: they hold an Access Grant with the action "record results on this tournament" (ADR 0018), which already has a scope, a window, and revocation, so a venue volunteer never sees the Community's other Events.

**Invitations, hand-overs, and one-off help are all Access Grants.** An invitation is a Grant with the action "join this Community": the role it confers is fixed at issuance, it has a use limit (one, several, unlimited), a window (seven days by default), and revocation; whoever opens it signed in joins. Organizers paste the link into Discord themselves; cobracket still never mails an address an Organizer typed (ADR 0018) — email-locked invitations are a later option, now that Organizers are verified (ADR 0024). An Event or standalone tournament moves to another Community through a Grant with the action "take over this Event", issued by the owner or an admin and accepted by the owner or an admin of the receiving Community (a person who holds one of those roles on both sides issues and accepts in one step); it works in every state but suspended or being deleted — draft, open, live, over, cancelled — since a Community that inherits another's series wants its history. What moves is one field plus what the Community scope requires: the receiving Community assigns its own participant numbers to the Event's people (paged after the ownership write, like a cancellation's notices, since Entries are uncapped), its bans are not applied automatically (the preview counts the active Entries of people it bans), Track blocks and Grants belong to the Event and stay (the preview counts the issuing Community's unended Grants, which the new Community may revoke), Activities and their audiences follow the Event, and the Discord webhook is deleted, because its URL is a credential of the old Community's server. Registrants of an Event not yet over are told in person that the Event is now run by the receiving Community; for an Event already over the hand-over is a list Activity on the page. The secrets of these three Grants are stored hashed, as ADR 0018 left open for strong Grants.

**Deleting a Community is the owner's, two-phase (ADR 0017), and refused while anyone relies on it.** It is refused while a published Event of the Community is not over, or any of its Events is suspended by the operator; the owner cancels or hands those over first. Otherwise drafts and standalone tournaments without a result are deleted, every Event and tournament with a record is kept whole under the Community's name — its row stays as a deleted marker holding the name, since Event pages and Player pages must still say who ran it — their Grants are revoked and webhooks deleted (nobody remains to manage them), members are told and their memberships, the Community's bans, dormant rows, and participant-number rows are deleted. There is no undo (ADR 0011). The last member leaving follows the same rules; only the account deletion of a root Community's sole member cannot be refused, and that path keeps ADR 0022's cascade.

**Scopes move from the Organizer to the Community.** Participant numbers are the same across a Community's Events and different for every other Community, so a team recognises and bans a person across what it runs and no Community correlates people across Communities (ADR 0013, amended). ADR 0014's audience "the subjects' organizers" is the owning Community's members at the time an Activity is read or sent, so a hand-over or a membership change needs no rewrite. ADR 0019's Organizer relation is membership of the owning Community. An MCP token is issued to a person and acts as them in every Community they belong to, like a browser session; narrowing a token to one Community is a later option.

## Considered Options

- **A user owns Events and co-organizers are a per-Event member list.** Every repeat Event by the same team is a fresh set of invitations, a Ranking has no publisher, and a block list per Event is what Communities exist to replace.
- **Either a user or a Community owns (GitHub).** Two authorization paths, and every solo-to-team move is a transfer; the auto-created first Community gives the same experience with one path.
- **The `convex-invite-links` component** (npm 1.0.0; evaluated 2026-10-14: a Convex Components Challenge entry, two commits, one maintainer, no tests visible). It is a second secret-link mechanism beside the Access Grant with its own membership tables and untyped roles in metadata; its feature list (email-locked invitations, link previews) is kept as a reference.
- **Several peer owners, or a primary owner above them.** Peers cannot protect the founder; a primary owner is a third role that a single owner plus organizers already covers, with "organizers may invite organizers" available as a table row if a team needs it.
- **Per-member permission flags, or fully custom roles.** Flags make the permission set a per-person computation; custom roles are Discord's answer to a problem a six-row table does not have.
- **Per-Event roles** (a helper on one Event). Reintroduces the per-Event member list; the results Grant covers the real case.
- **Moving Events only while they are drafts, or only between Communities the mover belongs to.** The inheriting-a-series case needs finished Events to move, and the hand-over Grant needs nobody to belong to both. Merging a whole Community into another is the bulk form of the same Grant, built if a case arrives.
- **A grace period or an archived state for a deleted Community.** What deletion loses beyond ADR 0011's terms is memberships and drafts; a Community with no members and only finished Events already reads as archived.

## Consequences

- Schema: `communities` (name, owner, successor, deleted marker) and `memberships` (community, user, role, joined instant); `tournaments.organizerId` becomes a Community id, Events and bans carry one, and participant-number rows are keyed by Community. The guard every public function calls (ADR 0020) resolves the caller's membership and role in the subject's Community through one function, so a per-Event assignment could be added behind it later without touching callers.
- `docs/specs/events.md` gains a slice for Communities, and the Organizer's home groups Events and tournaments by Community once a person belongs to more than one (the UI/UX pass decides the layout).
- ADR 0013, 0014, 0018, 0019, 0021, and 0022 are amended where they say "Organizer" and mean the owning Community; ADR 0022's "an Organizer's deletion revokes the Grants they issued" becomes "Grants belong to the Event and go when the Community does".
- A Ranking's publisher is a Community, and any Community may publish one over any non-private tournaments it can view, without consent ([ADR 0025](./0025-rankings-are-a-publishers-reading-of-public-results.md), 2026-10-14, replacing the consent question this decision left open): a Season's condition-based scope is evaluated against a tournament's current owner, so a hand-over moves it between such scopes, while hand-picked tournaments stay. Community handles are deferred (id URLs, the publisher's name on the page); the name is not unique and Events are addressed by id, which is what keeps a hand-over a one-field write.
- The privacy notice states that Organizers are the members of the owning Community and that an Event may be handed to another Community with notice; the legal review covers the change of controller. What a suspended Event blocks beyond deletion, and the ownership request for an absent owner, are [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md)'s (the absence rule as [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) replaced it). The team-competitions grilling reversed the note this decision carried: a competing team is a Community with a Squad ([ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md), 2026-10-15).
