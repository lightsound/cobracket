# Registrants share the Organizer's anonymous identity and users table; reachability requirements are Track policy

Amended by [ADR 0021](./0021-email-code-sign-in-and-reachable-organizers.md) (how sign-in works, and that publishing an Event requires a reachable Organizer) and [ADR 0022](./0022-account-deletion-keeps-the-records.md) (what deleting an account removes and keeps, and the non-personal `players` row Rankings aggregate on). Amended by [ADR 0023](./0023-communities-own-events-and-roles.md) (2026-10-14): the Organizer is a member of the Community that owns the Event, and participant numbers and bans are scoped to that Community rather than to a person; and by [ADR 0024](./0024-organizer-side-requires-an-account.md): Organizers are email accounts, so the anonymous identity this ADR shares with Registrants is now the Registrant side's alone. Amended by [ADR 0028](./0028-teams-are-communities-with-squads.md) and [ADR 0029](./0029-entries-and-entrants-how-teams-register.md) (2026-10-15): the participant number scoped to a Community below is a derived eight-character code — `HMAC(server secret, root Community id, identity id)`, shared across a Community tree — and the Entry's "holder" is now an Entrant row; a team Entry carries several. "One active Entry per user per Track" below reads as one active playing Entrant per person per Track, forming Entries included (ADR 0029's invariant 1), with "leave A and join B" offered as one step. The registration requirement below (reachable Registrants only by default, or anonymous allowed) is now a three-step ladder per Track: anonymous allowed ⊂ account required — the default, this ADR's "reachable only" — ⊂ Squad-linked teams only (ADR 0029). The wrong-claim reset below no longer removes the Entry and re-enters it: it happens at the Entrant (ADR 0029) — the wrong claimer's row ends and a typed-name row is re-entered in its place — while the Entry, its seat, and its Participant stay.

Event registration is cobracket's first write surface for people who are not the Organizer, and whatever identity a Registrant gets is what Player accounts and claiming will later build on. We give Registrants the same identity the Organizer already has: Convex Auth v2 Anonymous Sign-In (ADR 0003), one `users` table for everyone, and roles expressed as relations rather than as user attributes — a user is an Organizer of the Events and tournaments they own and a Registrant of the Tracks they hold an Entry in. With account sign-in, upgrading an anonymous user keeps the same row, so every Entry made anonymously follows the person into their account and, from there, into their Player record.

An Entry's holder is the user it belongs to. A self-made Entry has one; an Entry the Organizer enters by name has none until someone claims it through a claim Grant (ADR 0018), and claiming is subject to the registration gates that concern the person (a block, one active Entry, the Event's lifecycle — which an Entry whose tournament has been published passes, within the claim link's limits) — it takes no seat. A claimed Entry stays the Organizer's entry for the reachability requirement, and the Organizer is told of every claim. A user holds at most one active Entry per Track; claiming while already holding one offers to cancel the self-made one first. Every registration is a new Entry — registering again after cancelling does not revive the old one, whose history, Grant, and any bracket it reached stay as they were. Users cannot hand an Entry to someone else, which closes the resale and impersonation path; only the Organizer can undo a claim made by the wrong person — a previewed reset that removes that Entry (optionally blocking the person) and re-enters it by name in the same place, under the name the Organizer last gave it, with a fresh claim link. Entries are never deleted individually: they end as cancelled, removed, or lapsed, visible afterwards to the Organizer only.

Whether anonymous registration is good enough is decided per Track, defaulting to the Event's setting: reachable Registrants only by default, or anonymous allowed, which the Organizer chooses after being shown what it trades away. A reachable user has at least one verified, deliverable channel — typically an email address confirmed by a sign-in code, which to the Registrant feels like typing a name and an email into a form. Being signed in is not the test: a passkey-only account has no channel. Account sign-in with a verified channel therefore ships with Events (amended 2026-09-30: the owner made reachable-only the default, since anonymous-by-default left blocks and duplicate protection toothless). An amateur Track can still opt into anonymous registration while a pro Track at the same Event keeps the default — which stops one person entering several times from private windows, identifies the Registrant, and survives a lost browser — without anything in the Entry model changing. Entries the Organizer enters by name are the Organizer's own judgment and are exempt.

## Considered Options

- **No identity: an Entry is a record plus a secret management link handed to the Registrant.** Cheapest to build, but it is the hand-rolled secret-link scheme ADR 0003 already rejected for Organizers, and the first reachable-only Track would force the Entry model to be rebuilt around users anyway.
- **A verified account required for every registration** (Luma's model: a name and an email from every guest). Strong identity, but it abandons the zero-friction promise for every community event. Reachable-only is now the default, with anonymous registration one choice away for the Tracks that want it.
- **Separate tables or a role field for Organizers and Registrants.** Breaks the everyday cases — an Organizer entering their own Event, a Registrant of one Event organizing the next — and splits the identity seam `getOrganizer` exists to keep single (ADR 0009).

## Consequences

- Identity keeps resolving through `convex/auth.ts` only; the Registrant path reuses the session machinery rather than adding a second one. Chat surfaces authenticate as a linked user through the same seam, with token scopes (acting as Organizer or as Registrant) rather than a bot passing someone's id.
- An anonymous Registrant who changes browsers loses control of their Entry; registration warns that the Entry is tied to this browser, and the Organizer sees likely duplicates. The account-upgrade work that fixes this for Organizers fixes it here too (decided: ADR 0021).
- One person can hold several anonymous identities; every per-user limit therefore depends on minting an identity costing something, which [ADR 0027](./0027-abuse-limits-where-things-reach-others.md)'s robot check provides. A person under an Organizer ban who deletes their account and signs up again under the same email is recognised through a dormant row keyed by a tag of the email (ADR 0022).
- Organizer-facing data never carries user ids — Entries, their state, and whether a reachable person stands behind them. People appear under a participant number scoped to the Organizer (amended 2026-09-30): the same across that Organizer's Events, so an Organizer can recognise and ban a person across their own Events, and different for every other Organizer, so no Organizer correlates people across Organizers; from ADR 0023 the scope is the Community that owns the Event — the same across a Community's Events, different for every other Community. A person is visible across Organizers only through their public Player page, which lists public Events only.
