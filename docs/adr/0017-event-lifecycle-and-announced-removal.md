# Events are drafted, published, and cancelled; what others rely on is announced before it is removed

An Event is a draft (visible to its Organizer only, accepting no registration, holding no published tournament), then published, then possibly cancelled. Whether it is upcoming, ongoing, or over is derived from its start and end times, never stored as a lifecycle state. The rule that governs every removal is: **what nobody relies on may be removed freely; what someone relies on must be announced first.** A Registrant with an active Entry, or whose withdrawn Participant is still in a bracket, relies on the Event, so:

- An Event returns to draft only while nobody relies on it and it has no published tournament.
- A published Event that someone relies on cannot be deleted until it is cancelled, and then only once both the Event has ended and seven days have passed since cancelling. Cancelling closes registration, returns outstanding Offers to a frozen Waitlist, disarms automatic publication, notifies the Registrants, and keeps the page up, marked cancelled, together with its Entries, so a Registrant who opens the link still learns what happened. Its tournaments keep their Share Links with a cancelled banner and may still record results — and one already published may be unpublished, revised, and republished, take Organizer entries, hand confirmations, and substitutions, and have its Entries claimed — and the notice says so. Deleting a cancelled Event takes its tournaments with it, results included (ADR 0011). Cancellation cannot be undone; re-running the Event is a copy, not a revival. Deleting an Event is physical and whole, as for tournaments (ADR 0011).
- A Track someone relies on is deleted by a previewed operation that notifies its Registrants through the Event, which survives it; a Track whose tournament has a recorded result cannot be deleted.
- A tournament inside an Event can be published only while the Event is published, or republished after the Event is cancelled if it had been published before.
- After publication, changes to an Event's time or place notify its Registrants; renaming it or editing its description does not. A time change — which, once the Event has started, keeps its start and moves only its end and individual Track starts — moves the Tracks' start times, registration instants, publish times, and explicit Grant windows still in the future by the same wall-clock difference in the Event's timezone (so a daylight-saving boundary does not shift them by an hour), shown in its preview, which clamps or refuses any of them that would fall after the new end and sets any moved earlier than the commit to the commit; a timezone change asks whether to keep the instants or the local times, and only the latter is a time change.

This deliberately differs from Luma, where cancelling an event deletes its page and guests learn by email. Luma collects an email from every guest; cobracket accepts anonymous Registrants (ADR 0013), for whom the page is the only channel, so the page must outlive the cancellation.

**Operations that take away something a person holds — an Entry, a Participant's slot, what they can see through a relation — or expose data are two-phase in the operations API itself.** They first return, from a mutation, a preview of their impact and a token (a preview of an operation not yet allowed returns the earliest time it will be); the commit takes the token, recomputes the impact, and refuses if it no longer matches. The preview is stored — operation, target, arguments, calling Organizer, impact digest, a ten-minute expiry — and the token is a random secret stored with it, so a client cannot mint one without previewing, reuse one on another target, or commit twice: the commit checks the caller is the preview's Organizer, deletes the row, and expired rows are swept. The digest covers the kinds of impact and the per-person lists the Organizer decides on (whose first match changes, which fields become visible), not counts that only grow, so a busy Event does not refuse its own cancellation forever; recipient lists for notices are taken at commit. The operations: cancelling, deleting, rescheduling, or returning an Event to draft; deleting a Track or a tournament; publishing a tournament the first time (names become visible), republishing with changed pairings, and unpublishing; enabling automatic publication (attaching a tournament with a publish time, setting or re-arming one) — on Tracks that accept anonymous Registrants its preview warns that names chosen by strangers will be published unseen; changing any Visibility or Track setting; widening a field's audience; removing an Entry, resetting its holder, blocking a person, substituting, moving an Entry on the Waitlist, or — after a publication — taking a Participant off the Roster or swapping; regenerating a slug. Registration, withdrawal, closing registration, and issuing or revoking a Grant are not in scope — revoking together with the Entries still waiting through it is a removal, and is. The token proves a preview was fetched, not that a human read it, so chat surfaces must confirm with their user (MCP elicitation) before committing, and for clients that cannot, the MCP server returns a link to the stored preview for the human to confirm on the web, in that Organizer's own session (the page never mints an identity), showing the operation, its stored arguments, and the impact recomputed. A deletion's preview names any live tournament it takes with it, and result recording refuses under a deletion tombstone. This replaces ADR 0011's reliance on the UI for confirmation: with MCP and a Discord bot on the roadmap, no client may perform these operations without having been shown what they do.

## Considered Options

- **No draft; hide unfinished Events with a private visibility** (Luma). Visibility answers who may open a published Event; it should not double as "not ready yet", and a draft keeps Event and tournament lifecycles parallel.
- **Cancel means delete** (Luma). Leaves anonymous Registrants facing a missing page with no explanation.
- **Delete with registrants allowed, notifying them first.** The notice itself would survive (ADR 0014), but anonymous Registrants have no inbox to read it in from another browser — the page is the one channel they reliably come back to, so it must stay up for a while, marked cancelled.
- **A list of confirmable operations per decision.** Each new decision added cases; one rule over "reduces access or exposes data" covers them.

## Consequences

- Large deletions and cancellations run in batches; their first transaction flips the subject into a state every read and gate treats as gone or closed, and only then are audiences enumerated, so nothing registers into or reads a half-deleted Event.
- Tokens and impact digests never appear in logs; impact is computed from bounded reads, and counts in a preview come from an index read capped at a bound and stay outside the digest. On shared chat surfaces a preview is shown to the acting Organizer only.
